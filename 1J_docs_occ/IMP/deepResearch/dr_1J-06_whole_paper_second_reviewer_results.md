# Deep-Research Report dr_1J-06: Second Reviewer Expert Evaluation of Submitted Manuscript

## 1. Reviewer Recommendation and Deciding Points

**Recommendation:** Major Revision (resubmission required; manuscript contains fundamental internal contradictions, methodological mismatches, and mathematical typesetting errors that must be resolved prior to publication).

### The Three Deciding Points
1. **Severe Internal Inconsistencies in Core Quantitative Evidence:** The manuscript presents mutually contradictory values for its baseline "Default" schedule across different sections (alternating between 2% vacancy, 25% daytime step presence, 27.6% midday fraction, and 68.4% 24-hour mean), asserts a thermodynamic heating mechanism that directly contradicts its own empirical daytime occupancy findings for 2005 and 2015, and provides an anachronistic, factually disproven explanation attributing 2010 survey weights to the later 2011 NHS.
2. **Methodological Disconnect in Occupancy Integration and Building Archetypes:** The simulation pipeline applies household occupancy schedules derived exclusively from suburban single-detached 2-to-4-person households to mid-rise (4-storey) and high-rise (10-storey) urban core apartment units without demographic justification or type matching; furthermore, the core mathematical equation defining the Presence Filter (Equation 1, page 11) is algebraically garbled such that the active modulation terms cancel out to the default schedule.
3. **Unsubstantiated Methodological Scope and Overreaching Policy Claims:** The paper claims to provide a "nationally representative and replicable" methodology, yet survey sample weights were never applied to the schedule generation pool, code and data are not publicly deposited, the projected 2025 cohort ($n=323$) is evaluated without stochastic seed uncertainty, and national building code adjustments (+10% heating, -20% cooling) are prescribed on the basis of a single climate zone (Montreal, 6A) using a single weather file.

## 4. Overlap and Pre-Publication Check

To evaluate the risk of self-plagiarism, double submission, or unacknowledged prior publication, a literature search was conducted on public databases, preprints, and conference proceedings.

### Sources Searched
- Crossref metadata API (`https://api.crossref.org/works`)
- arXiv preprint server
- Google Scholar and academic conference archives

### What Was Opened and Found
1. **Conference Paper Opened:**
   - **Title:** "Longitudinal Analysis of Occupancy-Driven Energy Demand in Canadian Residential Buildings (1992-2022)"
   - **Author:** Orcun Koral Iseri, Department of Building, Civil and Environmental Engineering, Concordia University.
   - **Venue:** Presented in Session 20 ("Urban Energy and Community Modeling") at the eSim 2024 Conference (IBPSA Canada, Edmonton, June 2024; hosted on Fourwaves).
   - **Relationship to Manuscript:** The conference paper established the preliminary analysis of historical GSS cycles up to 2022. The submitted JBPS manuscript significantly expands this work by introducing the C-VAE generative projection to 2025, six distinct neighbourhood development typologies, and detailed diurnal metabolic profiles.
   - **Reviewer Impact:** An expert reviewer who attended eSim 2024 or searches the title will identify this conference paper. The authors should explicitly cite the conference paper in the Introduction or Acknowledgements as an earlier preliminary version, detailing the substantial new contributions (the 2025 generative projection, CBVM latent drift, and neighbourhood simulation).
2. **Companion Journal Article Opened:**
   - **Title:** "Occupancy modeling using population statistics and machine learning for urban residential built environment"
   - **Authors:** Orcun Koral Iseri, Ipek Gursel Dino, Sinan Kalkan.
   - **Journal:** *Energy and Buildings*, Volume 310, May 2026, Article 117155.
   - **DOI:** 10.1016/j.enbuild.2026.117155 (Crossref verified).
   - **Relationship to Manuscript:** This companion paper focuses on machine learning population synthesis and occupancy modeling for urban residential environments. The JBPS manuscript differentiates itself through its multi-cycle longitudinal time-use survey harmonisation over two decades (2005-2022), C-VAE latent space drift, and detailed cold-climate EnergyPlus neighbourhood archetypes.
   - **Reviewer Impact:** To avoid potential concerns regarding piecemeal publication or methodological overlap, the revised manuscript should cite this companion paper in the literature review, clearly stating how the two investigations address distinct research objectives.
