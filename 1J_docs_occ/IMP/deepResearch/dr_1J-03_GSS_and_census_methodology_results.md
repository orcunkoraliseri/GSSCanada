## 3. Official Comparability Notes and Cautions

### 1. The 2015 GSS Methodological Redesign Caution
Quoted from Statistics Canada GSS 2015 User Guide (Section 4.2, p. 10):
> "It is important to point out that any significant change in survey methodology (as outlined above) can affect the comparability of the data over time. It is impossible to determine with certainty whether, and to what extent, differences in a variable are attributable to an actual change in the population or to changes in the survey methodology. Consequently, at every stage of processing, verification and dissemination, considerable effort was made to minimize the effect of methodology changes on the comparability of the data."

Key structural breaks identified:
- **Sampling Frame Shift**: In 2015, the Random Digit Dialing (RDD) landline frame was abandoned in favor of the new Address Register (AR) telephone frame, incorporating cellular-phone-only households for the first time.
- **Activity Code Contraction**: In 2015, the activity classification was radically collapsed from 264 codes (in 2010) to 64 codes ("light diary"), while location coding was maintained at 22 categories.
- **Collection Mode Shift**: Transition from telephone-only CATI to mixed-mode collection incorporating an electronic questionnaire (EQ).
- **Tax Data Linkage**: In 2015, personal and household income questions were dropped from the survey questionnaire and replaced by direct administrative linkage to Canada Revenue Agency T1 Family Files (T1FF).

### 2. The 2022 GSSP Mode Effect and Frame Discontinuity
Quoted from Statistics Canada IMDB Survey 4503 Documentation:
> "Because an electronic questionnaire was used as a primary collection mode, Statistics Canada analyzed the potential mode effect on the estimates... In 2022, the survey transitioned completely to a dwelling-based frame using the Dwelling Universe File (DUF). Users must exercise caution when comparing 2022 estimates with earlier cycles conducted via telephone."

Key structural breaks identified:
- **Frame Transition**: Complete move to the Dwelling Universe File (DUF) from telephone lists.
- **Dominant Online Mode**: Majority of respondents completed diaries online via EQ, altering response dynamics.
- **Response Rate Decline**: Overall response rate dropped to 30.7% (from 38.2% in 2015, 55.2% in 2010, and 58.6% in 2005).
- **Hierarchical Activity Tree**: Restructured into a two-level hierarchy.

### 3. The 2011 National Household Survey (NHS) Data Quality Caution
Quoted from Statistics Canada 2011 NHS Hierarchical PUMF User Guide (Chapter 1, p. 3):
> "Comparability between estimates from the 2006 Census long form and the 2011 National Household Survey estimates: When comparing estimates from the 2006 Census long form and estimates from the 2011 National Household Survey (NHS) users should take into account the fact that the two sources represent different populations... Moreover, the NHS estimates are derived from a voluntary survey and are therefore subject to potentially higher non-response error than those derived from the 2006 Census long form."

### 4. Resolution of the 2010 GSS Weighting Hypothesis (Problem P3 in Revision Plan)
The submitted manuscript previously hypothesized (Section 4.1.1, p. 13) that anomalous low occupancy in 2010 might be caused by "GSS Cycle 24 inheriting the weighting framework of the voluntary 2011 NHS".
Official methodology conclusively refutes this explanation:
- GSS Cycle 24 was collected from **January to December 2010** and weighted in early 2011, **prior to the collection and release of the May 2011 NHS**.
- GSS person weights (WGHT_PER) in Cycle 24 were calibrated via raking ratio adjustments strictly to **post-censal demographic population estimates by age, sex, and province derived from the mandatory 2006 Census of Population**, completely independent of the 2011 NHS.
- The true methodological anomaly in 2010 is that Cycle 24 used an unusually detailed 264-activity classification and introduced simultaneous activity logging for the first time, causing respondents to disaggregate secondary activities differently than in Cycle 19 or Cycle 29.
