## 🔴 Refused, or flagged and not acted on

### 1. The CRKN 100 percent APC waiver claim. Do not select Gold open access on this.

`RV10` item 30 states that Building and Environment is covered by the CRKN Elsevier agreement with a
**100 percent APC waiver** for Concordia corresponding authors, and Section D tells us to select Gold
open access at "$0 out-of-pocket". **This is the exact shape of the claim that was wrong in the 2J
round**, where Building Simulation was assumed covered by the Springer waiver and was not.

Two things make it worse than a coin flip. Read-and-publish agreements much more commonly give a
*discount* or a *capped allocation* than a blanket 100 percent waiver, and eligibility is usually per
journal and per year, with allocations that run out. And the report's own list APC of **$3,690 USD**
is what is at stake if the claim is wrong.

**Nothing about this blocks submission**, because the subscription route is free either way. The rule
is simply: **do not tick Gold open access until someone loads the Concordia Library agreement page
and confirms this journal, this year.** Ticking it is an irreversible commitment to a real invoice.

### 2. Item 29 is answered as though complete, and it is not.

The prompt asked for the Editor-in-Chief **and the subject editors**, and asked to flag any editor at
Concordia. `RV10` names two Editors-in-Chief, states neither is at Concordia, and stops. **The
subject editors were never listed, so the Concordia conflict question is unanswered, not cleared.**
This matters because the previous paper found exactly such a conflict at a different journal, at
this author line's own department. An unlisted board is not an empty board.

### 3. `RV09` reference 5 contradicts itself, and it is the most dangerous row in the matrix.

The entry reads *Yamaguchi, Y., Chen, Y. and Shimoda, Y. (2017), "Development of a district energy
system simulation tool for carbon-neutral district planning", Applied Energy 190, 685-703*, and then
reports its own *Crossref returned title* as **"Transition pathways towards a carbon-neutral district
energy system: A case study of Osaka, Japan"**. Those are two different titles. Either the citation
is wrong or the verification field is, and the report presents the row as verified with full text
read.

**Yamaguchi is the closest competitor in the whole matrix**: it is the only row besides ours marked
Yes on time-use-survey-driven, multi-channel **and** activity-resolved simultaneously. It fails the
cell on future-year forecast and on single mixed-use building, so the novelty claim does not turn on
it, but that is a conclusion resting on a row whose own citation did not survive its own check. If
one row in Part B is opened by hand, open this one.

### 4. `RV09` disagrees with our own Table 1 about our own paper, and does not say so.

Its matrix marks **This Study** as **No** on *calibrated behavioural model*. `Table_01_gap_matrix.md`
marks the same axis for the same study as a tick. A report that contradicts the manuscript on the
manuscript's own attribute should say so in a disagreement section; this one does not, and the row
would have been read straight past. **This is now the only open BUILD NOTE in the paper**, because
the unoccupied cell Table 1 claims is defined partly by that axis.

### 5. `RV09` gives Doma and Ouf's data source as Telus mobile data. Our Table 1 says SafeGraph.

Both cannot be right, and Table 1's note quotes its own source saying "Mobile positioning data
(SafeGraph snapshots)". Whichever is correct, the axis verdict does not move (it is mobile
positioning either way, not a time-use survey), so this is a wording fix rather than a claim change.
Recorded so it is not discovered by a reviewer instead.
