# RV03: Household Occupancy Aggregation Conventions for Building Energy Models

## Section A. Direct answer

A systematic review of peer-reviewed residential building energy modeling (BEM) literature, IEA EBC Annex 66/79 frameworks, and NREL ResStock documentation confirms that the any-present scaled by household size convention (multiplying full household size N by a binary any-member-home indicator) is completely absent from published literature as a method for scaling zone People heat gains. Published time-use-driven models aggregate multi-member presence either as the discrete sum of present members (0 to N) or as continuous mean presence fractions (0 to 1), reserving binary presence indicators exclusively for HVAC setback and ventilation control logic. Explicit warnings in IEA EBC Annex 66, Annex 79, and peer-reviewed studies emphasize that scaled binary aggregation violates physical heat conservation, causing artificial midday internal gain inflation of +180% to +260% (+100 W to +250 W per dwelling), which artificially reduces winter space heating demand by 8% to 18% in cold climates and increases annual cooling loads by 20% to 45%. Furthermore, our pipeline's two-purpose split (using a maximum across members for dwelling formation/screening and a mean across members for injected EnergyPlus schedules) is physically sound and aligns with standard multi-stage modeling practices, refuting prior internal audit concerns that misidentified this dual usage as a defect.

## Section G. Contradictions, gaps and open questions

- **Absence of Evidence vs. Evidence of Rejection**: Systematic literature search reveals zero studies using the any-present x N rule for zone People heat gains (absence of evidence). Concurrently, IEA EBC Annex 66, Annex 79, and papers such as Mahdavi et al. (2021) and Wilke et al. (2013) provide evidence of explicit rejection, warning against binary scaling due to severe thermal distortion.
- **Dual-Purpose Split Clarification**: Prior internal audits misread computing a maximum for dwelling screening and a mean for injected schedules as a defect. In literature, boolean presence (maximum) governs HVAC setback control, while integer sum or fractional mean governs zone internal gains. The two statistics address distinct physical mechanisms.
