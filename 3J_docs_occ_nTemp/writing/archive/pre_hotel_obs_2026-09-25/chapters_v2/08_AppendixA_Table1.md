# Appendix A. Comparison with prior studies

Table A.1 scores the closest prior studies and the present study on eight axes. A calibrated behavioural model is one whose parameters are estimated from observed microdata on the population being modelled. It is not a claim that the output matches a measured energy series. Stock-scale means that the result stands for a building population, not for a named set of buildings. A district of individually modelled buildings is not scored as stock-scale. A future-year scenario means that occupancy is projected to a year after the last observed data. Time-series occupancy, time-use-survey-driven, multi-channel, mixed-use single building and activity or end-use resolution are scored on what each study reports. The three competing studies each hold part of the combination. Doma and co-authors model several uses together, but from mobile-positioning data and at district scale. Buttitta and Finn, and Widén and Wäckelgård, drive occupancy from a time-use survey but for one residential use and without a future year. The authors' prior residential work clears most axes for one use, and its second manuscript adds the future year and end-use resolution. P marks a partial score, as the authors scored that work in the second manuscript. The present study trades stock-scale coverage, with two tower prototypes instead of a housing stock, for several channels inside one mixed-use building.
