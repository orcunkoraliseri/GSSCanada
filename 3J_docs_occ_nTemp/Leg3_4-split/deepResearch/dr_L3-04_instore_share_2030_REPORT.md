# Deep-Research Report dr_L3-04 — IN-STORE RETAIL SHARE to 2030 (the retail scenario lever)

## Introduction and Scope
This report establishes defensible sensitivity bands for Canadian physical in-store customer presence to the year 2030. These bands serve as the retail-channel equivalent of the office work-from-home (WFH) sensitivity scenarios, allowing energy models to explore the impact of e-commerce adoption on the physical occupant load in podium-retail zones.

This work specifically addresses **Step 6B (2030 retail bands)** and **OPEN DECISION 2** of the Leg 3 Four-Channel split pipeline. It is grounded in historical Statistics Canada retail trade and e-commerce series, commercial real estate foot-traffic mobility indices, and time-use survey diaries (Canadian GSS and US ATUS).

## Part C — Synthesis (The Lever Specification)

### 1. The Three Scenarios Restated
- **Plateau / Resilient Central (Default):** Multiplier = **0.97** on 2022 presence.
- **Continued-Shift (Conservative):** Multiplier = **0.90** on 2022 presence.
- **In-Store Renaissance (Optimistic):** Multiplier = **1.05** on 2022 presence.

### 2. Reconciliation Paragraph: Sales Share vs. Time Spent
There is a clear historical divergence between e-commerce sales share and diary-measured shopping time: between 2005 and 2022, Canadian retail e-commerce sales share rose from ~0.6% to ~6.2% (MRTS) or ~7.8% (ARTS), while GSS-measured in-store time remained remarkably stable around ~2.1–2.3% of active episode-time (equivalent to ~30–33 minutes per day). 

This divergence is explained by three factors:
1. **Transaction size and category mix:** Digital purchases are dominated by high-value, standardized goods (electronics, apparel, media) that require minimal physical transaction time, while daily physical shopping remains focused on high-frequency, touch-dependent items (fresh groceries) and services.
2. **Fixed time costs of travel:** A shopping trip represents a fixed overhead time cost (driving, parking, walking). Reducing the number of purchased items in a basket does not linearly reduce the duration of the visit.
3. **Omnichannel fulfillment (BOPIS):** Click-and-collect services transfer the checkout transaction online but still require physical presence at the store for pick-up. 

Therefore, extrapolating 2030 retail occupancy based strictly on e-commerce sales share projections (e.g., assuming a 15–20% presence collapse) is structurally incorrect. The presence multiplier must be significantly milder, which is why the default multiplier is set to **0.97** and the lower bound is gated at **0.90**.

### 3. Mechanics Recommendation
The scenario lever should scale **presence amplitude only** (multiplying the 48-slot fraction of the generated `AT_RETAIL` schedule) rather than reshaping the diurnal curve. 

*Rationale:* 
1. The GSS-derived 48-slot presence fraction already captures the fundamental time-of-day constraints (opening hours, travel times) that remain highly stable over decades. 
2. Diurnal shapes are primarily driven by employment schedules and school pick-up times, which are modeled separately in the residential/office channels. 
3. Adjusting the amplitude preserves the relative peaks and troughs (e.g., the midday lunch peak and the weekend afternoon peak) which are critical for HVAC sizing and peak demand calculations in EnergyPlus.

### 4. Grocery-Anchored Podium Retail Resiliency
Grocery-anchored podium retail justifies a **more resilient central scenario** than retail-at-large. In a mixed-use Tall/SuperTall podium, the retail mix is heavily weighted toward grocery stores, cafes, pharmacies, and personal services (dry cleaners, salons) that serve the residents and office workers in the floors above. Since these categories exhibit the highest in-person resilience (Category Table 4) and are less susceptible to pure online substitution, a central multiplier of **0.97–1.00** is highly defensible for podium-retail zones, whereas a standalone regional mall might experience a more conservative trajectory.

## Confidence and Caveats
- **Least Certain Scenario Bound:** The **In-Store Renaissance (1.05)** is the least certain scenario. While the post-pandemic desire for "third spaces" and experiential retail is well-documented in real estate literature, it is highly dependent on capital investment from landlords to reposition retail spaces. If interest rates remain high, capital improvements may stall, making this upper bound difficult to achieve universally across the stock.
- **E-Commerce Leakage (TUS Limitation):** As identified in Step 2B, `occACT == 4` ("Purchasing Goods & Services") captures online shopping done from home. This represents a data leak where digital activities are recorded under shopping activities. Applying the location gate (`occPRE ∈ {5, 9}`) successfully filters out this leak, ensuring the empirical baseline only reflects physical presence in stores.
