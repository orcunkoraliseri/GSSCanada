🔴 Round 2, overnight — what the first end-to-end read found

  1. Two DOIs in the reference list point at the wrong papers. The returned research says 10.1016/j.apenergy.2023.122247 (Doma & Ouf, our closest prior work) actually resolves to a paper on hydrogen production via anion exchange membrane electrolysis, and 10.1016/j.enbuild.2019.109562 (Buttitta & Finn) to one on sleep and thermoregulation. Checked offline before believing it: dr_L3-10 really does cite both, so the error was not invented by the report. What cannot be settled from inside the project is which DOI is right — both citation forms are internally consistent, so consistency cannot discriminate and only opening the DOI can. Not swapped on one unverified report; both are flagged in the manuscript as DOI DISPUTED, DO NOT SUBMIT. Ten seconds of your time each.
  2. The paper stated its own headline retail median twice, differently. Abstract, Results and Discussion said 75.63, 5.47 % below the floor. Limitations and Table 7 said 75.4, 5.7 % below. Both were internally consistent arithmetic, which is exactly why neither looked wrong on its own. Re-derived from the frozen CSV: 56 retail rows, median 75.6260, 5.4675 % below. Corrected. No band moved and no verdict changed — retail was under its floor before and after. The finding is not that the number mattered; it is that no check in this project compares a number in one chapter against the same number in another.
  3. My own strip deleted a table caption, and every check passed. Making the clean submission copy removed the Table 4. caption: a ## Sources section was dropped by running from its heading to the next heading, and the caption sat in that span. The post-condition asked did any apparatus survive?, which is structurally blind to did any content disappear? A residue check and a loss check are different checks. Both now exist and both were seen failing. A near-miss the same night: a strip rule deleted a paragraph headed "Source of truth, and what is explicitly not the source of truth" — a caveat — caught only because every removal was diffed by hand.
  4. L8's residential central 130.6 is the midpoint of its own range. (113.9 + 147.2) / 2 = 130.55. The value appears nowhere in the deliverable CSV, which has no central column at all — and Table 5 explicitly declines to state that same quantity for exactly that reason, while Table 7 prints it. Left in place, because Table 7 is a declared transcription and the source document does say it; documented with a reopen trigger. Nothing operational depends on it. A number that is exactly the midpoint of its own range, absent from the data, and declined by a sibling table, is the shape of an invented figure.
  5. A blocked item turned out to be answered. V4-C3 is no longer blocked: the returned research gives a documented NOT FOUND — no open, machine-readable Quebec hotel-occupancy series covering any part of 2011–2018 exists, with six portals searched and listed. The manuscript's existing statement now has a citable negative behind it. The only route to the data is a paid custom extraction from ISQ, which is your call, not a block.
  6. The two returned reports disagree with each other. One says the Alberta series starts 2011 (matching our own record), the other 2010, and the second proposes splitting the citation at that boundary — which would leave 2010 unsourced. One also contradicts dr_L3-10 on whether Buttitta & Finn used four dwelling archetypes or multi-unit residential ones. Four annual Quebec occupancy figures were volunteered without being asked for, carry no resolvable locator, and are not being cited. One report proposes "zero uncharacterised cells" as a target; that framing is rejected — NOT FOUND is a publishable result here.
