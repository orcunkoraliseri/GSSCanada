# Step 9 — Figure Guide

This document describes each figure in `outputs_step9/`. These figures support the activity-driven internal-loads analysis (equipment and lighting) for the journal publication. The text explains *what each figure shows, what it is for, and how it should be read* — it deliberately avoids quoting specific numbers, since those depend on the simulation run and can change between versions.

> **Note — figures regenerated 2026-06-10.** All four figures were rebuilt from a corrected simulation campaign in which a 4-hour schedule-injection offset was fixed. The previously documented finding of an earlier activity-driven peak was an artifact of that bug. The corrected figures show baseline and activity peaks at essentially the same evening hour; the analysis below reflects this corrected result.

---

## figS6 — Equipment diurnal load shape

This figure shows the daily shape of equipment (plug-load) demand, one panel per building archetype, normalized so each curve is expressed relative to its own daily mean. In every panel a baseline shape is drawn against the activity-driven shape, with a vertical marker for each curve's peak hour and an annotation of the baseline-versus-activity peak-hour pair. Its purpose is to demonstrate that deriving equipment use from modelled occupant activity produces a fundamentally different intraday profile than a fixed, standard schedule. The reader should compare the two curves within each panel — where the activity curve rises and falls relative to the baseline, including the more pronounced morning rise and sharper evening concentration the activity curve carries — and note the labelled peak-hour pair, which shows that despite the difference in shape the demand peak falls at essentially the same evening hour under both schedules. Because the curves are normalized to their daily mean, this figure is about *shape and timing*, not magnitude; the magnitudes belong to the calibration figures.

## figS7 — Equipment baseline → activity peak-hour shift (all cells)

This figure summarises, for all archetype-by-city cells at once, the hour at which equipment demand peaks under the baseline schedule versus under the activity-driven schedule. Each cell is drawn as a stem: an open circle marks the baseline peak hour and a filled diamond marks the activity peak hour, so the length and direction of the stem encode the shift. Colour groups the cells by archetype and the cells are arranged in archetype blocks along the axis. Its purpose is to show that the peak-hour relationship seen in the per-archetype shape figure is systematic across the entire stock rather than an artifact of one or two cells. Read it by scanning the stems: across nearly every cell the circle and diamond coincide, meaning the activity-driven schedules reshape the intraday load profile without displacing the building-level equipment peak hour. The near-zero stem lengths, uniform across all archetype blocks and cities, are themselves the finding — activity-driven scheduling redistributes *when within the day* energy is consumed without shifting the hour at which demand crests.

## figS8 — Lighting diurnal load shape

This figure is the lighting counterpart to figS6: one panel per archetype showing the normalized daily shape of lighting demand, with the baseline shape drawn against the activity-driven shape, peak-hour markers, and a labelled baseline-versus-activity peak-hour pair. It exists to show that lighting, like equipment, takes on a different and more behaviourally plausible intraday profile when it is driven by modelled occupant activity instead of a fixed schedule. The reader should compare the two curves within each panel and read the peak-hour annotation to confirm that, despite the change in shape, the lighting peak falls at essentially the same hour under both schedules. As with figS6, the curves are normalized to their own daily mean, so this figure communicates timing and shape; absolute levels are handled by the calibration view.

## figV1 — Default vs Step-9 equipment demand (validation)

This figure validates the activity-driven equipment schedules against the default schedules, one panel per archetype. The single-detached panel is shown in absolute terms — actual equipment demand over the day, with the annual total held to the survey-based energy anchor so the two schedules are compared on equal annual energy — while the remaining archetype panels are shown normalized to the daily mean. Each panel overlays the default curve against the Step-9 curve with peak-hour markers. Its purpose is to confirm that the activity-driven reshaping conserves total energy (it redistributes *when* energy is used without inflating *how much*) and that the reshaping behaves sensibly across archetypes. Read the single-detached panel to confirm the annual-energy anchor and absolute magnitudes are reasonable, then read the other panels to confirm the shape change is consistent with the per-archetype shape figures. The peak-hour markers in each panel land at essentially the same hour for both curves, confirming that the activity-driven schedules are a faithful, energy-conserving replacement for the defaults — one that reshapes the intraday profile while preserving both annual energy and peak timing.
