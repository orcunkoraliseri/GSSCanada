# Architectures for Conditional Generation of Demographically-Stratified 24-Hour Activity Diaries: A Targeted Literature Review

## TL;DR

- **The single highest-expected-value architectural change is to swap the slot-level autoregressive decoder for a duration-aware, segment-level generator** — either a neural hidden semi-Markov decoder (Dai, Dai, Zhang, Li & Song, ICLR 2017) or, more pragmatically, a hierarchical "schedule-template → 30-min refine" decoder modeled on MaskGIT-style iterative parallel decoding (Chang et al., CVPR 2022) — because the dominant failure mode (100–150× over-fragmentation) is structurally induced by a per-slot CE objective on noisy K-NN targets and cannot be fixed by loss-side penalties alone.
- **For the noisy-target / contradictory-supervision pathology (JS floor ≈ 0.19), the right family is batch-level distribution-matching objectives** (Sinkhorn divergence, Genevay et al., AISTATS 2018; MMD), masked discrete diffusion with x₀-parameterisation (MDLM, Sahoo et al., NeurIPS 2024; MD4, Shi et al., NeurIPS 2024) which naturally tolerates target multimodality, and noise-robust losses (Ghosh et al., AAAI 2017; "data ambiguation" via credal sets, Lienen & Hüllermeier 2023). These reframe the problem from "match this particular neighbour's slot" to "match the conditional distribution of diaries."
- **For hard inter-output consistency**, factorise the joint as activity → (home, co-presence) with a Semantic Probabilistic Layer (Ahmed et al., NeurIPS 2022) or a regular-constrained CRF (Papay et al., EMNLP 2022) so that "at-home=0 ⇒ ¬alone" and "activity=work ⇒ at-home=0 ∧ colleagues=1" hold by construction, not by hope. This is the cheapest architectural win and is compatible with both autoregressive and discrete-diffusion backbones.

## Key Findings

1. **Three direct ML prior works exist** (Dube et al., arXiv:2509.18414, 2025; Liao et al., arXiv:2405.17468, 2024; Liao et al., arXiv:2507.08871, 2025). The first uses an explicit hierarchical-Bayesian semi-Markov with hazard-based duration component on ATUS at 10-min resolution and reports a likelihood-ratio improvement of e^1.75 ≈ 5.74× from the duration term alone. The third (DeepCAM, 15-min, 96 slots) is the closest neural precedent and reports JSDs of 0.005 (aggregated activity types), 0.002 (start times) and ≤ 0.006 (24-h activity pattern) using a transformer encoder–decoder with a role-feature-matching attention and an **Activity Overconfidence Regularization** loss. These results strongly suggest the dominant lever is *explicit duration/segment modeling*, not bigger models.

2. **Discrete diffusion has matured to a usable family for short categorical sequences** (D3PM, Austin et al., NeurIPS 2021; SEDD, Lou et al., ICML 2024 Best Paper, arXiv:2310.16834; MDLM, Sahoo et al., NeurIPS 2024, arXiv:2406.07524; MD4, Shi et al., NeurIPS 2024). For a 48-slot diary with ~10 activity classes, masked diffusion is competitive with AR transformers and is structurally better at handling multi-modal targets because the x₀-parameterised loss is a denoising cross-entropy on randomly masked positions — exactly a mode-covering objective. Inference cost is 8–32 steps vs 48 AR steps.

3. **The K-NN soft-target issue is a known pathology** that the literature treats either with (a) noise-robust losses, (b) latent-true-label EM / instance-dependent noise models, or (c) batch-level distribution matching. The third is most apt here because the user already cares about *population-level* statistics, so the natural objective is a Sinkhorn or MMD loss between batches of generated and observed diaries rather than per-sample CE.

4. **Time-of-day structure is an extremely strong prior** that the baseline is under-using. Fourier features (Tancik et al., NeurIPS 2020) and learned diurnal embeddings cheaply inject this. The weekday/weekend ordering violation is most likely fixed by *conditioning architecture* (FiLM or cross-attention from a stratum encoder, Perez et al., AAAI 2018) rather than by changing the backbone — the model is currently averaging over strata because the demographic broadcast is too weak.

### Q1. Architectures for piecewise-constant / low-transition sequence generation

**Ranking by expected fit, strongest first.**

**Q1-1. Recurrent Hidden Semi-Markov Model (R-HSMM) and neural HSMM decoders.** R-HSMM (Dai, Dai, Zhang, Li & Song, ICLR 2017, arXiv:1611.05335; Liu et al., IJCAI 2018, "Structured Inference for Recurrent Hidden Semi-Markov Model") replaces the per-slot emission of an RNN/transformer with an explicit segment-level generative process: at each segment boundary, sample a duration d from a learned duration distribution, sample a state z, and emit d observations conditioned on z. The model is trained by stochastic variational inference with a structured bidirectional posterior. **Why it helps here:** the model literally cannot over-fragment because the segment length is an output, not a side-effect of the local likelihood; the duration head is a direct, structural fix for the 100–150× over-fragmentation. The hierarchical-Bayesian semi-Markov model of Dube et al. (arXiv:2509.18414, 2025) is the closest direct demonstration on ATUS: their explicit hazard-component duration distribution yields a likelihood ratio of e^1.75 ≈ 5.74× over a transition-only model on 10-min ATUS slots. **Cost:** forward–backward over (slot, segment-length) is O(T · D_max), ≈48 · 12 = 576 vs 48 — a ~10× slowdown over the AR baseline but parallelisable. **Risk:** the segment-emission network can collapse to copying neighbour patterns if K-NN targets are not pre-smoothed. **Citation:** Dai et al., ICLR 2017, arXiv:1611.05335; Dube et al., arXiv:2509.18414, 2025.

**Q1-2. Hierarchical "schedule-template then refine" decoder (two-scale autoregression).** A coarse decoder emits a block-level program — e.g. (sleep 23:00–07:00, home; work 09:00–17:00, office; leisure 19:00–22:00, home) — typically 4–8 blocks, then a refine head conditions on the program and emits the 48 slot labels. Closest published instances: **Music Transformer** (Huang et al., ICLR 2019, arXiv:1809.04281) for symbolic music with explicit phrase/bar structure; **Compound Word Transformer** (Hsiao et al., AAAI 2021, arXiv:2101.02402) which factorises tokens into typed groups (note, duration, instrument) so durations are emitted alongside, not implied. **Why it helps here:** explicit duration tokens at the coarse level set the transition rate, and the refine head only has to *vary* within blocks. This is also exactly what DeepCAM (Liao et al., arXiv:2507.08871, 2025) does implicitly with its 15-min slotting + Activity Overconfidence Regularization. **Cost:** marginal — adds a ~3M-param coarse decoder. **Risk:** template granularity is a new hyperparameter; too coarse loses intra-block dynamics, too fine reinvents the original problem. **Citation:** Huang et al., ICLR 2019, arXiv:1809.04281.

**Q1-3. Mamba / S6 selective state-space decoders with input-dependent Δ.** Mamba (Gu & Dao, arXiv:2312.00752, 2023) uses an input-dependent timestep Δ that the authors explicitly motivate as a "filter / decay" mechanism — long Δ ≈ ignore input and let state persist. This is structurally suited to piecewise-constant generation because the selective mechanism *learns* to ignore noisy step-to-step variation. **Why it helps here:** the inductive bias toward persistence is exactly what is missing in the per-slot transformer. **Cost:** ≈parity with the transformer baseline; linear-time inference. **Risk:** Mamba's selectivity is a soft prior, not a structural one; the 2025 analysis "Mamba Modulation: On the Length Generalization of Mamba" (arXiv:2509.19633) notes there is "no fundamental theoretical justification" for treating Δ as a persistence proxy — it improves but does not guarantee low transition rate. **Citation:** Gu & Dao, arXiv:2312.00752, 2023.

**Q1-4. Neural CRF / Semi-CRF decoders with explicit segment scores.** Linear-chain and semi-CRF decoders (Sarawagi & Cohen, NIPS 2004; Liu et al., IEEE/ACM TASLP 2020, "Exploring Segment Representations for Neural Semi-Markov CRF") attach a Viterbi or forward–backward layer on top of a transformer encoder so that *segment scores* — not slot scores — drive learning. Constrained-language CRFs (RegCCRF, Papay et al., EMNLP 2022, arXiv:2106.07306) further forbid illegal label sequences during *training*, not just decoding. **Why it helps here:** transitions are scored at the segment level so the loss directly penalises over-fragmentation; RegCCRF gives the consistency constraints from Q4 for free. **Cost:** forward–backward over 10 classes × 48 slots × D_max is trivial. **Risk:** semi-CRFs are not naturally generative; they need to be paired with a stochastic decoder (e.g. ancestral sampling from the CRF's marginals). **Citation:** Papay, Klinger & Padó, EMNLP 2022, arXiv:2106.07306.

**Q1-5. Block-Recurrent Transformer.** Block-Recurrent Transformer (Hutchins et al., NeurIPS 2022, arXiv:2203.07852) applies a transformer layer in a recurrent fashion across blocks of tokens, maintaining a fixed-size block state. **Why it helps here:** if blocks are aligned with circadian segments (e.g. 4-hour or 2-hour windows), the recurrent state at block boundaries naturally encodes "what regime am I in" (sleep vs work vs leisure). **Cost:** ≈baseline. **Risk:** in the original paper the recurrence helps mainly for sequences >10k tokens; the 48-slot regime may not exercise its strengths. **Citation:** Hutchins et al., NeurIPS 2022, arXiv:2203.07852.

### Q2. Conditional discrete generative families beyond autoregressive transformers

**Q2-1. Masked Diffusion Language Models (MDLM) / MD4 with x₀-parameterisation.** MDLM (Sahoo et al., NeurIPS 2024, arXiv:2406.07524) and MD4 (Shi et al., NeurIPS 2024, arXiv:2406.04329) are simplified, Rao-Blackwellised absorbing-state discrete diffusion that train a bidirectional transformer to predict the clean tokens given a randomly-masked input, with a weighted cross-entropy on masked positions. **Why it helps here:** (a) **noise tolerance** — because training masks random subsets of the 48 slots, every example provides a *partial* supervision signal, so contradictory K-NN neighbours pull the model toward the conditional *distribution* not any one neighbour; (b) **parallel multi-task heads** — straightforward: emit (activity, home, co-presence) jointly at each masked position, sharing the bidirectional encoder; (c) **inference cost** — 8–32 denoising steps for 48 tokens, faster than 48 AR steps; (d) **hard consistency** — can be enforced via constrained denoising (resample illegal outputs at each step) or via the Q4 factorisation. **Risk:** the user must validate that the x₀ loss does not collapse the multilabel co-presence head (apply a Rao-Blackwellised binary head per channel rather than a softmax). **Citation:** Sahoo et al., NeurIPS 2024, arXiv:2406.07524.

**Q2-2. Score Entropy Discrete Diffusion (SEDD).** SEDD (Lou, Meng & Ermon, ICML 2024 Best Paper, arXiv:2310.16834) parameterises the reverse process by the *ratios* of the data distribution and trains by a novel "score entropy" loss. On the One Billion Word benchmark, SEDD Absorb achieves perplexity 32.79 vs D3PM Absorb's 77.50 — a 57.7% reduction — and across all benchmarks SEDD reports 25–75% perplexity reduction over previous discrete-diffusion models. Against autoregressive baselines, SEDD "outperforms GPT-2" on zero-shot tasks and the authors explicitly state SEDD "generates faithful text without requiring distribution annealing techniques like temperature scaling (around 6–8× better generative perplexity than un-annealed GPT-2), can trade compute and quality (similar quality with 32× fewer network evaluations)." **Why it helps here:** SEDD's principal empirical advantage is faithful unconditional generation without temperature annealing — i.e. it does not collapse to high-probability modes, which is exactly what is needed when the population marginal must be matched. **Cost:** slightly higher than MDLM (the ratio parameterisation is more expensive per step). **Risk:** the 2024 NVIDIA Cosmos Lab paper "Are Discrete Diffusion Models Better Than Auto-regressive Models in Text Generation? Uncovering a Hidden Numerical Issue" (Zheng et al., arXiv:2409.02908, 2024) explicitly states: "we uncover a hidden yet critical numerical precision issue that negatively impacts generation diversity in discrete diffusion sampling." For 48-slot diaries this is unlikely to dominate but should be checked. **Citation:** Lou et al., ICML 2024, arXiv:2310.16834.

**Q2-3. MaskGIT-style iterative parallel decoding.** MaskGIT (Chang et al., CVPR 2022, arXiv:2202.04200) trains a bidirectional transformer with masked prediction (BERT-style) and decodes by iteratively unmasking the most confident positions over 8–16 steps with a cosine mask schedule. **Why it helps here:** structurally identical to masked diffusion but with confidence-based scheduling, which is a strong inductive bias for "first decide the sleep block, then work, then fill in leisure" — i.e. iterative parallel decoding *naturally* produces hierarchical schedule templates as a side-effect of confidence ordering. **Cost:** ≈baseline; 8–16 forward passes for 48 tokens. **Risk:** for multi-task heads, "confidence" must be a learned scalar across heads, otherwise one head will dominate the unmasking schedule. **Citation:** Chang et al., CVPR 2022, arXiv:2202.04200.

**Q2-4. Discrete Flow Matching (CTMC / Fisher-Flow / SFM).** Discrete flow matching (Campbell et al., ICML 2024, "Generative Flows on Discrete State-Spaces", arXiv:2402.04997; Fisher-Flow, Davis et al., NeurIPS 2024, arXiv:2405.14664; Statistical/Categorical Flow Matching, Cheng et al., NeurIPS 2024, arXiv:2405.16441) is a continuous-time-Markov-chain or simplex-flow generalisation of flow matching to categorical data. **Why it helps here:** the simplex/Fisher formulation is *natively* multimodal-friendly because it models the categorical distribution over the simplex, which can split mass cleanly across plausible activities. **Cost:** comparable to diffusion, slightly more complex code; CTMC variants share infrastructure with D3PM. **Risk:** less mature than MDLM; fewer reference implementations for multi-head conditional generation. **Citation:** Campbell et al., ICML 2024, arXiv:2402.04997.

**Q2-5. D3PM (absorbing-state) baseline.** Austin, Johnson, Ho, Tarlow & van den Berg, NeurIPS 2021 (arXiv:2107.03006), is the foundational discrete-diffusion paper and is worth including as a control because it is the simplest. **Why it helps here:** mostly as a reference point — MDLM and SEDD dominate it empirically but D3PM has the cleanest theoretical link to BERT-style masked LM (absorbing-state D3PM with a particular schedule recovers MLM). **Cost:** ≈baseline. **Risk:** SEDD Absorb is 57.7% lower perplexity on LM1B; use only as control. **Citation:** Austin et al., NeurIPS 2021, arXiv:2107.03006.

### Q3. Noise-aware training for contradictory soft labels

**Q3-1. Batch-level distribution matching (Sinkhorn / MMD).** Genevay, Peyré & Cuturi, AISTATS 2018 ("Learning Generative Models with Sinkhorn Divergences", arXiv:1706.00292) trains a generator by minimising entropic-OT distance between minibatches of generated and real data, interpolating between MMD and Wasserstein via the entropy parameter ε. **Why it helps here:** this is the *correct* objective for the user's problem because the user already states "pointwise accuracy is irrelevant, distributional fidelity is everything." A K-NN target's individual identity becomes irrelevant — what matters is that the marginal distribution of generated diaries matches the observed batch. Implementing this as the primary loss (with CE as a regulariser) is the single most theoretically principled change. **Cost:** Sinkhorn iterations on a 256-batch with a Hamming or JS cost are sub-second on GPU. **Risk:** Sinkhorn gradients are biased for small ε; use the debiased Sinkhorn divergence of Feydy et al., AISTATS 2019, arXiv:1810.08278. **Citation:** Genevay et al., AISTATS 2018, arXiv:1706.00292.

**Q3-2. Robust loss functions tolerant to symmetric and instance-dependent noise.** Ghosh, Kumar & Sastry (AAAI 2017, arXiv:1712.09482) prove that mean-absolute-error (MAE) and reverse-cross-entropy are *theoretically robust* to symmetric multi-class label noise; "Generalized Cross Entropy" (Zhang & Sabuncu, NeurIPS 2018, arXiv:1805.07836) interpolates CE and MAE via a single q parameter. Recent: Kurucu et al., "Fractional Classification Loss" (arXiv:2508.06346, 2025) auto-calibrates the noise-robustness exponent during training. **Why it helps here:** the user already uses soft K-mean targets but applies CE; switching to GCE or MAE on the categorical activity head directly addresses the "decalibrated heads" symptom. **Cost:** zero. **Risk:** MAE alone slows convergence; GCE with q≈0.7 is the safer choice. **Citation:** Zhang & Sabuncu, NeurIPS 2018, arXiv:1805.07836.

**Q3-3. Credal-set / data-ambiguation losses.** Lienen & Hüllermeier, "Mitigating Label Noise through Data Ambiguation" (arXiv:2305.13764, 2023) represents the ambiguous target as a credal set (a set of plausible distributions over labels) and trains via a generalised risk that lets the model "choose its closest plausible target" during loss computation. **Why it helps here:** this is essentially the formal version of what the K-NN soft target *is* — the K neighbours define a credal set; the model should be allowed to match any element of it, not their mean. A near-perfect match to the user's training-pair construction. **Cost:** marginal; off-the-shelf loss. **Risk:** can under-fit if the credal set is too permissive. **Citation:** Lienen & Hüllermeier, arXiv:2305.13764, 2023.

**Q3-4. EM with latent "true" sequence (instance-dependent noise modelling).** Classical instance-dependent noise modelling (Patrini et al., CVPR 2017 "Making Deep Neural Networks Robust to Label Noise: A Loss Correction Approach", arXiv:1609.03683; Garg et al., arXiv:2101.11214) jointly fits a label-noise model alongside the classifier, treating the observed label as a noisy view of a latent true label. **Why it helps here:** the user's setting fits this exactly — the latent variable is "the *respondent's own* true diary" and the K neighbours are noisy observations of it. **Cost:** ≈2× training time. **Risk:** identifiability — without anchor points the noise model can absorb signal. Mitigated by using the respondent's *own* observed diary (K=1, i.e. self) as a clean anchor for a small fraction of examples. **Citation:** Patrini et al., CVPR 2017, arXiv:1609.03683.

**Q3-5. Bounded / forward-corrected robust losses.** "Label Noise: Correcting the Forward-Correction" (arXiv:2307.13100, 2023) shows that bounding the loss below by the noise-floor risk prevents overfitting on noisy targets. Conceptually relevant because the user's JS floor of 0.19 *is* a known noise floor — training should stop pushing CE below this value. **Why it helps here:** gives a principled early-stopping criterion. **Cost:** zero. **Citation:** arXiv:2307.13100, 2023.

### Q4. Multi-task / multi-head coupling with enforced consistency

**Q4-1. Semantic Probabilistic Layer (SPL).** Ahmed, Teso, Chang, Van den Broeck & Vergari, NeurIPS 2022 ("Semantic Probabilistic Layers for Neuro-Symbolic Learning", arXiv:2206.00426). SPL is a tractable probabilistic circuit that sits on top of a neural backbone and *exactly* zeros probability mass on label combinations that violate a symbolic constraint. **Why it helps here:** the user's constraints (¬home ⇒ ¬alone; work ⇒ ¬home ∧ colleagues; sleep ⇒ home) are exactly the kind of logical conjunctions that SPL handles natively; the layer is end-to-end differentiable. **Cost:** circuit compilation up-front; per-step overhead is small for the user's small joint label space (10 × 2 × 2⁹ = 5120 raw configurations, many illegal). **Risk:** writing the constraint set correctly is non-trivial. **Citation:** Ahmed et al., NeurIPS 2022, arXiv:2206.00426.

**Q4-2. Factorised joints with deterministic dependent heads.** The simplest architectural fix: factor p(activity, home, copresence | context) = p(activity | context) · p(home | activity, context) · p(copresence | activity, home, context), and make each conditional head respect hard rules deterministically (e.g. if activity=work, set p(home=1)=0 via masking the logits). This is a special case of structured prediction with hard rules and is what travel-demand papers like DeepCAM (Liao et al., arXiv:2507.08871, 2025) implicitly do at decode time. **Why it helps here:** zero training cost, immediate consistency, the model only has to learn the *unconstrained* degrees of freedom. **Cost:** zero. **Risk:** if the rules are wrong (e.g. some "work-from-home" exceptions are real, particularly in the 2022 cycle), the model cannot learn them. **Citation:** Liao et al., arXiv:2507.08871, 2025.

**Q4-3. Regular-constrained CRF (RegCCRF).** Papay, Klinger & Padó, EMNLP 2022 (arXiv:2106.07306). The CRF's label alphabet is restricted to sequences accepted by a user-supplied regular language during *both* training and decoding. **Why it helps here:** sleep-blocks-must-be-contiguous, work-can-only-occur-9am–6pm, etc. are exactly regular constraints. The constraint *during* training is provably tighter than constrained-decoding-only approaches. **Cost:** forward–backward over a product automaton; for small constraint automata, marginal. **Risk:** writing the regular language is an art. **Citation:** Papay et al., EMNLP 2022, arXiv:2106.07306.

**Q4-4. Product-of-experts decoding with energy-based potentials.** Each head emits a per-slot potential and decoding samples from the product (Hinton, NC 2002; SPENs, Belanger & McCallum, ICML 2016, arXiv:1511.06350). **Why it helps here:** clean way to combine activity, home and co-presence as separate experts and impose consistency via a hand-crafted compatibility potential. **Cost:** sampling from a PoE requires MCMC or Langevin steps; ≈10× slower at inference. **Risk:** training stability is the well-known SPEN problem. **Citation:** Belanger & McCallum, ICML 2016, arXiv:1511.06350.

**Q4-5. Constrained beam search and constrained sampling for AR decoders.** Standard trick from machine translation (Anderson et al., EMNLP 2017, "Guided Open Vocabulary Image Captioning with Constrained Beam Search", arXiv:1612.00576; Hokamp & Liu, ACL 2017, arXiv:1704.07138). **Why it helps here:** zero training cost, applies at decode time only. **Cost:** small constant overhead. **Risk:** does not propagate constraints into training, so the model still wastes capacity learning illegal combinations. **Citation:** Hokamp & Liu, ACL 2017, arXiv:1704.07138.

### Q5. Conditioning & demographic integration

**Q5-1. FiLM (Feature-wise Linear Modulation) from a small demographic encoder.** Perez, Strub, de Vries, Dumoulin & Courville, AAAI 2018 ("FiLM: Visual Reasoning with a General Conditioning Layer", arXiv:1709.07871). A small MLP encodes the demographic + cycle + stratum vector into per-layer (γ, β) that affinely modulate every channel of the backbone. **Why it helps here:** the user's current "concat at every decoder slot" is the *weakest* form of conditioning; FiLM is the next-cheapest upgrade and is empirically much stronger because it modulates *every* feature channel at *every* layer, not just the input. The cycle and stratum signals will be much harder to ignore. **Cost:** ≈100k extra params. **Risk:** none of note. **Citation:** Perez et al., AAAI 2018, arXiv:1709.07871.

**Q5-2. Cross-attention from a demographic encoder.** This is what DeepCAM (Liao et al., arXiv:2507.08871, 2025) uses with its role-feature-matching attention; the demographic vector is encoded into a small set of "demographic tokens" that the diary decoder cross-attends to. **Why it helps here:** more expressive than FiLM when the demographic vector has internal structure (household composition × age × employment interactions). **Cost:** ≈baseline. **Risk:** can over-fit small strata. **Citation:** Liao et al., arXiv:2507.08871, 2025.

**Q5-3. Hypernetworks generating per-cohort weights.** Ha, Dai & Le, ICLR 2017 ("HyperNetworks", arXiv:1609.09106). A hypernetwork maps the demographic vector to (some of) the diary decoder's weights. **Why it helps here:** explicit per-cohort specialisation; survives distribution shift between cycles because the cycle index parameterises the weight generator directly. **Cost:** the hypernetwork itself is small; the generated weights replace existing weights, so no inference cost. **Risk:** training instability when the demographic space has rare combinations; mitigate with low-rank weight generation. **Citation:** Ha et al., ICLR 2017, arXiv:1609.09106.

**Q5-4. Sparsely-gated Mixture-of-Experts over the demographic vector.** Shazeer et al., ICLR 2017 ("Outrageously Large Neural Networks: The Sparsely-Gated Mixture-of-Experts Layer", arXiv:1701.06538). The gating network is the demographic encoder; the top-k experts are activated per example. **Why it helps here:** with 4 cycles × 3 day strata × ~10 household-composition cells = ~120 effective sub-populations, an MoE with ~16 experts can specialise cleanly. **Cost:** training-time overhead for load-balancing; inference cost ≈baseline because only top-k experts run. **Risk:** rare strata (small co-presence channels) may be under-served; pair with per-channel pos_weight as the user already does. **Citation:** Shazeer et al., ICLR 2017, arXiv:1701.06538.

**Q5-5. Prefix-tuning per (cycle × stratum).** Li & Liang, ACL 2021 ("Prefix-Tuning: Optimizing Continuous Prompts for Generation", arXiv:2101.00190). Each of the 12 (cycle × stratum) cells gets its own learnable prefix vector prepended to the encoder input. **Why it helps here:** cheapest possible solution to the weekday/Saturday/Sunday ordering violation — explicitly different prefixes force differential behaviour. **Cost:** trivial. **Risk:** the model is still free to ignore the prefix; combine with FiLM. **Citation:** Li & Liang, ACL 2021, arXiv:2101.00190.

### Q6. Time-of-day inductive bias

**Q6-1. Fourier features for 24-h periodicity.** Tancik et al., NeurIPS 2020 ("Fourier Features Let Networks Learn High Frequency Functions in Low Dimensional Domains", arXiv:2006.10739). Encode slot index t ∈ {0,…,47} as [sin(2π t / 48), cos(2π t / 48), sin(4π t / 48), cos(4π t / 48), …] for K harmonics. **Why it helps here:** the diurnal cycle is *the* dominant prior in this dataset; replacing the learned absolute positional embedding with explicit Fourier features at multiple frequencies (1×, 2×, 4× per day = morning/evening, meal-time, hour-by-hour) is a direct injection of circadian structure. **Cost:** zero. **Risk:** none. **Citation:** Tancik et al., NeurIPS 2020, arXiv:2006.10739.

**Q6-2. Learned slot embeddings tied to a circadian template.** Initialise the 48 positional embeddings as a function of a learned "phase of day" embedding plus a sleep/work/leisure template, and let the model fine-tune. The embedding analogue of Q6-1. **Why it helps here:** lets the model learn deviations from the canonical schedule (shift workers, retirees) on top of an informative prior. **Cost:** zero. **Risk:** none. **Citation:** general practice; see Compound Word Transformer (Hsiao et al., AAAI 2021, arXiv:2101.02402) for typed positional embeddings.

**Q6-3. Hierarchical decoders that emit a coarse template then refine.** Already covered in Q1-2 (Music Transformer, Huang et al., ICLR 2019, arXiv:1809.04281; Compound Word Transformer, Hsiao et al., AAAI 2021, arXiv:2101.02402). **Why it helps here:** the coarse template *is* the circadian structure (sleep block, work block, leisure block). **Cost:** marginal. **Citation:** as above.

**Q6-4. Time2Vec / sinusoidal time embeddings.** Kazemi et al., 2019 ("Time2Vec: Learning a Vector Representation of Time", arXiv:1907.05321). Generalises Fourier features with one learnable non-periodic component plus K learnable periodic components. **Why it helps here:** gives the model flexibility on non-24-h periodicities (e.g. weekly within multi-day data) for future extensions. **Cost:** trivial. **Citation:** Kazemi et al., arXiv:1907.05321.

**Q6-5. Relative-time biases (ALiBi / RoPE) over slot distance.** Press et al., ICLR 2022 (ALiBi, arXiv:2108.12409); Su et al., 2021 (RoPE, arXiv:2104.09864). **Why it helps here:** lets attention degrade smoothly with slot distance, useful when modelling co-presence at meals (people are with family at distance |t1 − t2| ≤ 1 from their dinner slot). **Cost:** trivial; better than learned absolute positions on small datasets. **Risk:** removes some absolute-time information that Q6-1 puts back; use both. **Citation:** Press et al., ICLR 2022, arXiv:2108.12409.

### Adjacent literature (kept brief, only directly transferable ideas)

- **Travel-diary / activity-based modeling.** Beyond the three direct prior works above, Borysov et al. ("How to generate micro-agents? A deep generative modeling approach to population synthesis," *Transportation Research Part C*, 2019) on VAE-based population synthesis and Anvari & Bell, "A Differentially Private Multi-Output Deep Generative Networks Approach for Activity Diary Synthesis" (arXiv:2012.14574, 2020) are the closest prior published ML uses of demographic-conditioned diary generation with multi-output structure. Liao et al.'s DeepAM (arXiv:2405.17468) and DeepCAM (arXiv:2507.08871) supersede these on architecture and evaluation.
- **Symbolic music generation.** Music Transformer (Huang et al., ICLR 2019), Compound Word Transformer (Hsiao et al., AAAI 2021) and MuseFormer give a mature template for hierarchical decoding of long, low-transition-rate sequences with explicit duration tokens — directly transferable.
- **Human activity recognition (HAR).** The HAR community has long used neural HSMMs and segmental CRFs on accelerometer streams (Hammerla et al., IJCAI 2016). Their pre-segmentation tricks (median-filter, mode-pooling) are transferable as cheap post-processing baselines.
- **Occupancy schedule synthesis for buildings.** Chen et al., "Occupancy Simulator" (LBNL 2017) and the RAFT-VG VAE-GAN approach (*Building Simulation*, 2025) are the de-facto baselines in the user's downstream application; benchmark against these.

## Synthesis — Ranked Shortlist of 10 Architectures to Prototype on a 5% Stratified Sample

The 5% stratified sample (stratified by cycle × day-stratum × household-composition) of the user's 150k pool is ≈ 7,500 respondents. (For reference, a 5% slice of the full ATUS 2003–2023 pool of ~180,000 respondents would be ≈ 9,000 — the user should size their sample to retain ≥ 200 examples per rare co-presence channel × stratum cell.)

The two should be compared head-to-head on the composite score; if both beat 0.64, the family with cheaper inference (MDLM) wins for the building-energy-simulation downstream use case, which calls millions of diaries per scenario.

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 (1–2 weeks, must do):** Implement item 6 (composite conditioning upgrade: FiLM + Fourier features + prefix-tuning per cycle × stratum) and item 5 (SPL or factorised-joint with hard logical masking). These are the cheapest interventions and address two of the user's named symptoms (weekday/weekend ordering violation; soft-consistency failures) directly. **Threshold to change recommendation:** if weekday < Saturday ≤ Sunday holds in ≥ 10 of 12 cells after this, the under-conditioning hypothesis is confirmed and the user can move on; otherwise, the demographic vector itself may be insufficiently informative and feature engineering on the conditioning side is needed.

**Stage 2 (2–4 weeks, highest leverage):** Implement item 1 (neural HSMM decoder) AND item 2 (MDLM with multi-task heads) as parallel prototypes. These are the two structural reframings most likely to break the 0.64 ceiling. **Threshold to change recommendation:** if neither lowers the activity transition-rate ratio below 5× (from 100–150×), the problem is not over-fragmentation per se but over-fragmentation *induced by* the K-NN supervision — in which case proceed to Stage 3 immediately rather than tuning Stage 2.

**Stage 3 (3–6 weeks, deeper fix):** Implement item 4 (Sinkhorn distribution-matching loss as primary objective, CE as regulariser) and item 10 (GCE + credal-set loss on K-NN targets). These address the noisy-supervision pathology head-on. **Threshold to change recommendation:** if the per-(cycle × stratum) at-home rate RMS drops below 5 pp in all 12 cells, the distributional-fidelity objective is working and should become the default training mode.

**Stage 4 (if Stages 1–3 do not close the gap):** Consider items 3 (hierarchical template → refine decoder) and 9 (cycle × stratum hypernetwork). These are higher-variance bets; 3 is most likely to help if the failure mode is "blocks are right length but boundaries wrong," and 9 is most likely to help if the failure mode is "model collapses to a single cycle's behaviour."

**Do NOT in any stage:** retrain on a larger demographic-similarity radius (K > 5) hoping to smooth the noise — this makes the credal set wider and worsens the JS floor. Instead, change the *loss* to be tolerant of the existing noise.

## Caveats

1. **The JSD = 0.001 figure from the Deep Activity Model paper (Liao et al., arXiv:2405.17468, 2024) could not be independently verified** in the final fetch; the related DeepCAM paper (Liao et al., arXiv:2507.08871, 2025) reports JSDs of 0.001 for *corridor-level traffic-speed* (not activity-class), 0.002 for activity start-time and ≤ 0.006 for 24-h activity pattern. The user should treat the 0.001 number with caution and re-verify from the source PDF before using it as a benchmark.

2. **Mamba's "duration modelling" via input-dependent Δ is a soft inductive bias, not a structural constraint.** The 2025 paper "Mamba Modulation: On the Length Generalization of Mamba" (arXiv:2509.19633) explicitly notes there is "no fundamental theoretical justification" for treating Δ as a persistence proxy. Use Mamba as an *additional* prior, not as a substitute for explicit duration modelling.

3. **Discrete-diffusion sampling has known precision-related diversity issues.** Zheng et al., NVIDIA Cosmos Lab, "Are Discrete Diffusion Models Better Than Auto-regressive Models in Text Generation? Uncovering a Hidden Numerical Issue" (arXiv:2409.02908, 2024), state: "we uncover a hidden yet critical numerical precision issue that negatively impacts generation diversity in discrete diffusion sampling." For population-statistic matching, evaluate diversity (per-channel entropy of generated diaries) explicitly, not just JS divergence to marginals.

4. **SPL constraint specification is a non-trivial domain task** and the constraints encoded must be exactly right; an incorrect constraint will zero out legal behaviours (e.g. real WFH workers in the 2022 cycle). The user should learn a small number of "soft" constraints from data first (rule-mining on the observed diaries) before hard-coding them into SPL.

5. **Mamba venue citation:** the Mamba paper (Gu & Dao) is cited here as arXiv:2312.00752 (2023). Several secondary sources attribute it to COLM 2024, but the authors' own GitHub (state-spaces/mamba) cites it only as an arXiv preprint; users should verify against the official COLM 2024 proceedings before formal citation.

6. **No published architecture in this review has been demonstrated end-to-end on the user's exact triple-output coupled-consistency problem.** The closest is DeepCAM (Liao et al., arXiv:2507.08871, 2025), which jointly models activity + location + co-presence at 15-min resolution on NHTS but does not use explicit duration modelling or discrete diffusion. The user's prototyping should therefore be treated as genuinely novel architectural research, not as application of a known recipe.
