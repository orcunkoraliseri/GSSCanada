Dear Dr. Hachem-Vermette,

I hope you are well. I would like to update you on our second journal paper, "From 'How Much' to
'When': Forecasting the Residential Energy Load Shape from a Calibrated Behavioural Occupancy
Time-Series (Canada, 2005–2030)", and ask for your approval to submit it to Applied Energy.

Building Simulation rejected the paper on 15 September 2026 (three reviewers). The reviewers found the
research question valuable, but asked for stronger evidence and a clearer, shorter paper. Since then I
have revised it as follows:

- **Corrected 2030 projection:** the error in the 2030 occupancy is fixed and the building simulations
  were re-run (5,997 runs).
- **Scenarios instead of one forecast:** 2030 is shown under stated work-from-home scenarios (the shift
  stays, partly reverses, or fully reverses), plus one reversion check.
- **Confidence intervals:** every reported change has a confidence interval, so we only claim changes
  the data support.
- **Check against measured data:** the simulated hourly load shape is compared with a year of measured
  2022 electricity data for Toronto and Ontario.
- **Comparison with a simpler method:** a simple average-profile schedule, run on the same households,
  loses the spread of household peak times; our household-level model keeps it.
- **Held-out test:** the occupancy model is trained up to 2015 and tested on the unseen 2022 survey.
- **Clearer paper:** shorter sentences, six method sections instead of twelve, new method figures, a
  source for every equation, and a shorter Discussion that keeps all limitations.

The main message: under the main scenario, annual electricity stays almost flat (+0.12 %), while the
midday share of energy rises 0.73 and the load factor 0.49 percentage points. For grid planning, the
change is mainly about when homes use electricity, not how much.

The manuscript, supplementary material and cover letter are attached. If you approve, I will submit
it. I am happy to make any changes you would like first.
