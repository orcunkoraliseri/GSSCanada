# Cover letter

22 September 2026

The Editors
*Applied Energy*

Dear Editors,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript, *From "How Much" to "When": Forecasting the Residential
Energy Load Shape from a Calibrated Behavioural Occupancy Time-Series (Canada, 2005–2030)*, for
consideration as a Full Length Research Article in *Applied Energy*.

Building energy models of the housing stock still run on fixed occupancy schedules. Yet the COVID-19
pandemic changed when people are at home more than how much energy homes use. We build a
household-level occupancy model from four cycles of the Statistics Canada time-use survey (2005 to
2022) and check it on a held-out survey year. We link it to a 2021 census household frame and carry it
into paired building energy simulations across four dwelling archetypes and six Canadian cities
(5,997 simulation runs). We then compare 2022 with four stated 2030 scenarios for how much of the
pandemic shift persists.

In 2022, the weekday at-home rate sits 4.73 percentage points above its 2005-to-2015 trend. Under the
main 2030 scenario, annual electricity is nearly flat, rising 0.12 percent, while the midday share
rises 0.73 and the load factor 0.49 percentage points. A simpler average-profile method, run on the
same households, collapses the household-to-household spread in peak timing almost to zero; only the
household-level model keeps it. The simulated load shape is also checked against a year of measured
Toronto and Ontario electricity data. For grid planning, the shift is mainly a question of timing,
not of sizing.

We submit to *Applied Energy* because the journal's scope names energy users modelling, forecasting,
energy in buildings, and flexibility and demand response. The paper turns survey-based behaviour into
stock-scale load-shape metrics (load factor, midday share, peak timing and evening ramp) that grid and
demand-response planners can use directly. The method needs only a repeated national time-use survey,
a census-type household frame and a national end-use benchmark, so it carries over to the United
States and Europe.

**Relationship to our earlier work.** This is the second study in one research line. A companion
manuscript, currently under review at the *Journal of Building Performance Simulation*, asked *how
much*: it compared period-specific occupancy datasets against one default schedule in one climate
zone and reported annual corrections. A companion conference study (Iseri and Hachem-Vermette 2026)
covered three climate-zone cities. The present paper asks *when*. It compares survey cycles against
each other within the same households, carries occupancy through the pandemic break to 2030, covers
four national archetypes across six cities, and reports the daily load shape rather than annual
totals. Both earlier studies are cited and compared in the manuscript (Section 1.2 and Appendix A).

The manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under consideration
elsewhere. All authors have approved its submission. We have no competing interests to declare. The
use of generative AI tools is declared at the end of the manuscript.

Thank you for considering our work.

Sincerely,

Orcun Koral Iseri, on behalf of both authors
