## 4. Positive Control Result

- **Citation:** Barrero, J.M., Bloom, N. and Davis, S.J. (2021), *Why working from home will stick*, National Bureau of Economic Research (NBER) Working Paper No. 28731, Cambridge, MA.
- **Crossref DOI verification:** Verified on Crossref API (`https://api.crossref.org/works/10.3386/w28731`).
  Crossref metadata confirmation: Title: "Why Working From Home Will Stick"; Authors: Jose Maria Barrero, Nicholas Bloom, Steven J. Davis; Publisher: National Bureau of Economic Research; Issued: April 2021; DOI: `10.3386/w28731`.
- **Primary source confirmation:** Opened and read directly via NBER repository (`https://www.nber.org/papers/w28731`).
- **Key numbers extracted and verified:**
  1. Pre-pandemic baseline: 5.0% of full paid workdays worked from home (Section 1, p. 1; Section 3, Table 1).
  2. Pandemic peak: Over 60% of workdays worked from home in spring 2020 (Section 2, p. 4).
  3. Post-pandemic expectation (employer plans): 20.5% (roughly 20%) of full workdays supplied from home (Section 1, p. 1; Section 4, p. 13, Table 2).
  4. Core mechanism: Survey of Working Arrangements and Attitudes (SWAA) microdata (30,000+ US working-age respondents) demonstrates that WFH will stick at four times pre-pandemic levels due to reduced stigma, substantial sunk investments in physical and human capital for remote work, lingering health concerns, rapid innovation in remote collaboration technology, and high worker valuation of flexibility (average valuation of 8% of wage).
- **Status:** CONFIRMED AND RESOLVED. The positive control paper is genuine, accessible, and all referenced figures match the published text.

## 5. What I Could Not Find or Open

- I could not find any published primary survey or statistical agency release reporting that national aggregate work-from-home rates continued to rise after 2023 in either Canada or the United States (official Canadian LFS and US SWAA series both report stabilization or gradual downward drift between 2023 and 2026).
- I could not find any published peer-reviewed study or official statistical report finding or predicting that work-from-home prevalence will fall all the way back to 2019 pre-pandemic levels (around 7% of workers or workdays), as all opened sources project a permanent hybrid equilibrium at 2x to 4x pre-pandemic levels.
- I could not find any empirical utility meter study measuring simultaneous sub-hourly interval smart-meter electricity and gas telemetry for both residential homes and commercial office buildings under a controlled hybrid-work policy in Canada (existing Canadian evidence relies on calibrated building energy simulation archetypes coupled to empirical employee survey diaries, as in Sepanta et al. 2024).
