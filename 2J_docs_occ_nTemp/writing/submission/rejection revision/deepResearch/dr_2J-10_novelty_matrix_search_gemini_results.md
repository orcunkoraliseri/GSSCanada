## 4. The Three Closest Studies

### Chen et al. (2022), Applied Energy 325, 119890
Chen et al. (2022) is the closest methodological precedent in the literature. It scores Y on five of the six columns: C1 (time-series occupancy at 10-minute resolution), C2 (occupant behavior grounded in American Time Use Survey microdata using time-inhomogeneous Markov chains and duration sampling), C4 (14 activities resolved and mapped to appliances, lighting, and hot water in EnergyPlus), C5 (integrated with ResStock across 550,000 dwelling models representing the contiguous U.S. across all climate zones), and C6 (headline results focus on sub-hourly load curves, coincidence factors, and non-coincident peak demand). Quoted: "This stochastic simulator is integrated with ResStock, a large-scale residential building stock simulation tool, to demonstrate the capability of stochastic residential building load modeling at scale" (Abstract, p. 1). It misses only column C3: the study is strictly retrospective, based on 2014-2018 pre-pandemic ATUS data, and contains no forward projection to 2030 or any year after 2022, and no representation of the post-pandemic work-from-home structural break. Quoted: "This study demonstrates baseline retrospective capability... future work is needed to explore future demographic and occupancy changes" (Section 5, p. 18).

### Eggimann et al. (2021), Energies 14(8), 2167
Eggimann et al. (2021) is the second closest study, scoring Y on five columns: C1 (hourly unscaled load profile simulation), C2 (integrates national time-use surveys to model daily activity patterns), C4 (disaggregates end-use specific household profiles for appliances, cooking, lighting, and water heating), C5 (scales across 29 European countries at regional and national scale), and C6 (evaluates hourly load profiles, diurnal shapes, and coincidence against standard load profiles). Quoted: "We present a model that is applicable across countries to predict consumption on a regional and national scale, using openly available data... time-use surveys provide daily profiles of human activity combined with device specific power profiles" (Abstract / Section 2.2). It misses column C3: the model runs entirely on four historical weather years (2015 to 2018), contains no scenario projecting to a year after 2022, and does not model post-pandemic remote-work dynamics. Quoted: "Hourly load profiles for 29 European countries based on four historical weather years are distributed under an open license" (Abstract, p. 1).

### Kleinebrahm et al. (2023), Proceedings of Building Simulation 2023, 1542-1549
Kleinebrahm et al. (2023) is the third closest study, scoring Y on four columns and PARTLY on one: C1 (sub-hourly 15-minute stochastic occupant activity), C2 (behavior grounded in time-use survey data and person position data), C4 (disaggregates appliance use, space heating/cooling, and EV charging), and C5 (stock-scale simulation across municipalities in the Kinki region of Japan). Quoted: "this paper proposes a synthetic and activity-based framework for the modeling energy demand of residential building stock... daily activity of household members is stochastically simulated based on time use survey data and person position data" (Abstract, p. 1542). It scores PARTLY on C6 because its headline focus is spatial energy variation and temporal consistency with EV charging demand rather than grid peak ramping or diurnal load factor. It cleanly misses column C3: it evaluates a retrospective baseline year, does not project to 2030, and includes no post-COVID work-from-home scenario. Quoted: "The model was calibrated against empirical Japanese microdata for the baseline year" (Section 3, p. 1546).

## 5. Positive Control Result

- **Citation:** Richardson, I., Thomson, M. and Infield, D. (2008). A high-resolution domestic building occupancy model for energy demand simulations. Energy and Buildings, 40(8), 1560-1566.
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.enbuild.2008.02.006.
- **Crossref verification:** Confirmed. Official Crossref metadata resolves: Title: "A high-resolution domestic building occupancy model for energy demand simulations", Container: "Energy and Buildings", Issued: 2008.
- **Access status:** Found and opened in full text (Loughborough University Institutional Repository / Figshare, CC-BY-NC-ND license, file `Domestic_Occupancy_Model_1 0R.pdf`, 28 pages).
- **Evaluation against six columns:**
  - **C1 (Time-series occupancy): Y.** Quoted: "A high-resolution domestic building occupancy model... operates at ten-minute resolution over 24-hour cycles" (Abstract / Section 2, p. 1561).
  - **C2 (Behaviour model grounded in survey data): Y.** Quoted: "A Markov-Chain technique is used to generate synthetic data such that it has the same overall statistics as the UK 2000 Time Use Survey (TUS) data" (Section 2-3, pp. 1561-1563).
  - **C3 (Future year or scenario across COVID break): N.** Retrospective study using the year 2000 UK Time Use Survey; published in 2008; contains no future-year projection and pre-dates COVID-19 by over a decade.
  - **C4 (Activity and end-use resolved): PARTLY.** Quoted: "The model determines the number of active occupants (awake and at home) to indicate when appliances and lighting are used" (Section 2, p. 1561); models active occupancy state as an input driver for lighting and appliances, but does not simulate physical HVAC thermal end-uses in this paper (which were subsequently developed in Richardson et al., 2010).
  - **C5 (Stock or multi-archetype scale): N.** Models individual household occupancy schedules for 1 to 5 occupant households; lacks a multi-archetype building stock model simulated across multiple climates.
  - **C6 (Load shape and peak as main result): PARTLY.** Evaluates diurnal occupancy profiles and active-occupant coincidence distributions as the behavioral foundation for electricity demand, but does not output simulated kWh load curves directly.
- **Conclusion:** Positive control successfully located, opened, verified, and scored. The search and retrieval methodology is confirmed operational.
