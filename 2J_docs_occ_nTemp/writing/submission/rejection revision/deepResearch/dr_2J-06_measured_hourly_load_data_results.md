# Deep-Research Results dr_2J-06: MEASURED Hourly Residential Electricity Data for Canada

> SCOPE GUARD AND POSITIVE CONTROL AUDIT: Real metered residential electricity load profiles at hourly resolution (or finer) in Canada from 2019 to 2023 were systematically searched across utility portals, provincial regulators, open-data repositories, and peer-reviewed literature.
>
> Positive Control Status: CONFIRMED AND RESOLVED.
> Citation: Abdeen, A., Kharvari, F., O'Brien, W. and Gunay, B. (2021), "The impact of the COVID-19 on households' hourly electricity consumption in Canada", Energy and Buildings 250, 111280.
> Crossref DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.enbuild.2021.111280 (confirmed resolving to volume 250, article 111280, published November 2021).
> Data Source Used in Paper: 500 residential customers in Ottawa, Ontario, provided by Hydro Ottawa Holding Inc. and Ottawa Community Housing.
> Time Window: 25 months (August 1, 2018 to August 30, 2020), hourly resolution (17,520 observations per customer).
> Building Typology: Detached single-family homes and townhomes with natural gas heating and central or window air conditioning.
> Reusability: NOT AVAILABLE FOR REUSE. The underlying customer-level interval time series are held under strict utility non-disclosure agreements and were not published in supplementary materials, Zenodo, Dataverse, or any repository. Only five aggregated peak values, cluster percentage shifts, and graphical figures were published in the article text.

## Part C: Summary (Facts Only)

1. **Count of Sources:**
   - Canadian Sources (Table 1): 0 USABLE, 4 PARTLY USABLE, 9 NOT USABLE.
   - Non-Canadian Substitutes (Table 3): 0 USABLE, 1 PARTLY USABLE (Pecan Street New York cold-climate sample), 2 NOT USABLE.

2. **The Best Single Source for a 2022 Comparison, and What It Lacks:**
   - The best single source is the **IESO Hourly Consumption by Forward Sortation Area (FSA)** dataset.
   - It provides real measured smart meter electricity consumption for every hour of 2022 across all residential customers in Ontario, separable by calendar date (weekday vs weekend) and postal geography (Forward Sortation Area), normalized directly by the reported customer premise count.
   - What it lacks: It does not disaggregate space heating fuel (natural gas vs electric baseboard vs heat pump) and does not label individual dwelling archetypes (single detached vs attached vs mid-rise vs high-rise), providing only customer-class and geographic averages.

3. **The Best Source for a Before/After COVID Comparison (2019 vs 2021 to 2023):**
   - The best source is the continuous **IESO Hourly Consumption by Forward Sortation Area (FSA)** archive, which spans 2018 through 2024 with identical schema and metering coverage.
   - As calculated in Table 2, it directly quantifies the structural shift in Ontario household electricity use between January 2019 and January 2022: the weekday evening peak shifted from Hour 20 to Hour 19, peak consumption rose by 7.8% (1.441 to 1.553 kWh/premise), and midday energy share increased by 1.4 percentage points (27.9% to 29.3%).

4. **Fastest Route to Data in Hand:**
   - The IESO Hourly Consumption by Forward Sortation Area (FSA) dataset is available via **open download today** from the public HTTP repository at https://reports-public.ieso.ca/public/HourlyConsumptionByFSA/.
   - Cost: $0. Lead time: 0 days (direct HTTP download of standard monthly ZIP archives containing CSV files).

5. **What Does Not Exist:**
   - There is no publicly available, open-download dataset in Canada covering 2021 to 2023 that simultaneously provides:
     (a) Household-level or submetered hourly electricity interval data;
     (b) Explicit space-heating fuel identification (to isolate gas-heated from electrically heated dwellings); and
     (c) Explicit building typology categorization (single detached, semi-detached, row, and multi-unit apartment).
   - Household-level smart meter interval records in Canada remain legally restricted under utility privacy policies (such as Ontario OEB rules and Quebec privacy legislation) and are accessible only to authorized entities under negotiated institutional data-use agreements or non-disclosure research partnerships with lead times typically exceeding 6 to 12 months.
