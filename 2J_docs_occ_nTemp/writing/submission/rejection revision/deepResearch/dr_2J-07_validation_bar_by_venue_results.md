## Part C: Summary (Facts Only)

1. **Share of Sampled Articles with a Measured Hourly Check:**
   - Applied Energy: 4 out of 9 sampled articles (44.4%) checked simulated or predicted load profiles against measured hourly or sub-hourly interval data.
   - Sustainable Cities and Society: 2 out of 9 sampled articles (22.2%) checked against measured hourly or sub-hourly data.
   - Journal of Building Engineering: 3 out of 9 sampled articles (33.3%) checked against measured hourly or sub-hourly data.
   - Across the combined sample of 27 recent matching articles (2022 to 2026), only 9 articles (33.3%) included an empirical check of hourly load shapes against measured interval data. The remaining 18 articles (66.7%) relied on annual/monthly billing totals, EPC certificates, inter-model comparisons, or no measured data check at all.

2. **Articles Published with NO Measured-Data Check at All (with DOIs):**
   - Matching papers with zero measured energy data checks (relying instead on inter-model comparisons, literature benchmark ranges, or uncalibrated forward runs) were published in all three candidate journals:
     - Applied Energy:
       - Mitra et al. (2022): https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apenergy.2022.119765 (held-out survey distributions and uncalibrated EnergyPlus prototype runs)
       - Battini et al. (2023): https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apenergy.2023.121570 (inter-model comparison against full-order EnergyPlus models)
       - Palladino (2023): https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apenergy.2023.121365 (calibrated against national EPC certificate statistics, no interval meter check)
       - Gergely et al. (2025): https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apenergy.2024.124934 (simulated in IDA ICE, checked against published literature archetype standards)
     - Sustainable Cities and Society:
       - Duman et al. (2023): https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scs.2023.104628 (bottom-up appliance schedule simulation compared only to survey responses)
       - Michellod et al. (2025): https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scs.2025.106486 (national stock simulation compared only against national annual SFOE statistics)
       - Zhang et al. (2026): https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scs.2026.107348 (URBANopt synthetic simulation checked against feeder peak estimates)
       - Li et al. (2024): https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scs.2024.105718 (coupled WRF-EnergyPlus sensitivity comparisons)
     - Journal of Building Engineering:
       - Sood et al. (2023): https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jobe.2023.106872 (stochastic time-use schedules compared to CIBSE Guide A deterministic profiles)
       - Lyu et al. (2022): https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jobe.2022.104067 (compared against national energy survey seasonal distributions)
       - Tognon et al. (2026): https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jobe.2025.114950 (national stock cooling simulations compared only against EPC annual heating benchmarks)
       - Lee et al. (2023): https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jobe.2023.106962 (compared against ASHRAE 90.1 standard deterministic schedules)
       - Li et al. (2024): https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jobe.2024.108675 (evaluated against municipal annual energy quota benchmarks)

3. **Articles Validated Against Measured Data from a Different Country (with DOIs):**
   - YES: In Applied Energy, Gao et al. (2026, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apenergy.2026.128315) developed a general residential demand-side flexibility framework and validated its performance using public foreign datasets from the United Kingdom (UK-DALE) and the United States (REDD).

4. **What Neither the Articles Nor the Journal Pages Reveal:**
   - Neither the Guide for Authors nor the published articles articulate an explicit quantitative threshold for load-shape validation errors (e.g., maximum permissible hourly CVRMSE or NMBE) for stock-scale residential simulation manuscripts to be acceptable.
   - None of the candidate journals require that national or regional building stock models be validated against empirical smart meter interval data from the specific country or region being simulated. In peer-reviewed practice across all three venues, authors frequently rely on annual national statistical balances, held-out survey distributions, or benchmark code archetypes when empirical high-frequency load telemetry is unavailable or restricted by utility privacy frameworks.
