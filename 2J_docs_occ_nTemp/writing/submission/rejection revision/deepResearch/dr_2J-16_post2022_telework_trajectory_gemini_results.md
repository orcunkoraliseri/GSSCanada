# Deep-Research Results dr_2J-16: Post-2022 Work-from-Home Trajectory, Canada (Citation Search and Number Check)

## 1. Summary Verdict

* **Place 1 (Number Check for 41.1% in April 2020 and 18.7% in May 2024):** CONFIRMED (with essential denominator and questionnaire comparability caveats).
  Both numbers are genuine, verified Statistics Canada figures. However, they cannot be cited as an unadjusted, seamless single time series without methodological qualification. The April 2020 figure (41.1%) was calculated among workers aged 15 to 69 who were actively *at work* during the initial pandemic shutdown (excluding over 2.4 million employed workers temporarily absent), whereas the May 2024 figure (18.7%) is calculated across *all employed* persons aged 15 to 69 under normal seasonal absence levels. Furthermore, the questionnaire evolved through three distinct question variants (C1 in 2020 to 2021, C2 in early 2022 which suffered upwards overestimation, and C3 implemented in May 2022).
* **Place 2 (Post-2022 Trajectory of Work from Home):** FOUND.
  Empirical measurements from Statistics Canada (May 2022, May 2023, May 2024 LFS supplements) and international benchmarks (US Survey of Working Arrangements and Attitudes / Barrero et al. 2023 in *Journal of Economic Perspectives*; UK Office for National Statistics) document post-2022 dynamics. In Canada, mostly-home work experienced a gradual continuation of its post-peak decline (from 22.4% in May 2022 to 20.1% in May 2023 and 18.7% in May 2024), while hybrid arrangements expanded, stabilizing overall remote exposure well above pre-pandemic baselines.

### Methodological Assessment of Comparability for Place 1
1. **Denominator Differences:** In April 2020, 2.4 million Canadians were employed but absent from work due to pandemic shutdowns. If calculated over *all employed* persons (16.185 million), the 5.0 million home workers represent 30.9%. The 41.1% figure is obtained only when restricting the denominator to the 12.0 million workers who were *at work* and worked at least some hours. By May 2024, the labor market had normalized, so the 18.7% figure is reported across all employed persons aged 15 to 69.
2. **Question Formulation Differences:**
   - April 2020 used Question C1: "Last week, in which of these locations did [respondent] work the most hours? 1. Fixed location outside home; 2. Outside home with no fixed location; 3. At home; 4. Absent from work."
   - May 2024 used Question C3: Added explicit home references in response categories after Statistics Canada discovered that Question C2 (used from January to April 2022) had inflated work-from-home rates by 8 to 15 percentage points.
3. **Manuscript Recommendation:** When comparing these figures in the Discussion, state:
   "In Canada, the share of workers working mostly from home declined from an initial pandemic peak of 41.1 percent among active workers in April 2020 to 18.7 percent of all employed workers in May 2024 (Morissette et al., 2023; Statistics Canada, 2024)."

Consequently, the share of that shift retained in 2030 is treated as an explicit, stated assumption tested across more than one value."
