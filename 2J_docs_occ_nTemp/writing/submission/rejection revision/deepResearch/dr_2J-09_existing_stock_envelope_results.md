# Deep-Research Results dr_2J-09: Envelope of the Existing Canadian Single-Detached Housing Stock

## 1. Summary Verdict

**Verdict: USABLE**

Measured and audit-based envelope parameters for the existing Canadian single-detached housing stock were successfully identified across all required categories:
- Above-grade wall effective thermal resistance (RSI)
- Ceiling / attic effective thermal resistance (RSI)
- Foundation wall effective thermal resistance (RSI), with empirical confirmation that basements dominate the existing stock (89% to 95% of single-detached homes)
- Window thermal transmittance (U-value) and solar heat gain coefficient (SHGC), with complete empirical glazing type distributions
- Airtightness measured via calibrated blower-door depressurization tests (ACH at 50 Pa)

These parameters are available at both the national aggregate level and resolved by construction period (vintage) and province / region (Ontario, Quebec, British Columbia, Alberta, Manitoba / Prairies, and Atlantic Canada).

The values originate from large-scale on-site physical audits and blower-door pressurization tests conducted under Natural Resources Canada (NRCan) national programmes (the EnerGuide for Houses Database, EGHD, and the EnerGuide Rating System, ERS), statistically stratified against Statistics Canada population surveys (Survey of Household Energy Use, SHEU-03 and SHEU-2015). Key sources include the peer-reviewed CSDDRD database (Swan et al. 2009, Swan 2010; N = 14,030 single-detached homes drawn from 187,821 audits), the CanmetENERGY national housing archetype database (NRCan 2020; N = 5,023 single-detached archetypes drawn from the national audit database), and the national blower-door depressurization dataset analyzed by Khemet and Richman (2018; N = approximately 900,000 single-family houses).

All values are reported below with explicit provenance, sample sizes, collection years, evidentiary labels (MEASURED, SURVEY, or ASSUMED), and table or page citations.

## 3. Positive Control Result

The positive control was verified and is confirmed:
- Citation: Swan, L.G. and Ugursal, V.I. (2009). Modeling of end-use energy consumption in the residential sector: A review of modeling techniques. *Renewable and Sustainable Energy Reviews*, 13(8), 1819-1835. DOI: 10.1016/j.rser.2008.09.033.
- Verification status on Crossref: Verified. Container: Renewable and Sustainable Energy Reviews, Volume 13, Issue 8, pp. 1819-1835, publication year 2009.
- Linkage to a Canadian housing database with envelope values:
  Yes. In section 4.2 ("Sample method"), Swan and Ugursal review the Canadian residential housing stock modelling lineage, specifically citing Farahbakhsh et al. (1998) who utilized 8,767 house audit files from the 1993 Survey of Household Energy Use (SHEU-93). 
  More importantly, concurrent and subsequent work by the same research group (Lukas G. Swan, V. Ismet Ugursal, and Ian Beausoleil-Morrison) directly resolved this data gap by constructing the Canadian Single-Detached and Double/Row Housing Database (CSDDRD) and coupling it to the Canadian Hybrid Residential End-Use Energy and GHG Emissions Model (CHREM):
  1. Swan, L.G., Ugursal, V.I., Beausoleil-Morrison, I. (2009). A database of house descriptions representative of the Canadian housing stock for coupling to building energy performance simulation. *Journal of Building Performance Simulation*, 2(2), 75-84. DOI: 10.1080/19401490802491827.
  2. Swan, L.G. (2010). *Residential Sector Energy and GHG Emissions Model for the Assessment of New Technologies*. PhD Dissertation, Department of Mechanical Engineering, Dalhousie University, Halifax, Nova Scotia, Canada. Direct link: https://dalspace.library.dal.ca/bitstream/handle/10222/12998/Swan_Lukas_PhD_MECH_August_2010.pdf.
  3. Swan, L.G., Ugursal, V.I., Beausoleil-Morrison, I. (2013). Hybrid residential end-use energy and greenhouse gas emissions model - development and verification for Canada. *Journal of Building Performance Simulation*, 6(1), 1-23. DOI: 10.1080/19401493.2011.594906.
  
  In these works, the team obtained 187,821 on-site energy audit records collected from 1997 through 2006 under NRCan EnerGuide for Houses programme. They filtered out post-retrofit records to retain only initial pre-retrofit evaluations (129,389 initial "A" audits), extracted 112,066 single-detached records, and applied a multi-parameter stratification algorithm matching Statistics Canada SHEU-03 control marginal distributions to construct the 16,952-record CSDDRD (14,030 single-detached and 2,922 double/row houses).
  In Swan (2010, Table 3.4, p. 79), the authors published full empirical stock averages across 5 vintages (pre-1946, 1946-1969, 1970-1979, 1980-1989, 1990-2003) and 5 regions (Ontario, Quebec, British Columbia, Prairies, Atlantic) for:
  - Above-grade living wall effective thermal resistance (1.6 to 2.9 m2K/W)
  - Ceiling / attic effective thermal resistance (3.8 to 5.6 m2K/W)
  - Basement wall thermal resistance (1.4 to 2.5 m2K/W)
  - Blower-door depressurization airtightness (4.4 to 10.2 ACH50)
  - Foundation distributions (basements present in 90% to 95% of single-detached stock)
  - Window glazing type distributions (74.0% double clear air, 7.7% single clear, 6.4% double Low-E argon, 5.7% double clear thin air, with center-of-glass and assembly thermal/optical ratings).

  Furthermore, this methodology was subsequently institutionalized by Natural Resources Canada / CanmetENERGY-Ottawa (CE-O) under Rasoul Asaee and Alex Ferguson (2020) to create the open-source "Statistically Representative Existing Canadian Low-Rise Housing Archetypes, 2019" library (https://github.com/canmet-energy/housing-archetypes), which provides 5,023 single-detached archetypes with measured pre-retrofit envelope properties derived directly from the federal EnerGuide evaluation database and SHEU-2015.

## 4. What You Could Not Find

1. I could not find measured slab-on-grade edge and under-slab insulation RSI values for existing single-detached homes because basements account for 89% to 95% of Canadian single-detached foundations, making slab-on-grade homes an extreme minority (<5% in most provinces).
2. I could not find direct on-site spectrophotometric field measurements of existing window SHGC; the reported stock SHGC values are simulated center-of-glass or assembly optical ratings from LBNL Window 5.2 applied to audited glazing layer and coating configurations.
3. I could not find public access to the raw, unamalgamated house-by-house microdata files of the pre-2004 EnerGuide for Houses audits due to Canadian federal privacy restrictions protecting homeowner personal information.
4. I could not find an official Statistics Canada SHEU table reporting measured blower-door ACH50 values, because SHEU is an occupant questionnaire survey and does not perform physical fan depressurization testing.
