# RT48. UK Data Service Licence Assessment: Derived Outputs and Model Training

## Section A. Direct answer

The UK Data Service End User Licence (EUL Version 16.00) and published guidance do not explicitly define trained neural network models, model weights, or simulation outputs generated by models trained on EUL microdata. While Clause 4 explicitly prohibits sharing any derived datasets, synthetic datasets included, with non-registered users, Clause 8 and the companion Security Guide (CD171 Version 18.00) permit publishing non-disclosive aggregate statistical outputs such as R-square statistics and district totals. The licence text does not settle whether fine-tuned language model weights or synthetic diary schedules produced under leave-one-country-out training constitute prohibited derived datasets or permissible non-disclosive research outputs. Regarding artificial intelligence, Clause 5 prohibits uploading EUL data to third-party web or cloud services without prior written permission, but CD171 Section 4.5.4 explicitly exempts tools running entirely locally offline. Because the legal boundary between a derived synthetic dataset and an unconstrained model output is not resolved in published licence text, only the UK Data Service can formally settle whether these specific generator weights and diary schedules may be shared. However, publishing the paper and its aggregate EnergyPlus surrogate results (pass counts, R2, district totals) without raw UK diary rows is fully compliant under established statistical disclosure control rules.

## Section E. What this changes in our planning

### Analysis of the two legal readings

#### Reading A: Outputs of a model trained on UK diaries are not Data or Derived Data under the EUL

* Consequence 1: Aggregate simulation results and surrogate model predictions (pass counts, R2 scores, hourly district totals) are classified as non-disclosive research findings under Clause 8 rather than restricted datasets. Under CD171 Section 5.2.1, these outputs can be published freely in a journal article because they contain no respondent identifiers, no cell counts below 3, no unit records, and no individual residuals.
* Consequence 2: The neural surrogate model weights (trained on simulated EnergyPlus heating, cooling, and electricity sequences) can be shared in an open code repository (such as Zenodo or GitHub) because they parameterize building energy physics, not human diary records.
* Consequence 3: Citation and notification obligations under Clause 11 and Clause 12 must still be fulfilled in full: the paper must cite SN 8128 (Sullivan and Gershuny, 2023), include CTUR and UKDS acknowledgements, and provide bibliographic details to the UKDS Helpdesk upon publication.
* Consequence 4: Under Clause 18 and CD171 Section 7, all copies of the downloaded UK microdata files (SN 8128) must be securely deleted from the computing cluster at the conclusion of the research project, while analytical code scripts and simulation pipelines are retained.

#### Reading B: The fine-tuned diary generator and its synthetic outputs are Derived Data under the EUL

* Consequence 1: Under Clause 4 ("Not to give access to the Data Collection(s)... or to any Dataset(s) derived from the Data Collection(s) (including synthetic Dataset(s)), except to Registered Users"), the intermediate synthetic diary days generated for Spanish and Italian households cannot be distributed in an open repository alongside the paper without prior written permission from UKDS.
* Consequence 2: If the fine-tuned language model weights of the diary generator are classified as a derived dataset (embodying learned conditional representations of UK diary microdata), sharing those model weights publicly would violate Clause 4 and Clause 9.
* Consequence 3: The paper itself can still be published, because published academic figures, district sums, and aggregate performance tables represent statistical outputs governed by Clause 8, not dataset redistribution under Clause 4.
* Consequence 4: The data sharing statement of the paper must state that the underlying UK diary data are available to registered users from the UK Data Service under study SN 8128, and that synthetic intermediate diary files can only be shared with verified Registered Users under EUL terms.

## Section G. Contradictions, gaps, open questions, and your own negative controls

### Disagreements, ambiguities, and literature gaps

* Contractual ambiguity around "Derived Data": While Clause 4 explicitly restricts "any Dataset(s) derived from the Data Collection(s) (including synthetic Dataset(s))", Section 4 of the EUL does not contain a legal definition for "Derived Data" or "synthetic data". The licence defines "Dataset" as a computer file organized under a single title, but does not specify whether trained mathematical weights of a neural network constitute a dataset.
* Distinction between synthetic data and model outputs: Published guidance on the UKDS Learning Hub treats synthetic datasets as data collections that mirror microdata structures, whereas statistical disclosure control guidance in CD171 Section 5.2.1 treats model parameters (such as regression coefficients and R2 values) as publishable outputs. A learned language model represents an intermediate artifact between a statistical summary and a generative dataset, a hybrid category unaddressed in current UKDS text.
* Precedents of open distribution vs. strict licence text: In published precedents (CREST, Richardson et al. 2008; Synthetic Population Catalyst, Salat et al. 2023), authors constructed models or synthetic generators from UK time-use survey data and released the resulting software under open licenses (GPL-3.0 and MIT) on institutional repositories and GitHub, while acknowledging the UK Data Archive. In contrast, the SIPHER consortium archived its synthetic population directly within the UK Data Service under a Safeguarded EUL (SN 9277). This reveals a dual practice in the research community: software and abstract generator tools are distributed openly, while microdata-like synthetic individual tables are deposited under safeguarded licences.
