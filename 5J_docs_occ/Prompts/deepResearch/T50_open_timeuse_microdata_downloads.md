# T50. Open time-use diary microdata in Europe that can be downloaded today without an application

Paste `00_MASTER_BRIEF_5J.md` first, then `_RESPONSE_TEMPLATE.md`, then this prompt, in one fresh session. **Run it alone.**
Written 2026-10-01 by the 5J manager at the author's request ("check available open HETUS datasets in order to expand our
datasets from Italy, Spain, UK to others"). T49 gives the overall country list and access routes; T50 goes deeper on one
question only: **which diary files are truly open (download without an application), proven by a working download page.**
Your files are `RT50_open_timeuse_microdata_downloads.md` and `RT50_pages.log` (one line per page or file you opened: URL,
what it was, full / partial / title only, date). Save both in this folder.

## Why we are asking

Spain's 2009-2010 time-use survey is an open download from INE under CC BY 4.0, and Italy's 2013-2014 survey has a public-use
file from ISTAT. Both were obtained in days. The study rule is that each country's households come from that country's own
diaries only, so every new country needs its own diary file. An open file can join the study within the current GPU window
(about four weeks); a file behind an application usually cannot.

The researcher: postdoctoral, Concordia University, Montreal, Canada (outside the EU), non-commercial, sole author.

## What we need

For every European country (EU and non-EU: also Norway, Switzerland, Serbia, Turkey, the UK and others), any national
time-use survey since 2000, HETUS-harmonised or not:

1. Is a **microdata file with the diary episodes** downloadable without an application (open data portal, public-use file,
   click-through licence)? Give the direct URL of the download page or file, and say whether you reached the download
   control (`REACHED`) or only a description (`DESCRIBED`).
2. Its licence (name and URL) and, quoted, any clause on derived data, synthetic data, publication of results, and use of
   online or AI tools with the data. `NOT STATED` is a good answer.
3. What it holds, each yes / no / not stated with the documentation page: diary time slots (10 or 15 minutes), main and
   secondary activity, location at home, all household members diarised or household composition linked, diary day type
   (weekday or weekend, month or season), region, weights.
4. File format and size if shown (SPSS, Stata, CSV, other).

Positive controls (we hold both; your rows must agree or quote why not): Spain INE 2009-2010 (open, CC BY 4.0) and Italy ISTAT
2013-2014 public-use file.

## Hard constraints specific to this prompt

* **Negative control:** look up "Statistics Iceland Time Use Survey 2010, open microdata file TUS2010_PUF.csv". It must come
  back `NOT FOUND` unless you open a real download page for exactly that file. If you describe it without a page, the round fails.
* A catalogue search page, a press release or a table of aggregates is not a microdata file; the row then reads
  `NO OPEN MICRODATA`.
* Do not download, open or describe the content of any data file beyond its documentation. Nothing here needs microdata.
* Open every page you cite and log it. No em dashes and no en dashes anywhere.

## Deliverable

**Section A** (at most six sentences): how many countries have open diary microdata reached at a download control, and which
of them meet all the study's needs. **Section B**: one row per citable statement. **Sections C and D**: `not applicable to this
prompt`. **Section E**: the countries that could join now, in order of how completely they meet the needs (not by scientific
interest). **Section F**: one row per country and survey: country, survey and year, `REACHED` / `DESCRIBED` /
`NO OPEN MICRODATA`, URL, licence and quoted clauses, the needs (yes / no / not stated), format. **Section G**: controls, pages you
could not open, contradictions, the four standard questions. **Section H**: full references.
