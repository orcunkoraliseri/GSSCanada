# RT42. Has anyone already done it: a language model reading building records for retrofit and cooling state, with abstention, feeding an energy model

## Section A. Direct answer

The four-part combination of reading municipal building records for retrofit and cooling state with a language model, emitting prediction sets or abstaining under thin evidence, handling French or bilingual text, and propagating that uncertainty into an urban building energy model with time-use occupancy is completely open and unclaimed in the literature [INFERENCE].
The closest prior work on reading building permit text is by Zhang, Hong, and Luo (2020), who applied BERT to multi-label classification of San Francisco building permits into four broad categories: mechanical, electrical, plumbing, and building [L6, L7, L173].
However, that study did not resolve equipment or envelope retrofit states (such as heat pumps, air conditioning, insulation, windows, or heating fuel), had no abstention or conformal prediction sets, operated exclusively in English, and did not feed an energy model or EnergyPlus simulation [L7, L173, INFERENCE].
The closest Canadian study on permit text mining is by Gunay, Wills, Knudsen, and Macdonald (2023), who used association rule mining across seven Canadian cities to extract physical building form and foundation attributes, but explicitly omitted equipment, HVAC, retrofit states, conformal prediction, and language models [L2, L4, L176].
Conformal prediction in building performance simulation is documented exclusively for numerical load forecasting, notably by Borrotti (2024), with zero applications to building record classification or textual abstention [L47, L48, L171, INFERENCE].
No study in the literature has applied neural language models to French or bilingual Canadian municipal permit records for retrofit or cooling extraction [L30, L31, L34, INFERENCE].
Furthermore, no study has propagated permit-derived retrofit uncertainty into building stock energy simulations driven by time-use occupant schedules [L38, L39, L40, INFERENCE].
Therefore, candidate angle A7 in its climate form is completely unclaimed across all four components and their full union [BRIEF s.10, INFERENCE].

## Section D. Gap and fit assessment

Item 2 analysis for each part and their full combination:

* **Part P1 (Language model reading building records for equipment or retrofit state):** The nearest prior study is Zhang, Hong, and Luo (2020), who trained BERT on San Francisco building permits [L6, L7]. That work did not extract specific heating, cooling, or envelope equipment states such as heat pumps, air conditioning, insulation R-values, window types, or heating fuel switches, but instead predicted four high-level administrative categories: mechanical, electrical, plumbing, and general building alterations [L7, L173, INFERENCE]. Gunay et al. (2023) analyzed Canadian housing permits across seven cities, but explicitly restricted extraction to architectural geometry and foundation types (basement, slab-on-grade, crawlspace, storeys, and garage) using association rules, omitting equipment and HVAC systems entirely [L2, L4, L176, INFERENCE]. P1 is unclaimed for granular retrofit and cooling state extraction [INFERENCE].

* **Part P2 (Prediction sets or abstention under thin text evidence):** The nearest prior study in building performance simulation is Borrotti (2024), who introduced conformal prediction for building energy simulation [L47, L48]. However, Borrotti applied conformal prediction solely to regression intervals of simulated heating and cooling loads, completely outside text processing or municipal records [L48, L171, INFERENCE]. No study in building energy modeling, building stock analysis, or urban building records has implemented selective classification, reject options, or conformal prediction sets that allow a model to abstain when permit text lacks sufficient diagnostic evidence [L23, L24, L25, L27, L28, INFERENCE]. P2 is completely unclaimed [INFERENCE].

* **Part P3 (French or bilingual French and English permit text):** The nearest prior study is Gunay et al. (2023), which included the City of Montreal alongside six English-majority Canadian municipalities [L2, L4]. However, Gunay et al. handled French text purely through hardcoded keyword substitutions (for example, mapping 'sous-sol' to basement and 'étage' to storey) within rule mining, rather than applying a natural language processing model or multilingual neural classifier [L4, L176, INFERENCE]. Dedicated queries for French construction permits ('permis de construction', 'permis de construire') coupled with NLP, text classification, or machine learning yielded administrative and legal commentaries, but zero automated retrofit extraction studies [L30, L31, L32, L33, L121, L128, INFERENCE]. P3 is completely unclaimed [INFERENCE].

* **Part P4 (Inferred states with uncertainty feeding UBEM with occupant schedules):** The nearest prior studies are Gunay et al. (2023), which weighted National Building Code archetypes by permit frequency in an aggregate stock analysis, and Geske and Voelker (2025), which simulated archetype refurbishment uncertainty in UBEM using statistical sampling [L4, L138, L176]. However, neither study fed machine-learning-inferred individual building states into an EnergyPlus or UBEM workflow, and neither propagated textual prediction set uncertainty into thermal load or overheating outcomes [L4, L138, INFERENCE]. Furthermore, no prior study in the literature couples permit-derived building envelope and cooling states with empirical time-use occupant schedules (such as Statistics Canada GSS or HETUS) to evaluate occupant-centric demand or indoor heat exposure [L38, L39, L40, BRIEF s.10, INFERENCE]. P4 is completely unclaimed [INFERENCE].

* **Full Combination (P1 + P2 + P3 + P4):** The full combination of an open-weight language model extracting retrofit and cooling states from bilingual Canadian municipal permit text, equipped with conformal prediction sets or abstention for sparse records, feeding an urban building energy model with time-use occupant schedules, is entirely unclaimed in the scientific literature [INFERENCE]. No existing pipeline connects municipal administrative free text to physics-based building stock simulation with mathematically grounded abstention bounds [INFERENCE].

## Section E. What this changes in our planning

* **Focus model design on fine-grained equipment classification rather than generic work types (tied to Section B Row 2 and Section C Row 1):** Because Zhang et al. (2020) already established that BERT can classify broad categories like mechanical or electrical work, the contribution of 5J cannot merely replicate administrative labeling [L7, L173]. The model must explicitly target granular climate-relevant states: heat pump presence, air conditioning addition, insulation upgrades, window replacements, and heating fuel switching [BRIEF s.10, INFERENCE].
* **Establish conformal abstention as the core methodological contribution (tied to Section B Row 5 and Section C Row 3):** Since Borrotti (2024) demonstrated conformal prediction only for continuous thermal load forecasting, applying conformal prediction with a reject option to sparse, noisy municipal text is a first in the building engineering literature [L48, L171, INFERENCE]. The pipeline should output prediction sets that guarantee marginal coverage per archetype and abstain when permit descriptions are uninformative [BRIEF s.10, INFERENCE].
* **Capitalize on bilingual Montreal data as a unique geographical and technical asset (tied to Section B Row 4 and Section C Row 2):** Gunay et al. (2023) identified that Montreal archetypes exhibited significant mismatch and required ad-hoc French keyword matching [L4, L176]. Operating directly on Montreal French and Toronto English permit text establishes a genuine bilingual capability that no prior building energy study possesses [L34, L121, INFERENCE].
* **Close the loop from permit text to occupied UBEM simulation (tied to Section B Row 6 and Row 7):** Prior works stopped at permit mining statistics (Zhang 2020) or code archetype distribution weights (Gunay 2023) [L4, L7]. 5J should propagate the conformal prediction sets directly into OpenUBEM with GSS time-use occupant schedules, quantifying the indoor temperature and peak demand spread induced by unobserved retrofit states [BRIEF s.3, BRIEF s.10, INFERENCE].
