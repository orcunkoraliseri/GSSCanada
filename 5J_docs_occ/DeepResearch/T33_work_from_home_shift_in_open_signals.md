# T33. The work-from-home shift as recorded by open data other than time-use surveys

Paste `00_MASTER_BRIEF.md` first (read its section 9). Answer with the schema in `_RESPONSE_TEMPLATE.md`.
All sections used. Run in wave 6, after `T19`. Read with `T24` and `T28`.

## Why we are asking

Our eSim and 2J work measured, from four Canadian time-use cycles, that the share of time at home rose
between 2005 and 2022, with a jump after 2020 (brief section 2 gives the eSim figures; use no others).
A time-use survey sees that shift at two points only: before and after. Other open signals saw it
continuously: phone mobility, smart meters and thermostats, labour-force telework questions, transit
ridership, office attendance indices. If those signals agree with the diaries on size and timing,
they can extend the 2022 snapshot forward year by year (`R4`); if they disagree, which to trust is a
real question. We need the signals, their trajectories to the latest date available, and any study
that compared them.

## What we need

### Item 1. The signals, Canada first

For Canada, and then for Spain, Italy and the UK, one data-source card (brief section 9) per open
signal of time at home or telework from 2019 to the latest date: Labour Force Survey telework
estimates, Google residential mobility (until it stopped), smart-meter or utility reports of daytime
residential load change, thermostat-based occupancy reports, transit ridership recovery, office
attendance indices. State the last date each covers.

### Item 2. Trajectories

For each signal, what the source itself reports about the shift: size at the 2020 to 2021 peak, size
at the latest date, and whether it has reverted, plateaued or kept rising. Quote the source. No
numbers without a source row.

### Item 3. Agreement between signals

Studies that compared two or more of these signals, or compared any of them with a time-use survey,
on the size of the shift. Section C rows.

### Item 4. Use in building energy

Works that used any of these signals to build post-2020 occupancy scenarios or to correct pre-2020
schedules. Section C rows.

## Named leads

Statistics Canada LFS releases and The Daily; Eurostat LFS; ONS opinions and lifestyle survey;
utility and system operator reports (Hydro-Québec, IESO); WFH Research (SWAA) for the US as a
methodological comparator; *Energy and Buildings*, *Applied Energy*, *Energy Research and Social
Science*, *Nature Energy*, *Joule*.

* Do not reuse or estimate our own at-home figures beyond brief section 2.
* Separate "telework share of workers" from "time at home of all residents"; they are different

* No em dashes and no en dashes anywhere in the output.
