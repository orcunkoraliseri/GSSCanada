### The Canadian versus European evaluation conflict
- The Canadian Perspective (NSERC PDF): Evaluators penalize doctoral continuity. NSERC explicitly mandates that the proposed postdoctoral research must be significantly distinct from the Ph.D. thesis. If the thesis was on generative occupancy modeling from time-use data, proposing A3 (generative occupancy models) will result in immediate rejection under the distinctness criterion. In contrast, A9 (passive survivability during grid outages) and A7 (conformal prediction bounds on records) represent clean, defensible pivots.
- The European Perspective (MSCA PF and Digital Futures): European panels reward established track record and institutional continuity. Proposing an abrupt pivot away from building energy modeling into unproven natural language processing risks low scores under the Feasibility and Excellence criteria. European panels prefer A2 (coupling generative models to UBEM for district climate adaptation), where prior published software and datasets directly de-risk two-year execution.

## Section E. Recent award patterns and programme conflicts

### 1. Award distribution in recent competitions (2023 to 2026)
- Digital Futures Postdoctoral Cohorts: Awarded projects in the built environment overwhelmingly emphasize cyber-physical systems, graph-based digital twins, sensor fusion, and machine learning with formal uncertainty bounds. Projects consistently combine domain engineering (architecture/civil) with theoretical computer science (graph algorithms, conformal inference).
- Horizon Europe MSCA Fellowships: Funded projects in energy and climate (e.g., CORDIS energy cohorts) prioritize open-source software, FAIR data pipelines, and clear socio-environmental impacts (decarbonization, heat vulnerability mitigation). Pure machine learning without domain thermodynamic grounding is rarely funded; projects must show measurable physical validation.
- NSERC PDF: Awardees in civil and building engineering are heavily concentrated in physical resilience, extreme weather adaptation, structural durability, and energy security under grid disruption. Theoretical algorithm development without physical infrastructure relevance receives low ratings.

### 2. Strategic programme conflicts
- The Climate-Resilience versus AI-Methodology Conflict: A passive survivability paper (A9) strongly reinforces applications to UC Berkeley (Climate Futures cohort) and NSERC (Canadian infrastructure resilience), but provides zero evidence for the Schmidt AI in Science fellowship at Toronto, which mandates cutting-edge AI methodology.
- The NLP-Agent versus Physics-Simulation Conflict: An LLM-records and conformal prediction paper (A7) perfectly positions the candidate for Digital Futures (KTH) and Schmidt AI in Science (Toronto), but risks alienating building engineering reviewers who demand validated thermodynamic simulations.
- Strategic Resolution: To serve both sides, paper 5J should be selected as Angle A7 if the researcher prioritizes the early October 2026 AI fellowship deadlines (Schmidt AI Oct 5, Digital Futures Oct 16), or Angle A2 / A9 if the researcher prioritizes the November Berkeley Climate Futures and Canadian NSERC submissions.
