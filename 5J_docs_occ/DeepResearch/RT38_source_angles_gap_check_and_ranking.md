# RT38: Gap Check and Ranking of the Data-Source Forms of Angle A14

## Section A. Direct answer

Across the twelve data-source forms of Angle A14, the highest-ranking viable form is Form 4: Open low-voltage feeder or substation load as a check on occupancy-driven residential load (RT23 round 2, aggregate score 21 / 25), followed closely by Form 3: Activity-based travel-model populations as the occupancy engine of an urban building energy model (RT27 round 2, aggregate score 19 / 25). Conversely, Form 1 (smart-thermostat presence from ecobee Donate Your Data) and Form 2 (measured bias of time-use diaries on presence at home) rank lower at 14 / 25 and 15 / 25 respectively, because their data redistribution licences remain unread (scoring 0 on criterion c) and their bare Canadian presence comparison is already published by Doma, Prajapati, and Ouf (2024) in Building and Environment. Forms 5 through 12 originate from failed vetting rounds, carry unread licences, and are strictly constrained by the prompt rule to rank below the four forms with vetted support. If licensing compliance (criterion c) is enforced as a hard pass or fail gate, the ranking changes fundamentally: only Form 4 and Form 3 pass, while all other ten candidate forms fail because their licence terms are unnamed or unread. In the flattering-direction check, the smart-thermostat form (Form 1), which was described with the greatest enthusiasm in earlier planning, ranks last among vetted forms and fails the licence gate, confirming that this evaluation is governed by verified licensing and empirical gap analysis rather than institutional warmth.

## Section D. Gap and fit assessment: Detailed scoring and strongest objections (Items 2 and 4)

### The Stated Ranking Rule

Before scoring, the rule is stated without exception:
1. Every candidate form is evaluated on five criteria scored from 0 to 5:
   - **(a) Openness**: 5 for unrestricted public download, down to 0 for proprietary or completely closed data.
   - **(b) 3-month access**: 5 for immediate download, down to 0 for access exceeding three months or inaccessible to a Canadian university.
   - **(c) Licence publishability**: Scored strictly per the prompt instruction: *Score (c) only from a licence named in the vetted table (CC BY 4.0, CC BY-NC 4.0, GPL-3.0). Where no licence is named, score (c) as `licence unread` and give it 0, and say so beside the score.*
   - **(d) Method spine fit**: Alignment with Brief Section 2 (occupancy generator, pre-registered gates, paired EnergyPlus campaign).
   - **(e) Fellowship fit**: Alignment with Brief Section 5 fellowship themes (climate resilience, passive survivability, AI in science, digital transformation).
2. Scores are summed with equal weights (maximum 25 points).
3. **Hard constraint on failed rounds**: Under the prompt's hard constraint, a form whose only support is a failed-round row cannot rank above a form with vetted support. Forms 1 to 4 (vetted support) therefore take ranks 1 to 4; Forms 5 to 12 (route only, failed rounds) take ranks 5 to 12 regardless of nominal points.
4. **Hard pass/fail gate on criterion (c)**: We then report whether the ranking order changes if criterion (c) is treated as a non-negotiable pass/fail filter.

### Impact of Licence Compliance (Criterion c) as a Hard Pass / Fail Gate

If criterion (c) is treated as a strict, non-negotiable pass/fail gate:
* **PASS**: **Form 4** (CC BY 4.0 / CC BY-NC 4.0 confirmed) and **Form 3** (GPL-3.0 confirmed).
* **FAIL**: **Forms 1, 2, and 5 to 12**. All ten forms receive an immediate FAIL because their dataset licence terms are either explicitly unread (Forms 1 and 2) or unnamed in the vetted table (Forms 5 to 12).
* **Consequence on Ranking Order**: The relative order between the top two viable forms does not change (Form 4 remains Rank 1, Form 3 remains Rank 2). However, all other candidate forms are completely eliminated from consideration until their legal end-user agreements are formally retrieved, read, and verified for derivative redistribution rights.

## Section G. Contradictions, gaps, open questions, and negative controls

### Flattering-direction check

* **The Flattering Direction**: In the original research brief (Brief Section 9) and earlier candidate rankings, smart-thermostat presence data (`Form 1` / `T20`) and measured diary bias (`Form 2` / `T32`) were viewed with the greatest warmth and enthusiasm. They were anticipated to rank first because ecobee is based in Toronto, Concordia holds ecobee data, and smart thermostats provide massive real-world home presence records.
* **The Reality**: Form 1 ranks **last among vetted forms** (4th of 4, total 14 / 25), and Form 2 ranks 3rd (15 / 25). Furthermore, if licensing compliance is a hard pass/fail gate, **both Form 1 and Form 2 fail completely**.
* **Evidence Other Than Warmth Driving the Result**:
  1. *Published Prior Art*: Cold review of published literature shows that Doma, Prajapati, and Ouf (2024) at Concordia University have already published the bare Canadian ecobee presence generator and GSS time-use comparison in *Building and Environment*, and Jung et al. (2023) published the US counterpart. The direct validation question is already taken.
  2. *Licensing Barrier*: The ecobee Donate Your Data research agreement has not been read to confirm whether derived synthetic schedules can be published or redistributed (`licence unread`, scoring 0 on criterion c).
  3. *Unflattered Winners*: Form 4 (21 / 25) and Form 3 (19 / 25) rank at the top because they rely on verified, open licences named in the vetted table (CC BY 4.0, CC BY-NC 4.0, GPL-3.0) and confirmed accessible data (Hydro-Quebec LCPR download and TMG GitHub repository).

### Contradictions and Gaps

* **Traffic Sensors versus Travel Surveys**: A significant contradiction in earlier reports was citing Berres et al. (2021) as evidence that household travel surveys have been used for building occupancy. Checking the CrossRef record shows that Berres et al. generated occupancy schedules from a grid of traffic sensors in Chattanooga, Tennessee, not from a household travel survey. Travel surveys remain entirely uncoupled to UBEM in published literature.
* **Travel Models In-Home Blindness**: Activity-based travel models provide network routing and arrival/departure times, but collapse all residential time into `"home"`. They cannot serve as stand-alone building occupancy engines without being coupled to a stochastic time-use activity generator (such as GSS).
