# RT05: Climate Change, Heat and UBEM: Future Weather Files, Overheating at Stock Scale, and Occupancy-Heat Coupling

## Section A. Direct answer

No published UBEM-scale overheating study couples dynamic, heat-responsive occupant presence or behaviour (such as heat-driven evacuation, reduced indoor metabolic activity, or distress mobility) to building thermal exposure simulations; all stock models rely on static, deterministic occupancy schedules (such as CIBSE Guide A, ASHRAE 90.1, or DIN V 18599) or weather-blind stochastic Markov chains. Fully redistributable, open-licence future hourly weather files (EPW) exist for Montreal and Toronto via the Pacific Climate Impacts Consortium (PCIC) and National Research Council Canada (NRC) under the Open Government Licence - Canada, and for our four European districts via the Copernicus Climate Data Store (C3S) under the Licence to Use Copernicus Products (both permissive and CC BY 4.0 compatible), whereas commercial tools (WeatherShift, Meteonorm) and legacy academic tools (CCWorldWeatherGen) impose closed, paid, or non-redistributable terms. Across urban stock overheating literature, indoor temperatures are evaluated using static degree-hours, CIBSE TM59/TM52 exceedance, or Indoor Overheating Degree (IOD), but validation against measured indoor temperatures is virtually nonexistent at district scale: no study has ever validated simulated hourly indoor temperatures dwelling-by-dwelling across more than a handful of uncalibrated archetypes (the largest empirical comparison matched statistical distributions across 122 London homes without individual dwelling calibration). City heat-vulnerability indices completely omit time-at-home and simulated indoor heat exposure, relying instead on census demographics and satellite land-surface temperature proxies. To enable candidate angles A2 and A4, our simulation engine requires three modular additions: an ingest pipeline for PCIC/C3S future EPWs or on-the-fly Belcher morphing via the open-source MIT-licensed `pyepwmorph` library (v2.0.0); an indoor overheating metric post-processor implementing CIBSE TM59 and IOD; and a heat-responsive presence/window-opening rule adapted from IEA EBC Annex 79 and Annex 95 empirical curves.

## Section E. What this changes in our planning

* **Drop the claim of dwelling-by-dwelling indoor temperature validation (tied to B10, C10):** We must not promise or claim that our engine predicts exact indoor temperatures for individual real-world dwellings. Instead, frame our output explicitly as an uncalibrated archetype-based comparative exposure index, and conduct validation against empirical distribution bounds (such as the UK 4M / Pathan et al. datasets or Canadian MURB survey ranges).
* **Adopt PCIC and C3S future weather files immediately (tied to B2, B3, B5, B6):** Eliminate reliance on proprietary or restrictive commercial datasets (Meteonorm, WeatherShift). Use PCIC's OGL-Canada v3 CWEC2020 future files for Montreal and Toronto, and C3S open-access datasets for European districts. Both are fully redistributable with paper code.
* **Integrate `pyepwmorph` for on-the-fly future weather morphing (tied to B8, B9, C1):** Rather than hard-coding static EPW downloads, integrate `pyepwmorph` (v2.0.0, MIT License) into the preprocessing pipeline to enable parametric morphing under CMIP6 SSP1-2.6, SSP2-4.5, and SSP5-8.5 warming levels.
* **Implement standard overheating post-processing metrics (tied to C4, C7):** Add modular metric evaluators in Python for:
  1. CIBSE TM59 Criterion 1 (hours above 26C exceeding 3% of occupied summer hours) and Criterion 2 (hours above 26C exceeding 1% of sleeping hours 22:00-07:00).
  2. Indoor Overheating Degree (IOD, Hamdy et al. 2017) to measure cumulative degree-hours above adaptive thresholds.
  3. Passive Survivability hours (hours below health-critical thresholds, e.g. 30C operative temperature, during complete power outages).
* **Design the heat-responsive occupancy module as the core novelty (tied to B1, B11, C6):** The defensible contribution of A2/A4 is replacing the universal, weather-blind static occupancy assumption with an empirically grounded, heat-responsive model:
  - Higher daytime at-home presence for vulnerable populations (elderly, low-income) drawn from time-use surveys (Vellei et al. 2017; GSS Time Use).
  - Thermal-threshold window opening (opening when outdoor temperature is lower than indoor temperature; closing when outdoor air exceeds indoor air to prevent convective heat gain).
  - Distress relocation / cooling shelter visits for uncooled homes exceeding 32C operative temperature.

## Section G. Contradictions, gaps, open questions, and your own negative controls

* **Contradiction on Future Weather Integration in Canadian Building Codes:** Technical literature from 2020-2022 stated that Canadian model codes ignore climate change and rely exclusively on stationary historical weather data. However, forward-looking climatic design data developed under the NRC CRBCPI initiative are now operationalized via ClimateData.ca for building design, making future weather modeling policy-relevant today.
* **Licensing Contradiction across Future Weather Tools:** Earlier academic workflows relied on CCWorldWeatherGen or WeatherShift. Both are non-viable for an open-science benchmark: CCWorldWeatherGen relies on obsolete IPCC SRES A2 HadCM3 data with closed VBA distribution terms, and WeatherShift is a commercial paid service. In contrast, PCIC and C3S provide completely free, redistributable EPW and gridded data under OGL-Canada and Copernicus open data terms, while `pyepwmorph` offers an open-source MIT implementation.
* **Negative Control on Stock-Scale Indoor Overheating Validation (Item 2.3):** We searched specifically for any peer-reviewed paper that validated simulated indoor operative temperatures dwelling-by-dwelling against measured sensor data across more than a handful of dwellings in a district or urban stock model. Finding: `NOT FOUND`. Every published UBEM study that simulates indoor overheating across an urban stock either:
  1. Compares simulated archetype distributions against uncoupled survey or temperature statistics from independent monitoring campaigns (such as the UK 4M project of 122 homes in London/Leicester, or the English Housing Survey), where dwelling-by-dwelling error is admitted to be large due to unmeasured occupant window and blind operation; or
  2. Validates energy consumption (gas/electricity) against utility bills and presents indoor temperatures as an unvalidated physical simulation output.
* **Negative Control on Heat-Responsive Occupancy Presence (Item 3.2):** We searched for any building energy or thermal exposure model where occupant presence during heatwaves is dynamically altered based on time-use surveys, distress mobility, or heat alert triggers. Finding: `NOT FOUND`. All existing stock models maintain static weekday/weekend diversity schedules regardless of whether outdoor air is 20C or 40C.
