# RT01: Field Map of Hot Topics, Saturation, and Funding Signals (2024-2026)

## Section A. Direct answer

We identified ten distinct topic clusters across the four target domains, of which six exhibit measurable, accelerating momentum between 2024 and 2026, two are plateauing into mature application niches, and two remain highly nascent. Across the four foundational fields (large language models, occupant behaviour modelling, urban building energy modelling, and climate change and heat), the single most crowded and saturated area is conventional occupant behaviour prediction using standard machine learning and basic black-box deep learning for single-building presence and window-opening classification. Conversely, the sharpest unaddressed gap sits at the intersection of open-weight generative sequence models, multi-country time-use diaries, and dwelling-resolved urban building energy simulation under extreme climate stress.

## Section E. What this changes in our planning

1. **Abandon ungrounded "Agentic UBEM" as a primary contribution (tied to Rows C1, L01, L02):** The literature on LLMs for building simulation has exploded from 0 papers in 2023 to 30 in 2026. However, existing work is split between high-level roadmaps (Ma et al., 2026) and single-building prompt-to-IDF translators (Jiang et al., 2024). Attempting an open-weight agent to script OpenUBEM end-to-end (Angle A1) will face fierce skepticism unless grounded in a strict, pre-registered verification bar that catches agent hallucinations in physical geometry.
2. **Double down on time-use grounded passive survivability and overheating (tied to Rows C5, C6, L13, L16, L17):** The passive survivability literature is surging (counts up from 4 in 2024 to 34 in 2026), yet virtually every published paper (Wijesuriya et al., 2024; Bayomi et al., 2021) makes the simplistic assumption of 100% constant occupancy. Introducing demographically resolved, time-use derived presence to determine who is inside during compound heat-wave blackouts addresses the primary flaw named by the field's own authors.
3. **Recognize that occupant-centric demand flexibility is saturated (tied to Rows C7, L19):** With Annex 79 concluded and publication counts plateauing at roughly 10 papers per year, standard demand flexibility papers (Angle A5) offer minimal novelty unless coupled with empirical compliance boundaries.
4. **Position language model record extraction as an uncertainty and conformal coverage problem (tied to Rows C10, L24):** Schmid et al. (2025) have already proven that LLMs can extract material attributes from 20,000 EPC records at ETH Zurich. To claim genuine novelty, Angle A7 must move beyond extraction to formal abstention, prediction sets, and conformal coverage on a UBEM engine.
5. **Align proposals directly with IEA EBC Annex 95 themes (tied to Table B3):** Annex 95 (Human-centric Building Design and Operation for a Changing Climate, 2024-2029) is the exact international umbrella covering our work. Proposals for Canadian (NSERC), Swiss (MSCA/ETH), and European grants must cite Annex 95 Subtask 1 (Individual behavior under climate extremes) and Subtask 2 (Community equity).

## Section G. Contradictions, gaps, open questions, and negative controls

### 1. Saturation warnings (Item 5)
A reviewer in late 2026 will reject manuscripts proposing the following as closed or incremental:
* **Single-building occupancy presence prediction from IoT environmental sensors (CO2, temperature, humidity):** Closed by Annex 79 benchmarks and comprehensive reviews (Zhang et al., 2022; Dai et al., 2020). Novelty here is now purely incremental.
* **Basic Bayesian calibration of UBEM archetypes against monthly gas/electricity bills:** Closed by Sokol et al. (2017) and Chen et al. (2020). The equifinality problem is well documented; calibration without sub-metered validation is seen as mathematical curve fitting.
* **Pure LLM prompt engineering for EnergyPlus IDF generation:** Closed by EPlus-LLM (Jiang et al., 2024). Simply showing that an LLM can write an IDF syntax block is no longer a journal-level contribution.

### 2. Where our assets create asymmetry (Item 6)
* **Harmonised multi-country diaries (HETUS Spain, UK, Italy + Canadian GSS):** Highly rare. 95% of published occupant simulation studies use either local single-building sensor convenience samples or single-country surveys (US ATUS). Holding harmonized multi-country diaries allows empirical cross-national transfer experiments that virtually no other building performance group can run.
* **Fine-tuned open-weight generator with a documented negative result:** Unique. In a literature overwhelmed by positive LLM hype, a rigorously documented failure where an open-weight 7B model loses to a raked demographic donor pool (Null baseline) provides strong credibility for fellowship applications (especially Digital Futures and Schmidt AI in Science).
* **District-scale engine with dwelling-level division and no-core rule (OpenUBEM):** Rare. Most UBEM platforms (CityBES, UMI, TEASER) model entire multi-family buildings as lumped shoe-box zones or single multi-floor zones. True dwelling-by-dwelling division matching individual household demographic diaries is an asymmetry.
* **Pre-registered validation gates with perturbation tests:** Extremely rare in civil and building engineering. Almost no published UBEM literature uses pre-registered checksum gates frozen before scoring.

   * `A7` (LLM reading records) is partly taken for material stock by Schmid et al. (2025), but completely open regarding conformal abstention and operational UBEM parameters.
   * `A2` (Occupancy under heat) and `A9` (Passive survivability with occupants) are entirely unclaimed in their proposed coupling of demographic time-use presence with blackout physics.
