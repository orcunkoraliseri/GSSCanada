## 2. `FINDING 164` — the source assigns no temperature to any volume, and says so in as many words

Table 1 of the report, read directly with `pdftotext -layout`:

```
                cat A:       cat B:        cat C:   cat D:    Sum
                short load   medium load   bath     shower
Vdot in l/min   1            6             14       8
duration in min 1            1             10       5
inc/day         28           12            0.143    2
sigma           2            2             2
vol/load in l   1            6             140      40
vol/day in l    28           72            20       80        200
portion         0.14         0.36          0.10     0.40      1
```

🔴 **There is no temperature column.** Flow rate, duration, incidences, sigma, volume — nothing
else. And the abstract states the omission deliberately:

> *"For the cold water temperature distribution during the year, **a local profile should be
> used**."*

The only temperature anywhere in the report is a **35 K rise**, used twice, in worked
maximum-energy examples (`14 l/min * 10 min * 1.16 Wh/(kgK) * 35 K = 5680 Wh`, and the same shape
for the 6-minute profile). It is an example, not a basis for the volumes.

🔴 **Consequence: a 60 °C-equivalent volume cannot be recovered from this source, by us or by
anyone.** The brief's `(40−10)/(60−10)` conversion has nothing to operate on. It is refuted, not
merely unconfirmed.

⚪ **And if one grants `RL25`'s temperatures anyway** — A and B at 60 °C, C and D at 40 °C, 10 °C
inlet — the conversion is **×0.800**, not ×0.600: `(28+72)·50 + (20+80)·30` over `200·50`. That
takes `es` from 100.16 to **80.12**, still outside the band. **The temperature story cannot carry
the failure even when it is granted premises the source does not supply.**

## 3. `FINDING 165` — `G9.7`'s scored quantity is `200 / n_members`. It is a test of household size, not of the DHW model

The per-person column is arithmetically derived and carries no independent content:

| check | result |
|---|---|
| max abs difference between `dhw_litres_per_person_per_day` and `dhw_litres_per_day ÷ n_members`, over all 300 rows | **0.0005** — the rounding step, nothing more |
| the emitted volume's dependence on household size | **none, by ruling.** `--dhw-l-per-day` defaults to 200.0 and `D-S9-2` item 5 (a) holds it constant — *"that scaling is not in the report and would be ours"* |
| the source's own basis | **per dwelling.** *"one-family house => 73 000 litres (= 365 days \* 200 l/day)"*; *"five-family house => 365 000 litres (= 365 days \* 200 l/day \* 5)"* — five families, five times 200, not one house at 200 |

🔴 **So `G9.7` compares `200 / n` against `30–50`, and it is satisfiable for and only for
`n` between 4.00 and 6.67 — integer households of 4, 5 or 6 people.**

| fold | household-size histogram | structurally able to pass at the nominal 200 L/day | actually inside the band |
|---|---|---|---|
| `es` | 1:33 · 2:43 · 3:17 · 4:3 · 5:2 · 6:2 | **7 / 100** | 8 / 100 |
| `uk` | 1:39 · 2:47 · 3:10 · 4:3 · 5:1 | **4 / 100** | 6 / 100 |
| `it` | 1:29 · 2:41 · 3:21 · 4:7 · 5:2 | **9 / 100** | 5 / 100 |

*(the two columns differ because the emitted volume is stochastic — per-dwelling totals run from
**16.3 to 645.7 L/day** about the 200 L/day mean — so a few 3-person households drift in and a few
4-person households drift out. The stock means are 200.792 / 201.015 / 199.466, on target.)*

🔴 **The median household in every fold is 2.0 people, so the median is pinned near `200 / 2 = 100`
before the model runs.** `es`'s 100.16 is that identity. `uk`'s 117.65 and `it`'s 91.06 are the same
identity at mean household sizes of 1.80 and 2.12.

🔴 **THIS IS THE ANSWER TO §1.3's QUESTION. Scale will not fix it, and now we know why: there is
nothing stochastic left to average down.** A stock-scale re-measurement would return `200 / n̄` at a
bigger `n`, with a narrower confidence interval around the same wrong comparison. **Work item 11.5
must not re-measure `G9.7` at stock scale, and `G11.7` must not inherit the band until `D-S11-1` is
ruled.**
