🔴 **Headline. `G11.12`'s stock-scale failure (uk R²=0.4347, it R²=0.0781 vs 0.85 floor) is NOT
primarily a clock-hour offset (`FINDING 142`), and no amount of stock scale or circular time-shifting
can make it pass.**
1. **The timing offset is frozen across scales, not widened**: `ours` peaks at 20:00 (UK) and 18:00 (IT)
   at both 100-dwelling and stock scale (7,602 and 29,902 flats), while CREST's published reference peaks
   at 11:00 (+9 h UK, +7 h IT; Spain peaks at 14:00 vs 12:00 ref, +2 h).
2. **The apparent "best-shift R²" of ~0.61–0.64 is a mathematical artefact of squaring negative
   Pearson $r$ (anti-correlation)**: shifting by +9 h (UK) or +7 h (IT) places the simulated evening
   peak at 05:00/01:00 AM (the reference's nocturnal trough) and our daytime trough into the reference's
   midday peak, producing strong negative correlation ($r = -0.78$ and $-0.80$). Evaluated strictly on the
   positive-correlation branch ($r > 0$), UK best-shift R² gains only +0.0055 (0.4347 → 0.4402), and peak
   alignment (moving 20:00 to 11:00) collapses R² to $\approx 0.0001$. Italy peak alignment reaches only 0.2679.
3. **The true physical mechanism is laundry suppression (`FINDING 139` / `D-S9-1`) creating inverted
   diurnal hierarchies**: CREST's reference profile is overwhelmingly dominated by laundry appliances
   (tumble dryer 3,346 W + washing machine 1,319 W = 4,664 W of the 6,011 W peak, >77%) peaking at
   11:00 AM based on UK-2000 statistics. But by project ruling `D-S9-1` (primary activities only), our
   model suppresses laundry starts by 80–90% because laundry is recorded as a secondary activity in HETUS
   (`FINDING 137`/`139`). Without laundry, CREST's reference profile peaks at 21:00 (within 1 hour of our
   simulated UK 20:00 peak!). Because the model simulates an evening lighting/cooking/TV load while CREST
   expects a massive midday laundry surge, the two diurnal profiles have structurally incompatible shapes.
4. **The apparent 30–50 pp occupancy distribution drift is an artefact of Step 9's manifest omitting
   `household_sizes`**: Step 9 fell back to a two-point linear interpolation `{1: 0.20, 2: 0.80}`. The
   real underlying populations are virtually identical (~40% 1p, ~45% 2p). Conditioning on the real

5. **The aggregation arithmetic is verified bit-for-bit exact (`max_diff = 0.0000000000e+00`)**:
   `4thJ_step11_aggregate.py` has zero scoring, alignment, or indexing bugs.

🔴 **Nothing in this document moves a band, edits a checker, or changes a measured number.**

## 2. `FINDING 271` — Best-shift R² peaks are an anti-correlation artefact ($r < 0$); positive alignment yields negligible gain in UK and caps at 0.27 in Italy

`tools/4thJ_gates_step9.py` defines `r_squared(a, b)` as Pearson correlation squared:
$$R^2 = \left(\frac{\text{cov}(a, b)}{\sigma_a \sigma_b}\right)^2 = r^2$$
Because this formula squares $r$, an anti-correlation ($r = -0.80$) produces an identical $R^2 = 0.64$
to a positive correlation ($r = +0.80$).

Holding `ref` fixed and circularly shifting `ours` through all 24 hour offsets ($s \in [0, 23]$ where
$\text{shifted}[i] = \text{ours}[(i - s) \bmod 24]$) reveals that the apparent high R² values are
mathematically inverted:

| dataset | reported R² ($s=0$) | reported Pearson $r$ | Math Max R² (any $r$) | shift | Pearson $r$ at math max | Positive Max R² ($r > 0$) | shift | Pearson $r$ at pos max | Peak-Align R² (align peaks) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Step 9 / es** | 0.2967 | +0.5447 | **0.6457** | +22 h (−2 h) | **+0.8036** | **0.6457** | +22 h | +0.8036 | 0.2005 (at +2 h) |
| **Step 9 / uk** | 0.4106 | +0.6408 | **0.6004** | +9 h | **−0.7749** | **0.4504** | +22 h | +0.6711 | 0.6004 (math) / 0.0001 ($r>0$) |
| **Step 9 / it** | 0.0346 | +0.1861 | **0.6855** | +7 h | **−0.8279** | **0.3589** | +17 h | +0.5991 | 0.6855 (math) / 0.3589 ($r>0$) |
| **Step 11 / uk** | 0.4347 | +0.6593 | **0.6098** | +9 h | **−0.7809** | **0.4402** | +1 h | +0.6635 | 0.6098 (math) / 0.0001 ($r>0$) |
| **Step 11 / it** | 0.0781 | +0.2794 | **0.6415** | +7 h | **−0.8009** | **0.2679** | +17 h | +0.5176 | 0.6415 (math) / 0.2679 ($r>0$) |

🔴 **What this decomposition proves:**
1. **For Spain (Step 9), timing shift DOES work positively**: Shifting by +22 h (i.e. −2 hours, aligning
   14:00 lunch to 12:00) yields a genuine positive correlation $r = +0.8036$ and $R^2 = 0.6457$ (+0.3490 gain).
   Spain is the only country where daytime peak aligns with daytime peak.
2. **For UK and Italy, the mathematical maxima are ANTI-CORRELATIONS**:
   - In Step 11 UK, shift +9 h produces $r = -0.7809$ ($R^2 = 0.6098$). Why? Shifting by +9 h moves the
     simulated evening peak (20:00) into 05:00 AM (the nocturnal trough of CREST ref), and moves our daytime
     trough into CREST's midday peak. The profiles are inverted.
   - Restricting to positive correlation ($r > 0$), the best shift for UK is +1 h, raising R² from 0.4347 to
     only **0.4402** (a negligible +0.0055 gain).
   - If one shifts by +15 h to positively align the UK peak (20:00) with CREST's peak (11:00), Pearson $r$
     collapses to **−0.0080** ($R^2 \approx 0.0001$). Positively aligning the peaks completely destroys the
     correlation because our night trough is placed in CREST's evening shoulder.
   - In Step 11 Italy, shift +7 h produces $r = -0.8009$ ($R^2 = 0.6415$). Restricting to positive correlation,
     shift +17 h (aligning 18:00 to 11:00) raises R² from 0.0781 to **0.2679** ($r = +0.5176$).
3. **The takeaway**: Shifting clock hours can never bring UK or Italy above ~0.44 or ~0.27 on the positive branch,
   let alone the 0.85 gate threshold. The failure is **NOT** a simple phase offset.

## 3. `FINDING 272` — Laundry suppression (`FINDING 139` / `D-S9-1`) creates an inverted diurnal shape that cannot match CREST

Why does positive alignment fail so completely? Decomposing the appliance power in CREST's reference
profile (`crest_expected_diurnal`) explains the entire mechanism:

### Appliance power in CREST UK Reference Profile (at 11:00 AM vs 20:00 PM)

| Appliance | Profile | Category | Power at 11:00 AM | Share of 11:00 load | Power at 20:00 PM | Share of 20:00 load |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Tumble dryer** | 2 | Laundry | **3,345.5 W** | 55.7 % | 465.2 W | 13.5 % |
| **Washing machine** | 2 | Laundry | **1,319.0 W** | 21.9 % | 239.5 W | 6.9 % |
| TV 1 | 0 | Media | 306.0 W | 5.1 % | 906.8 W | 26.3 % |
| TV 2 | 0 | Media | 176.1 W | 2.9 % | 521.7 W | 15.1 % |
| VCR / DVD | 0 | Media | 155.3 W | 2.6 % | 459.4 W | 13.3 % |
| TV receiver box | 0 | Media | 47.9 W | 0.8 % | 374.3 W | 10.9 % |
| Other appliances | various | Cooking, IT, etc. | 662.1 W | 11.0 % | 482.8 W | 14.0 % |
| **Total Reference Load** | | | **6,011.9 W** | **100.0 %** | **3,449.7 W** | **100.0 %** |

🔴 **The mechanism:**
1. **CREST's reference is 77.6% laundry at 11:00 AM**: In CREST's published activity statistics (from UK 2000),
   laundry activity (Profile 2) peaks in the late morning (10:00–12:00). Because tumble dryers (~2.5 kW) and
   washing machines (~2.0 kW) have massive cycle wattages, this creates a towering 6.0 kW morning peak,
   dwarfing the 3.4 kW evening entertainment hump.
2. **Our simulation suppresses laundry by 80–90% (`FINDING 139` / `D-S9-1`)**:
   - `D-S9-1` ruled that appliance triggers fire on **primary activity codes only**.
   - `FINDING 137` established that laundry is overwhelmingly recorded as a **secondary activity** in time-use
     surveys (people start a wash and do something else).
   - `FINDING 139` measured the consequence: eligible laundry minutes in our diaries are only 1,589 min/year (UK)
     and 781 min/year (IT), against the 27,036 min/year CREST asks for. Modelled washing machine cycles are
     only **0.179** of published in UK and **0.092** in Italy!
3. **The two profiles have inverted daily hierarchies**:
   - **`ref`** has a massive **midday mountain** (laundry) and a minor evening shoulder.
   - **`ours`** has no morning laundry surge, and is instead dominated by evening TV, lighting, and cooking,
     producing an **evening mountain** (at 20:00 in UK, 18:00 in IT) and a low daytime plateau.
4. **The proof**: Rebuilding CREST's reference with laundry (Profile 2) excluded shifts CREST's peak hour
   from **11:00 AM to 21:00 PM** (3,011 W) — placing CREST's non-laundry peak **within 1 hour of our simulated
   UK peak (20:00)**!

Because `D-S9-1` suppresses laundry in `ours` while `G11.12` scores against a reference profile dominated by
laundry, `ours` and `ref` are fundamentally comparing an evening load against a morning load. No clock shift
can align them without mapping night into day.

## 4. `FINDING 273` — Occupancy-distribution drift is an artefact of Step 9's manifest; true populations are identical

Step 11 computes `occupancy_distribution` directly from flat household sizes (`n_members`). In Step 9,
`_occupancy_distribution()` in `4thJ_gates_step9.py` read:
```python
    sizes = m.get("household_sizes")
    if not sizes:
        mean = m["n_people"] / total
        lo = int(math.floor(mean))
        frac = mean - lo
        dist[lo] += 1.0 - frac
        dist[min(5, lo + 1)] += frac
        return dict(dist)
```
Because `step9_manifest_{es,uk,it}.json` did NOT store `"household_sizes"`, the code fell back to a 2-point
fractional interpolation around the mean:
- UK: mean = 180 / 100 = 1.80 → `{1: 0.20, 2: 0.80}`
- Italy: mean = 212 / 100 = 2.12 → `{2: 0.88, 3: 0.12}`

🔴 **The true underlying dwelling populations did NOT drift**:
`FINDING 165` recorded the actual household sizes of Step 9's 100 dwellings: UK had 39% 1-person and 47% 2-person;
Italy had 29% 1-person, 41% 2-person, 21% 3-person. Comparing those numbers against Step 11's 7,602 and 29,902 flats
shows differences of $\le 2.3$ percentage points across all bins! The populations are virtually identical because
Step 11 draws from the same Step 7 household pool.

### Impact on R² of Swapping Occupancy Distributions:
- **UK**: Step 11 native occ R² = 0.434695. Swapping in Step 9 fallback occ yields R² = 0.378698 ($\Delta = -0.0560$).
- **Italy**: Step 11 native occ R² = 0.078060. Swapping in Step 9 fallback occ yields R² = 0.055513 ($\Delta = -0.0225$).
- In Step 9 itself: Evaluating Step 9 UK with the true multi-point distribution raises R² from 0.4106 to **0.4665** (+0.0558).
  Evaluating Step 9 IT with the true multi-point distribution raises R² from 0.0346 to **0.0566** (+0.0220).

Conditioning on the true distribution rather than the 2-point fallback shifts R² by ±0.02–0.06 in the direction
of higher R², but leaves both numbers orders of magnitude below 0.85.
