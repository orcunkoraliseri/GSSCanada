## 3. 🔴 The base arm's 1.000000 was checked before it was believed

The `es` and `it` run summaries report **one single rejection reason for every one of 16,795
records** — `{"whitespace present": 16795}` — with `n_valid: 0`. A perfectly homogeneous reason
list over a whole arm is the signature of a formatting artefact, so the number was not accepted on
sight.

**What the reason list actually is.** `validate_record` tests whitespace **first**, at
`tools/4thJ_step7_grammar.py:260`, ahead of the `<eor>` terminator and every structural check. It
returns on the first failure, so any output carrying one space is filed under *whitespace* and its
real defects are never reached. 🔴 **The rejection histogram of a badly-formed arm is first-check-
wins and must not be read as a diagnosis.** `uk` shows the seam: it reports
`{"does not end with <eor>": 19, "whitespace present": 16776}` — the 19 are simply the records that
happened to contain no whitespace at all.

**The control.** Every base record was re-tested with **all** whitespace deleted — the most
generous rescue the V3.c rule could ever permit — and checked for the `<eor>` terminator, a
necessary condition for validity that needs no alphabet or policy:

| arm | n | ends `<eor>` after `.strip()` | ends `<eor>` after ALL whitespace removed |
|---|---|---|---|
| es base | 16,795 | **0** | **0** |
| uk base | 16,795 | **0** | **0** |
| it base | 16,795 | **0** | **0** |

**0 of 50,385.** No whitespace policy could have rescued a single record, so **1.000000 is a
property of the model, not of the formatting rule.**

🔴 **The control was seen registering a positive**, because a check that cannot fail is not a
check. The identical `<eor>` test on the other two arms:

| arm | es | uk | it |
|---|---|---|---|
| finetuned_unconstrained, ends `<eor>` | 0.9983 | 1.0000 | 1.0000 |
| finetuned_constrained, ends `<eor>` | 1.0000 | 1.0000 | 1.0000 |
| **untuned_base, ends `<eor>`** | **0.0000** | **0.0000** | **0.0000** |

⚪ Corroborating, not load-bearing: the raw text is prose, not a near-miss record — `es` record 1
opens `es,65-74,male,couple_with_children,retired,weekday|Living standards Zip and hood positions
were derived from U.S. Census Group Quarters and Metropolitan, Micropolitan, atthe` and then runs
on in free text. ⚪ And it is **not truncation**: `stop` rather than `length` was the finish reason
on **8,225 / 14,633 / 11,813** of the 16,795 (es/uk/it). The model ends its turn of its own accord
and still never emits the terminator.

## 4. 🔴 `FINDING 154` — the run summary's `rejection_reasons` is a masking histogram

**What.** `rejection_reasons` in `generated_*_summary.json` counts **first failures**, not failures.
On the base arms it reports whitespace at 16,795 / 16,776 / 16,795 and thereby implies the arm is
one `.strip()` away from validity. It is not: the same records fail the `<eor>` terminator at
**100 %** independently.

**Why it matters.** The obvious misreading — "the untuned backbone nearly has the format, it just
emits spaces" — is the exact opposite of the truth, and it is the reading the summary invites. This
is the same shape as `FINDING 151` and `FINDING 152`'s Control B: **an artefact that looks like a
diagnosis while the discriminating work is being done somewhere else.**

**Not fixed, deliberately.** Reordering the checks or recording all failures would change what
`validate_record` returns to every caller including the scored gates, on the day the arm is being
reported. ⚪ The mitigation is documentary: **`rejection_reasons` must be quoted as "first failing
check", never as a defect profile**, and any claim resting on it needs the independent test that
§3 ran.
