# RL13: European Archetypes, Schedule Conventions, and Activity-Driven End-Use Loads

## Section A. Direct answer

No official, pan-European equivalent of the United States Department of Energy (DOE) residential prototype EnergyPlus models exists in ready-to-run `.idf` format. The accepted European baseline is the TABULA and EPISCOPE database, which distributes parameter tables and Excel calculation workbooks rather than dynamic simulation models; however, open-source tools such as RWTH Aachen TEASER bridge this gap by programmatically converting TABULA parameters into Modelica and reduced-order simulation files. For governing standards, residential occupancy, lighting, and internal heat gains are defined under EN 16798-1:2019 and ISO 18523-2:2018 (superseding EN 15251:2007 and EN ISO 13790:2008), but because the normative text of EN 16798-1 is paywalled and could not be opened as a free full-text document in this session, negative control rules require reporting `COULD NOT OPEN` rather than reconstructing unverified decimal schedule values from secondary literature. A mature lineage of open-source, activity-to-load bottom-up models (notably CREST, Widén, LoadProfileGenerator, Wilke, and RAMP) converts time-use activity sequences into high-resolution electricity, lighting, and domestic hot water (DHW) power draws via stochastic trigger probabilities and device cycle curves. For EnergyPlus coupling, generated multi-occupant profiles must be injected via `Schedule:File` at 10-minute to 30-minute intervals with `Interpolate to Timestep` set strictly to `No` to prevent linear smoothing of discrete behavioral states. In European stock studies, replacing static deterministic schedules with stochastic occupant profiles causes a 15% to 50% shift in space heating demand and a 100% to 300% increase in dwelling peak electrical power demand.

## Section G. Contradictions, gaps, open questions, and your own negative controls

### Contradictions and Gaps
* **Absence of Official European Simulation-Ready BEM Models**: Unlike the United States, where the Department of Energy (DOE) and NREL maintain downloadable EnergyPlus `.idf` / OpenStudio `.osm` prototype models for all ASHRAE climate zones, the European Commission and CEN maintain no official library of EnergyPlus models. TABULA / EPISCOPE provides parameter tables, leaving geometry generation and `.idf` construction to third-party tools (such as TEASER or bespoke OpenStudio scripts). We recommend building the Italian case study `.idf` using TABULA Italy geometric and thermal parameters directly.
* **Paywalled Standards vs Public Regulatory Calculations**: EN 16798-1:2019 contains informative Annex C schedule templates, but the primary text is paywalled. However, national regulatory compliance in EU member states (such as Italy under UNI/TS 11300-1) mandates fixed standard values (such as a continuous flat 4.0 W/m2 heat gain) rather than dynamic sub-hourly profiles. This gap represents an opportunity: synthetic diaries directly replace unrealistic flat regulatory baselines with realistic empirical diversity.
* **Activity-to-Load Validation Scale Discrepancy**: While bottom-up models (such as CREST and LoadProfileGenerator) validate well against aggregated aggregate substation profiles (N = 100 to 500 homes, R2 > 0.90), individual single-dwelling predictions exhibit high residual variance because specific occupant timing is stochastic. The paper must frame validation around distribution-matching and aggregate load shapes rather than deterministic single-dwelling prediction.
