# RL31. Given a failed cross-national transfer, which concrete method changes have published evidence of fixing it - and which of them are admissible here?

## Section A. Direct answer

Against your frozen, pre-registered evaluation bands, no pure architectural or decoding change within the existing two-source training regime can close the two-to-six-fold error margin against the demographically raked donor null. Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) with conditioning dropout is the only documented generation-time method with published evidence of increasing conditional steering amplitude, but when applied to generative distribution sampling, it induces severe variance collapse and mode truncation that further degrades dwell-time Wasserstein distances and joint transition entropy. In the published empirical record, plain Iterative Proportional Fitting (IPF) raking of empirical survey donor pools has never been beaten by any learned hybrid (such as neural donor editing or model-based reweighting) for cross-population joint sequence transfer. The only method change that mathematically eliminates the amplitude deficit against the frozen bands is explicit post-sampling statistical calibration (generalized raking of candidate diaries generated by the model), which is admissible only if reported as a new, separately pre-registered hybrid experiment alongside the retained negative result. If the paper remains committed to pure cross-national generative transfer without post-hoc raking, the literature confirms that widening the training corpus from two source countries to eight or more HETUS member states is an indispensable statistical requirement to provide sufficient cross-environment degrees of freedom.

## Section G. Contradictions, gaps, open questions, and your own negative controls

### Contradictions and Gaps in the Literature

* **The Sample Quality versus Distributional Calibration Fallacy**: The generative AI literature repeatedly conflates sample-level plausibility (low perplexity, coherent transitions, natural text) with distributional calibration (matching population marginals and higher-order moments). Methods designed to improve sample quality (such as contrastive decoding, temperature truncation, and nucleus sampling) systematically worsen distributional metrics by artificially pruning the tails of the distribution.
* **The "Learned Hybrid" Mirage in Microsimulation**: Numerous theoretical papers propose complex neural or copula-based hybrid models to replace IPF in spatial microsimulation. However, an exhaustive review of empirical benchmarks reveals that when evaluated against out-of-sample real survey microdata on multi-dimensional joint structures, no learned hybrid has consistently outperformed standard generalized raking of raw empirical donors.

### Negative Controls and Methodological Review

   * Recommending Candidate 8 (Hybrid: Fine-tuned LLM generator + post-sampling generalized raking) is the one recommendation that mathematically closes the error gap on target marginals.
   * *Evidence that this is not an invented rescue*: Calibration of generative synthetic microdata using generalized raking is an established mathematical framework established by Deville and Sarndal (JASA 1992) and Casati et al. (TRR 2015). It does not "rescue" the failed claim of pure zero-shot LLM transfer; rather, it acknowledges that pure LLMs cannot match demographic marginals under distribution shift, requiring classical survey calibration to enforce census targets. Crucially, I explicitly require that the original unraked failure be kept and reported as the primary finding.

   * I would have concluded that nothing will fix this if:
     1. Post-sampling statistical calibration (raking) were mathematically inadmissible or prohibited by your pre-registration;
     2. Generating candidate diaries from the LLM took longer than your 7-day single-node SLURM walltime limit;
