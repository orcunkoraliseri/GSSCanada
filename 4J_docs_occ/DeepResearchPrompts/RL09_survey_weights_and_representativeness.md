# Response: L09. Survey Weights and Population Representativeness

## Section A. Direct answer

We recommend a strictly decoupled, two-stage generation pipeline: synthesize concrete urban households from census marginals first using established demographic methods (Iterative Proportional Fitting or Combinatorial Optimisation), and then condition the language model on each synthetic demographic vector to emit the time-use diary. During training of the conditional sequence model, sample weights should not be applied to the token-level loss; because the model is conditioned on the full demographic and calendar vector (which contains the design strata variables), the sampling mechanism is conditionally ignorable ($P(\text{diary} \mid X, \text{selected}) = P(\text{diary} \mid X)$), and unweighted empirical risk minimization avoids the severe gradient variance and optimization instability proven to afflict importance-weighted deep networks. Post-hoc raking of generated diaries is mathematically incapable of repairing distorted joint interaction structures and should be avoided in favor of demographic conditioning. For decoding, truncated sampling (top-$k$ or nucleus top-$p$) systematically discards rare nocturnal and irregular schedules that drive urban building peak energy loads; decoding must employ temperature scaling calibrated on validation log-likelihood combined with grammar-constrained masks rather than tail truncation. Source survey non-response in European time-use surveys introduces an uncorrectable selective non-response bias of 30% to 65% across member states that must be bounded and explicitly declared as a core limitation.

* Reject post-hoc output raking in the Methodology section, noting that while raking adjusts univariate marginals, it cannot mathematically restore unlearned or distorted multi-way interaction structures between activity chains and occupant attributes (Little & Wu 1991; Deville & Särndal 1992) [Row 7].

* Insert quantitative bounds into the Consolidated Limitations section: state that the generative model inherits the baseline coverage and non-response limitations of the source HETUS surveys, which exhibit 30% to 65% unit non-response across European member states and an average Kish design effect of $deff_w \approx 1.25 - 1.60$ [Row 4, Row 10, Row 11].

## Section G. Contradictions, gaps, open questions, and your own negative controls

### Critical Contradictions and Methodological Tensions
* Survey Statistics vs Deep Learning on Loss Weighting: Survey statistics (Pfeffermann 1993, Chambers & Skinner 2003) prescribes pseudo-maximum likelihood (weighted loss) to guarantee design consistency under informative sampling. In contrast, deep learning theory and empirical findings (Byrd & Lipton 2019) prove that sample-weighted loss in overparameterized networks trained via SGD/Adam causes severe gradient variance explosion and fails to alter learned representations once training loss approaches zero. We resolve this contradiction by leveraging conditional ignorability: because the model is trained conditionally on demographic attributes ($P(\text{diary} \mid X)$), the sampling design is conditionally non-informative, allowing unweighted training for $P(\text{diary} \mid X)$ while population-level representativeness is enforced during demographic synthesis in Stage 1.
* Stratified Batching vs Diary Day Weights: HETUS diary weights (`DIA_WGT`) already incorporate an inverse-probability multiplier for day-of-week allocation (e.g. 5/2 ratio). If a training pipeline uses stratified batching (enforcing equal or fixed proportions of weekdays and weekend days per batch) while simultaneously multiplying loss by `DIA_WGT`, the day-type probability adjustment is applied twice. If loss weights were ever applied, they would need to be normalized conditionally within each batch stratum. Under our recommended unweighted conditional architecture, this conflict is completely eliminated.
* Fidelity vs Peak-Load Diversity in Truncated Decoding: Standard natural language generation literature recommends nucleus (top-$p$) sampling to prevent degenerative gibberish in open-ended text. However, in building energy simulation, rare behavioral sequences (e.g. nocturnal cooking, early-morning electric vehicle charging, irregular shift work) constitute the decisive tail events driving peak electrical transformer loads. Applying standard top-$p$ ($p=0.90$) truncates these critical tail behaviors. The tension must be resolved by using grammar-constrained decoding (which guarantees 100% validity without probability truncation) combined with validation temperature scaling.

### Literature Gaps and `NOT FOUND` Items
* Literature Search for LLM Fine-Tuning on Weighted Survey Data: We conducted comprehensive searches across arXiv, ACL Anthology, IEEE, and Google Scholar for studies fine-tuning autoregressive language models on weighted survey microdata (using Census, CPS, ACS, ATUS, or HETUS weights). We report `NOT FOUND`. The nearest existing analogues are: (1) importance-weighted empirical risk minimization in deep classification (Byrd & Lipton 2019); (2) class-imbalanced token/sequence loss weighting in instruction tuning; and (3) demographic opinion alignment via prompting without weight-modified loss (Santurkar et al., ICML 2023).
