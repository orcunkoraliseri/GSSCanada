# RL33. Venue positioning, re-run for a null-result paper: AI/data-driven venue versus building-science venue, and where Software X fits

## Section A. Direct answer

A pre-registered null result where an iterative proportional fitting donor-pool baseline beats a fine-tuned 7 B language model fits significantly worse at an AI or data-driven methods venue than a positive result would. Mainstream applied AI venues, including Expert Systems with Applications and Applied Intelligence, judge applied papers on technical novelty and empirical outperformance of baselines; in those venues, a null result is routinely dismissed by reviewers as an engineering failure or insufficient parameter tuning. Software X is decisively eliminated for this manuscript: its aims and scope mandate original, reusable software packages under open licenses (typically under six pages), whereas this manuscript is an 18,000-word empirical scientific paper whose adapter weights and British synthetic population are legally and ethically withheld. While multidisciplinary open-science venues like Scientific Reports and Data-Centric Engineering tolerate or encourage null results, Scientific Reports requires an out-of-pocket article processing charge of $2,690 USD (excluded from CRKN fee waivers) and Data-Centric Engineering lacks reviewers versed in EnergyPlus building simulation. RL14's original recommendation of Energy and Buildings as the primary target and Building and Environment as the secondary target firmly stands. In building science, establishing that 50-year-old survey calibration outperforms fine-tuned language models directly addresses an urgent stock-modeling problem, perfectly embodies recent flagship calls for rigorous AI benchmarking in building engineering (Hong and Li, 2026), and is 100 percent fee-waived under the Concordia CRKN agreement.

## Section G. Contradictions, gaps, open questions, and your own negative controls

### Contradictions and gaps identified

* **The Author's Venue Preference vs. Editorial Realities:** The author proposed redirecting the paper to an AI/data-driven venue (and specifically suggested Software X) under the assumption that a null result regarding an LLM is a machine-learning finding. This contradicts the fundamental operational culture of applied AI journals. Applied AI venues (ESWA, Applied Intelligence) operate under an aggressive SOTA-seeking paradigm; reviewers evaluate manuscripts on whether the proposed method achieves superior metrics against baselines. Submitting a paper whose headline finding is that an LLM fails to beat a simple 1970s statistical raking technique will almost certainly trigger immediate rejection on the grounds of "poor model performance" or "lack of algorithmic contribution." Conversely, building-science venues evaluate whether a method solves a practical building simulation problem. In Energy and Buildings, showing that practitioners should use real donor diaries raked onto target marginals rather than burning GPU hours on an LLM is an impactful, highly cited finding.
* **Software X Misconception:** Software X is designed for software developers to publish original, reusable research code (typically under 6 pages). The present manuscript is an 18,000-word empirical scientific paper that deliberately withholds the fine-tuned adapter weights and British synthetic microdata. Attempting to submit the current manuscript to Software X would result in an immediate desk rejection for violating article type, length, and mandatory open-license requirements.
* **The Open Access Cost Trap:** While Scientific Reports and Energy and AI welcome or tolerate null results, both are Gold Open Access journals where the author would incur substantial out-of-pocket costs ($2,690 USD for Scientific Reports; ~$2,550 USD for Energy and AI) because both are excluded from 100 percent CRKN fee waivers for Concordia authors. In contrast, Energy and Buildings and Building and Environment are hybrid journals offering 100 percent fee waivers ($0 cost) under the CRKN-Elsevier agreement.
