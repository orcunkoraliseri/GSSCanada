# RL30. Our cross-national transfer claim FAILED against its pre-registered null. Is that result already known in the literature, and on what evidence?

## Section A. Direct answer

A like-for-like empirical comparison between a fine-tuned or prompted large language model and a demographically raked real-donor null on an unseen population distribution is not published anywhere in the literature. The contemporary tabular and human mobility LLM literature has systematically avoided this test by evaluating almost exclusively on random in-distribution splits or against unraked, unconditioned strawman baselines. However, across classical survey statistics, spatial microsimulation, and travel demand modeling (independent of LLMs), the finding that an uncalibrated transferred parametric model loses decisively to an Iterative Proportional Fitting (IPF) raked donor pool is an established, mathematically grounded reality: raked donors preserve true empirical higher-order joint correlations while matching target marginals by minimum-divergence calibration. Furthermore, domain generalization theory proves that two source training countries (K = 2) provide only one cross-environment degree of freedom, which is mathematically insufficient to disentangle invariant behavioral mechanisms from domain-specific spurious correlations across twenty demographic covariates. The observed amplitude failure (slope 0.40 to 0.53 despite R-squared at or above 0.80) is a known manifestation of conditional regression dilution and Bayesian posterior mean contraction under standard cross-entropy loss: without explicit guidance, conditional autoregressive models heavily shrink their conditional outputs toward the empirical prior of the training mixture. Publishing negative results that refute fashionable neural scaling claims in favor of rigorous classical baselines has prominent, high-impact precedent in top computing and machine learning venues, provided the paper relocates its primary contribution from the failed substantive claim to the diagnostic apparatus, benchmarking rigor, and the empirical boundary conditions of generative transfer.

In the results section, explain the fictional-country control outcome (R-squared >= 0.80, slope 0.40 to 0.53) using conditional mean shrinkage and autoregressive prompt attenuation (Sanchez et al. 2023): the model learns correct directional associations, but standard cross-entropy loss penalizes deviations from the empirical training prior, compressing the amplitude toward the multi-country mixture mean.
* **Adopt the diagnostic narrative structure of RecSys 2019 and AAAI 2023** (tied to Section B Rows 12, 13). In the concluding sections, adopt the constructive critical tone of Ferrari Dacrema et al. ## Section G. Contradictions, gaps, open questions, and your own negative controls

### Contradictions and Gaps in the Literature

* **The Tabular LLM Evaluation Contradiction**: Papers promoting large language models for tabular synthesis (e.g. Borisov et al. 2023, Solatorio and Dupriez 2023) celebrate low Wasserstein distances and high machine learning efficacy, but exclusively test on random in-distribution splits of individual datasets. When evaluated on actual cross-population transfer, as demonstrated in this study, the LLM loses decisively to classical donor calibration. The published literature presents an illusion of generative superiority created by selecting weak or inappropriate evaluation splits.
* **The "Generalization" Myth in Spatial Travel Modeling**: The transport literature frequently asserts that activity-based microsimulation models represent fundamental human behavioral utility and should therefore be spatially transferable (e.g. Bowman et al. 2014). However, rigorous empirical transfer studies (Sikder et al. 2013, Yasmin et al. 2015) repeatedly contradict this assertion, showing that uncalibrated model transfer produces 30% to 100% error unless local constants or marginals are updated.

6. **What is the strongest published evidence AGAINST our interpretation that this is a genuine negative result about the method, rather than an artefact of our corpus, our null, or our metric?**
   * The strongest counter-argument rests on domain generalization and statistical sample complexity theory (Rosenfeld et al. 2021, Gulrajani and Lopez-Paz 2021). An adversarial reviewer can forcefully argue that evaluating cross-national transfer with only two training countries and one held-out country (Spain, UK, Italy) is a fundamentally ill-posed experimental design, not a failure of language model capacity. With K = 2 training environments, the degrees of freedom between source populations is exactly one (K - 1 = 1). Meanwhile, the conditioning vector spans twenty or more demographic and structural attributes. In any statistical or deep learning system, trying to isolate invariant country-independent behavioral mappings from a single pair of training countries while varying twenty covariates is completely under-determined. Furthermore, conditioning solely on published demographic marginals starves the generator of essential macro-level explanatory variables: differences in national time-use between Spain and the UK (such as meal hours, school schedules, and shop opening hours) are driven by national labor laws, institutional norms, daylight hours, and climate, none of which can be inferred from age and employment marginals alone. Under this reading, your negative result does not prove that large language models cannot perform cross-national transfer; it proves only that no machine learning model can extrapolate cultural time-use across a high-dimensional covariate space when trained on exactly two national points.
