# RL28. Does Inter-Dwelling Occupancy Diversity Change Building Peak Demand - and by How Much, as a Function of How Many Dwellings Are Modelled Independently?

## Section A. Direct answer

The published literature in building performance simulation and electrical distribution network planning confirms that inter-dwelling occupancy diversity causes coincident peak demand per dwelling to decrease monotonically as a function of the number of independently modelled dwellings $N$, following Rusck's square-root aggregation law $\gamma(N) = \gamma_\infty + (1 - \gamma_\infty)/\sqrt{N}$. This diversity effect saturates rapidly, with over 70% of total peak reduction achieved by $N = 10$ and over 90% by $N = 20$ to $30$, reaching an asymptotic plateau beyond $N \approx 50$. However, the magnitude of this effect differs fundamentally across energy channels: it drives a 60% to 80% peak reduction in occupant-driven domestic electricity demand ($\gamma_\infty \approx 0.20 - 0.35$), but produces only a 2% to 6% peak reduction in space heating demand ($\gamma_\infty \approx 0.85 - 0.95$). This divergence occurs because space heating peaks are synchronized by exterior weather forcing ($UA \cdot \Delta T$) and heavily damped by building thermal mass ($C_m \approx 45 - 87\text{ Wh}/(\text{m}^2\cdot\text{K})$), yielding envelope thermal time constants of $\tau = 50 - 150\text{ hours}$ that attenuate sub-daily internal gain fluctuations by over 93%. Under a strictly conserved-mean injection (fixed annual average of 3.0 W/m²), redistributing schedule timing without altering energy magnitude produces an annual space heating delta of near zero because the heating gain utilization factor $\eta_{H,gn}$ remains near unity throughout European winters. Consequently, Step 8's annual null is the physically expected outcome of conserved-mean injection in heavy-mass stock, while the surviving dwelling-class ordering represents the underlying $1/\sqrt{N}$ peak diversity mechanism. To isolate this effect in Step 10 without geometric confounding, OpenUBEM must execute a strictly within-building paired test comparing synchronized ($N = 1$) against independent ($N = N_u$) stochastic assignments across identical building footprints.

## Section G. Contradictions, gaps, open questions, and negative controls

### Contradictions and Gaps
* **Thermal Peak vs Electrical Peak Coincidence Gap**: Electrical distribution literature (Bary 1945; Rusck 1956; Richardson et al. 2010) frequently reports coincidence factors dropping below 0.30 at $N = 30$. In contrast, thermal building simulation studies (Baetens et al. 2012; Fischer et al. 2015) demonstrate that space heating coincidence factors rarely drop below 0.80 to 0.85 because building heat loss is fundamentally synchronized by ambient outdoor temperature. Authors who cite electrical diversity numbers to justify space heating diversity claims commit a category error.
* **Conserved-Mean vs Magnitude-Shift Discrepancy**: A widespread contradiction in building performance literature is the conflation of behavioral schedule timing with internal gain magnitude. Studies showing 15-50% heating energy differences invariably varied total occupant numbers or appliance wattage budgets. When annual mean gains are held constant at 3.0 W/m², the thermodynamic impact on annual heating in heavy envelopes is negligible ($\Delta < 1\%$).
* **Dwelling Class Ordering in Homogeneous Archetypes**: In Step 8, each TABULA box used a single diary ($N = 1$). Despite the absence of inter-dwelling diversity, the effect was monotone in dwelling class (`AB` > `MFH` > `TH`). This occurs because multi-family archetypes have lower exterior surface-to-volume ratios ($S/V$) and higher internal thermal mass per unit of envelope loss, making their relative internal temperature floating dynamics slightly more responsive to temporal gain variations than exposed single-family structures.
