# P04. Demand-response intervention typologies, and the actual field efficacy of peer-comparison / social-norm messaging

## Why we are asking

We need to settle a factual question, and it has to be settled correctly because a judgement rests on
it.

A common three-way typology of residential demand-response interventions is: **direct command /
regulatory compliance**, **price or economic information**, and **social norm / peer comparison**. We
want (a) the correct citations for that typology, and (b) — the part that matters — **what the field
evidence actually says about the effectiveness of the third type.**

Our understanding is that peer comparison, delivered at scale through home energy reports, is one of
the better-evidenced behavioural interventions in the energy literature, with average savings on the
order of a couple of percent across very large samples, and larger effects for high-consuming
households. If that is right, then a statement that field evidence shows peer comparison has
*limited* efficacy would be a misreading. **But we may be the ones misreading, and this prompt exists
to find out.**

## What we need

### Item 1. The typology and its correct sources

1. Where does the direct-command / economic-information / social-norm typology of residential DR
   interventions actually come from? Give the primary sources with resolvable DOIs.
2. Is there a more standard or more recent taxonomy that has superseded it (price-based vs
   incentive-based vs behavioural; or the IEA / ACEEE / EPRI classifications)? Name it.
3. 🔴 Two works are frequently cited together for this typology in building-energy papers:
   * Gyamfi and Krumdieck, *Price, environment and security: exploring multi-modal motivation in
     voluntary residential peak demand response*, Energy Policy 39(5) 2011, `10.1016/j.enpol.2011.03.012`
   * Albadi and El-Saadany, *A summary of demand response in electricity markets*, Electric Power
     Systems Research 78(11) 2008, `10.1016/j.epsr.2008.04.002`

   **Open both.** For each, state precisely: what it is, what it measured, and — specifically —
   **whether it contains any result on peer-comparison or social-norm messaging efficacy.** A yes/no
   with the page or section. We need this exactly right.

### Item 2. Peer comparison / social norms — the actual measured efficacy

1. Give the canonical large-scale field evidence. We expect the home-energy-report / OPOWER
   randomised trials to be central. Report: **number of households, number of trials, average
   treatment effect in percent, and the heterogeneity across the pre-treatment consumption
   distribution.** With the source and the table it comes from.
2. Report the **persistence** literature: what happens when reports stop, what the decay rate is,
   whether effects habituate.
3. Report the **cost-effectiveness** framing (cost per kWh saved) since that is why utilities adopted
   it.
4. Report the **negative and null results** honestly: the boomerang effect for low consumers and the
   injunctive-norm correction; failures to replicate outside the US; contexts where it did not work.
5. 🔴 **Then answer directly, in one sentence in Section A: is "field evidence shows peer-comparison
   messaging has limited efficacy" a fair summary of this literature, an unfair one, or a defensible
   reading of a specific sub-part of it?**

### Item 3. The comparison across intervention types

This is what we actually want for modelling, not just for adjudication.

1. Is there evidence **comparing** the three intervention types **head to head** in the same
   population — direct load control / command versus price information versus social norm?
2. What are the relative effect sizes, and do they differ by **household demographics** — income,
   employment status, age, presence of children, home occupancy during peak?
3. 🔴 Specifically: is there evidence that **cost-sensitive households respond more to economic
   framing** and that **older or retired households respond more to authority/direct framing**?
   Those are plausible-sounding claims that get asserted in modelling papers; we want to know whether
   they are measured or merely plausible.
4. What is known about response to **direct load control** signals in residential settings, and how
   is "acceptance" or "compliance" typically operationalised and measured?

### Item 4. Non-US evidence

Our own work is on Spain, the UK and Italy.

1. What field evidence on any of the three intervention types exists for **European residential**
   populations?
2. Are the effect sizes comparable to the US ones, and is there a documented reason if not?
