## Decisions taken here, and what they were based on

1. **`FINDING 68` → per-cell verdict on the sample-size-matched floor.** 🟢 **Ruled (a) by the
   author, 2026-08-21.** It is registered discipline, not new design: the Overview already
   pre-registers the split-half bootstrap comparison. The absolute bands stay and are enforced at
   population level.
2. **The floor is the WORST of 20 real-real draws**, and the family-level null is printed with it
   (analytic: each metric exceeds all R floors with probability 1/(R+1) under the null). Reported as
   counts against expectations, never as "k cells are broken".
3. **`MIN_CELL_N` = 100**, taken from `G4_1_MIN_STRATUM_N` — the project's one existing precedent
   for "too small to score". A scoring parameter, not a threshold.
4. **Transition-matrix TVD on the JOINT (from, to) distribution**, because it is bounded in [0, 1]
   without a per-row sample-size guard and so can carry a fixed 0.050 band honestly.
5. **Level-1 activity = the first digit** of the three-digit code — the 10 categories the Tier 1 row
   already counts.
6. 🔴 **No diary weights.** `D-S6-4` (which weight Step 6 scores on) is **OPEN**. `--weight-field`
   exists so the ruling applies without the statistic changing, and it **raises** rather than
   silently weighting on a basis nobody chose.

## 🟢 2026-08-21 (night) — `D-S6-4` RESIDUE CLOSED: `G6.8` NO LONGER REFUSES `--weight-field`, AND THE BASIS TURNS OUT TO BE WORTH MORE THAN THE BAND

`D-S6-4` was ruled by the author on 2026-08-21 (`Step6_docs/4thJ_06_transfer.md:1059`): **Step 6 scores
on `weight_dia_cal`**, with `weight_dia` reported beside it as a **declared sensitivity**, and the two
**never mixed**. The ruling left one residue, and this is it: `G6.8`'s checker still carried
`raise SystemExit("--weight-field is not implemented")`, so the gate that the ruling is *about* could
not be run on the ruled basis. The author ruled the residue **(a) — wire it in now**, rather than wait
for `L27` Part A's external confirmation, which has no return date.

### What was wired, and the one thing that made it non-trivial

🔴 **`4J_step3_corpus.jsonl` carries no weight column at all.** Its records are
`country, hid, pid, diary_day, split, text` and nothing else. Every weight lives in
`harmonised.parquet`, one row per **episode**. So the weight is **joined**, on the diary key
`(country upper-cased, pid, diary_day as a string)`, which is unique at **73,254** rows — exactly the
corpus size, so the join is total by construction and not by luck. ⚪ Two UK diaries (89 episodes)
carry a null `weight_dia` and therefore a null `weight_dia_cal`; they are **dropped under weighting
and the drop is printed**, never promoted to `1.0` among numbers in the thousands.

Weighting now runs through **every distribution** `G6.8` scores — the dwell-time ECDFs (a weighted
W1, exact on the merged support, no binning), the transition joint, transitions per day, Level-1 time
budgets, the 144-slot diurnal profile, and the co-presence table — and through the three registered
negative controls and the Markov comparator, so a synthesised diary inherits the weight of the real
diary it stands in for and the two sides are never one weighted and one not.

🔴 **It touches no COUNT.** `MIN_CELL_N` (100) and `MIN_DWELL_N` (30) still read the **unweighted**
number of diaries and episodes. Sample size decides what is *scorable*; weight decides what it
*represents*. A cell of three diaries carrying a large weight is still three diaries and is still
skipped.

⚪ Weight scale is not normalised and does not need to be: every statistic here is a weighted mean or
a normalised distribution, and `G6.8` is always scored **within one country**. The scales differ by
orders of magnitude between countries — ES mean 8,490, IT 4,255, UK 1.004 — which is exactly why
nothing in this module ever pools two countries' weights.

### Additivity proved, then the wiring proved live

The `G6.8` selftest was run **before and after** the refactor with the weight left at its unweighted
default, and the two outputs are **byte-identical** — all 22 pre-existing checks, including the
across-diary control felling all three sequence arms and preserving both marginal arms to machine
zero. The refactor is additive and that is a `diff`, not a claim.

Five new checks were then added, section 8, and all pass:

| check | result |
|---|---|
| a **constant** weight leaves every registered statistic unchanged | PASS — 6 of 6 statistics, `w=1` vs `w=7` |
| a **non-constant** weight moves them | PASS — 6 of 6 moved; the wiring is live, not decorative |
| the weight table keys the whole corpus | PASS — 73,252 keyed, 2 null and excluded |
| every real diary of the fold joins a weight | PASS — 38,260 loaded, 0 dropped, 0 unkeyed |
| the ruled weight re-bases the real time budgets | PASS — largest Level-1 shift **36.15 min/day** on `it` |

### 🔴 `FINDING 72` — the choice of weighting basis is LARGER than `G6.8`'s own tolerance, and it is country-correlated by construction

Having made the three bases runnable, the distance between them was **measured** rather than assumed
small. Level-1 population time budgets, transitions per day, and the transition-matrix TVD, per fold:

| fold | comparison | worst Level-1 budget shift | transitions/day | transition TVD |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **es** | none → `weight_dia` | 9.18 min/day | −0.121 | 0.0115 |
| **es** | `weight_dia` → **`weight_dia_cal`** | 🔴 **27.14 min/day** | +0.145 | 0.0279 |
| **uk** | none → `weight_dia` | 33.46 min/day | −0.263 | 0.0247 |
| **uk** | `weight_dia` → **`weight_dia_cal`** | ⚪ **0.03 min/day** | −0.000 | 0.0000 |
| **it** | none → `weight_dia` | 7.71 min/day | −0.008 | 0.0068 |
| **it** | `weight_dia` → **`weight_dia_cal`** | 🔴 **43.87 min/day** | −0.055 | 0.0356 |

`G6.8`'s registered population band on the same statistic is **8.0 min/day**, and its transition-TVD
band is **0.050**. So:

1. 🔴 **Switching between two defensible weight columns moves a scored statistic by up to
   `43.87` min/day — `5.5×` the band it is scored against** — and consumes up to **71 %** of the
   transition-TVD band (`0.0356` of `0.050`) before the model has been asked to do anything. The
   basis was never a presentational choice; it was decisive, and had it stayed open until after a
   fold was scored it would have been a choice made while looking at the result.
2. 🔴 **The move is country-correlated by construction, and in the direction `FINDING 53` predicts.**
   The UK diary weights already hit the calendar week (71.45 / 14.32 / 14.24), so `weight_dia_cal`
   has almost nothing to do there — `0.03` min/day, TVD `0.0000`. Spain (50/25/25) and Italy
   (33/33/33) do not, and they move by 27 and 44 min/day. In a leave-one-country-out design this is
   the worst possible shape for an unruled basis: it would have changed two folds and left the third
   untouched, and any cross-fold comparison would have carried the difference as if it were a
   transfer result. **This is a second, independent argument for the ruling the author made**, and it
   was not available when the ruling was made.
3. ⚪ **Read the middle column, not the outer ones.** `none → weight_dia` is large on the UK (33.46)
   and small on Italy (7.71) — the reverse ordering. Nothing here says one country's weights are
   "bigger"; it says the two *corrections* land in different places, which is precisely why the
   headline basis has to be fixed once, for all folds, in advance.

⚪ **Reported, never thresholded.** None of these numbers is a gate. They exist so the sensitivity
`D-S6-4` requires can be *stated with a magnitude* instead of being called "a check we also ran", and
so that the paper's weighting sentence is defensible.
