## 1. What a collapse is, and what it is not

`tools/4thJ_step6_rakeddonor.py`'s `rake()` takes an optional
`collapse={variable: {donor_category: target_category}}`. Before raking, each donor carrying
`donor_category` on `variable` is re-labelled to `target_category`. It touches the **donor pool
only**: no marginal file is rewritten and no synthetic person is edited. The collapse is stamped onto
the run's provenance label, e.g.

```
outputs_step5/marginals_es.csv@2026-08-20|collapse=strat_econ_status:homemaker>other_inactive
```

`rake()`'s Guard 5 raises `RakeError` if, after the collapse, any donor category is still absent from
the target, so a collapse cannot silently mask a second orphan. `FINDING 52` — orphan donors
multiplied by zero while `max_dev_pp` reported 5.6e-15, deleting 16.67 % of the pool in silence — is
the defect that forced Guard 5 to exist. The fix was additive; the selftest went 23 → 34 green.

**Not a collapse, and not covered by this file:** the donor-seed sensitivity in
`tools/4thJ_step5_synthesise.py` *drops* `strat_hh_type == unknown` donors instead of re-labelling
them. That is a drop, it is counted and printed on every run, and it rests on `D-S3-14`.

## 2. The three collapses

| # | Collapse | Binds on fold | Donor diaries moved | Ruling it follows from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | `strat_hh_type: unknown → other_complex` | `es`, `it` | 551 (all UK) | `D-S3-14` |
| **B** | `strat_econ_status: homemaker → other_inactive` | `es` | 5,584 (uk 710 + it 4,874) | `D-S5-4` (b), `FINDING 51` |
| **C** | `strat_econ_status: unknown → other_inactive` | `it` | 68 (all UK) | `D-S5-11` (b) |

Collapse A is *passed* on all three folds and *binds* on two: on `uk` the 551 diaries are held out
with the rest of the fold, so there is nothing to move.

### A — `strat_hh_type: unknown → other_complex`

`D-S3-14` ruled that the UK-only `strat_hh_type = unknown` value — 551 diaries, a blank UKDA-derived
`dhhtype` with no alternative field — STAYS in the corpus as a declared limitation. No census in any
country publishes an `unknown` household-type row, so whenever the UK is a **donor** those 551
diaries are orphans. Without the collapse `G6.1`'s null **refuses to build at all** on `es` and `it`.

`other_complex` rather than a drop: dropping deletes 551 real diaries from two of three donor pools
and moves the null's base; `other_complex` is the residual category the household mapping already
uses for households fitting no named nucleus type.

**Demonstrated:** `--no-collapse` makes `es` and `it` REFUSE with `RakeError` naming the orphan
category and its count. `uk` still converges, correctly.

### B — `strat_econ_status: homemaker → other_inactive`

`FINDING 51`: the Spanish Censo 2011 `RELA` variable has six values and **none is *Labores del
hogar***. There is no `homemaker` row to rake onto, and `D-S5-4` (b) accordingly fits `es` on FIVE
economic bands. The donors' `homemaker` label must land on an admissible band.

`other_inactive` is the band the Spanish census residual corresponds to, and it is the same merge
direction Eurostat itself applies — the merge that made Route 3 (national statistical offices)
necessary under `D-S5-1`.

**Cost, stated.** Spain's five-band fit cannot distinguish a homemaker from another economically
inactive person. The corpus is 11.140 % homemaker; the Spanish census residual inactive band is
8.787 % in total. Any Step 6 or Step 9 reading that turns on that distinction is unavailable for
`es` and must not be reported as if it were.

### C — `strat_econ_status: unknown → other_inactive`  🔴 the one nobody had counted

`D-S5-11` (b) split the constructed `unknown` band back over real economic bands using the donor
pool's own mix at those ages. On `it` that emptied the band completely — no donor country labels a
minor `unknown` — so Italy's fitted population has **no `unknown` economic row at all**. The 68 UK
donor diaries carrying `econ = unknown` therefore became orphans on that fold, and the generic
"a donor band the target lost entirely must go somewhere" branch of
`tools/4thJ_step6_g61_rake_folds.py` collapses them to `other_inactive`.

**It is small and it is not negligible.** 68 diaries is 0.09 % of the corpus, but they are the *same*
68 British diaries `FINDING 62` was about — Italy's null used to rest on them at ESS 46 %.
`D-S5-11` (b) took Italy's ESS to 76.8 %, and this collapse is part of why: those donors are now
weighted as `other_inactive` rather than being orphaned.

**Cost, stated.** A UK respondent whose economic status the UKDA left blank is asserted to be
economically inactive when the UK is a donor into `it`. That assertion is not supported by the UKDA
record; it is the cheapest admissible landing place, and it moves 68 of 73,254 diaries.

## 4. What would invalidate this addendum

* A Step 6 score for any fold found to predate this file.
* **A collapse applied anywhere in the codebase that is not A, B or C above.** This is enforced, not
  asserted: `tools/4thJ_step6_g61_rake_folds.py` refuses to rake if the collapse dict it built
  contains a pair this file does not register, and names the unregistered pair.
* A collapse applied WITHOUT the provenance label carrying it — the label is what makes any of this
  checkable after the fact.
