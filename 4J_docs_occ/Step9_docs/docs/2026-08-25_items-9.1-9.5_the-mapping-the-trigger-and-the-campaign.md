## 4. The trigger — what is CREST's and what is ours

**Reproduced from CREST**, via the open re-implementation rather than reinvented: the OFF /
RESTART-DELAY / RUNNING state machine; the constant start hazard over eligible minutes; the cycle
that **runs to completion** and is never truncated by the end of the activity episode; the pause rule
with CREST's own LEVEL / laundry / CUSTOM exceptions; the TV cycle-length draw
`round(70·(−ln(1−u))^1.1)`; the staged washing-machine power curve.

**The one adaptation, stated plainly.** CREST has no diary, so it reads `P(activity at minute t)`
from a table of TUS statistics. **We have the diary.** The table look-up is replaced by the diary's
own 0/1 indicator. Because a 0/1 indicator is not a population share, the start hazard is
recalibrated with **CREST's own calibration equation** so that expected annual starts still equal the
published `Cycles per year (n)`.

⚪ The geometric skip over eligible minutes is an **exact** restatement of the per-minute Bernoulli
draw, not an approximation: with a constant hazard the number of eligible minutes to the next start
is geometric. It exists because a per-minute loop over 33 appliances × 525,600 minutes × 100
dwellings does not finish.

### 🔴 The closed form was not enough, and the loop that replaced it is CREST's too

The first campaign returned ratios of **0.46 to 0.54** against the published cycles for televisions
and cooking appliances. The cause is measurable: CREST's closed form subtracts the minutes an
appliance is busy **pro-rated over the year**, which is right when the activity probability is a
small population share and wrong when the indicator is 0/1 — a television is busy through most of its
**own** eligible minutes, not a year-averaged sample of them.

🟢 **Iterating to hit the published annual target is CREST's own procedure** (the reference
implementation bisects a calibration factor against an annual consumption target for the same
reason). The loop raises the hazard until the stock mean meets the published count, and **the
published count is never adjusted**. Trace kept in every manifest.

**On fold `es`, 20 of the 23 appliances are evaluable** -- three are standby-only devices CREST carries at 1e-05 cycles a year -- **and of those 20, 15 land inside the +/-15 % band and 13 within 2 %.** The five that do not are §5, and they are below the band, not above it.

## 7. 🔴 `FINDING 141` — the diary day starts at 04:00, and Step 8's schedules were written as if it started at midnight

**Full record and the author's options: `2026-08-25_D-S9-3_FINDING-141_the-diary-day-starts-at-0400.md`.**

`D-S2-5` harmonised every diary onto a **04:00** origin. `4thJ_step7_schedules.py` writes minute 0
into `Schedule:File` position 0, and EnergyPlus reads that from **midnight**. Every occupancy
schedule Step 8 simulated is rotated **four hours early**.

Confirmed on three independent artefacts: the Step 2 ruling and its code
(`NATIVE_ORIGIN_HOUR = {"es": 6, "uk": 4, "it": 4}`, `TARGET_ORIGIN_HOUR = 4`); the generated
diaries (sleep 0.99 at indices 0-2, waking at index 3-5); and the **shipped** Step 8 presence files
(trough at index 7 = 11:00, peak at index 0 = 04:00).

🟢 **Step 9 is corrected**: `DIARY_ORIGIN_HOUR = 4`, one **cyclic shift of the whole year series**
before emission, `"rotated_to_midnight": true` in every manifest. The rotation is cyclic over the
year and not within each day, because a diary day's last four hours belong to the **next** calendar
day.

🔴 **Step 8 is not corrected, and that is the author's call** — `D-S9-3` recommends option (a),
re-emit and re-run, at a cost of about 13,108 local EnergyPlus runs and no queue. At risk:
`FINDING 133`'s **peak** channel and `FINDING 135`'s hour labels. Not at risk: every annual quantity,
`FINDING 134`, and — because a rotation applied identically to every rule point cannot create a
difference between rule points — decision 14's null.

## 8b. 🟢 `FINDING 142` — the activity-driven appliance peak falls at a different hour in each country, and the difference is six hours wide

Mean electricity per dwelling by **clock hour**, from the rotated stock series:

| fold | trough | **peak hour** | peak W | second feature |
|---|---|---|---:|---|
| **es** | 05:00, 86 W | **14:00** | **503** | evening plateau 19:00-22:00 (436 / 360 / 375 / 321 W) |
| **it** | 05:00, 86 W | **18:00** | **404** | broad 12:00-23:00 shoulder, no single lunch spike |
| **uk** | 04:00, 86 W | **20:00** | **416** | steady climb from 13:00, no midday peak at all |

🟢 **Spain peaks at lunch, Italy in the early evening, Britain at eight.** Six hours separate
the Spanish and British peaks. The three folds share one appliance set, one set of published
parameters, one calibration procedure and one trigger; **the only thing that differs between them is
the diary**, so the whole of this spread is the activity data speaking.

🔴 **This is the step's positive result, and it is the one to quote.** It is also the reason
`G9.12` fails: CREST's activity statistics are UK-2000, so a Spanish load shape built from Spanish
diaries is *supposed* to disagree with them. The FAIL and this finding are the same measurement read
from two sides.

🔴 **Quote it with two qualifiers.** It is a *shape* result at stock scale over 100 dwellings
per fold, not a magnitude claim — the levels rest on CREST's UK ownership shares (limitation E2,
`D-S9-2` item 2). And it depends on `FINDING 141`'s rotation being right: on the unrotated
convention the same three peaks read 10:00, 14:00 and 16:00, which is why `D-S9-3` has to be ruled
before this is written into anything.
