## 1. `D-S4-11` — `G4.1`: two implementations, two different failure modes

### What is on disk

`G4.1` (at-home-share variance band) is currently failing on every scored fold, but **it is not failing the same way in every file**:

| producer | how it reports the failure |
|---|---|
| the Step-4 gate battery | fails **on the band** — the measured statistic sits outside the pre-registered interval; the direction flips between lower / upper / both across folds |
| `tools/4thJ_step4_g47_coverage.py` | fails on `"reason": "V4.a: only 0 strata reach N >= 100 on BOTH sides..."`, `n_scorable_strata: 0` |
| `tools/4thJ_step4_genperturb.py` | same `V4.a` reason, same `n_scorable_strata: 0` |

Two of the three never compute a band at all. They refuse at the eligibility step, because no stratum reaches N ≥ 100 on both sides of the comparison, and then report the refusal under the gate's name.

### Why this matters more than it looks

If the coverage/genperturb refusal is the *non-canonical* implementation, then for **seven scored runs** those two files have been emitting a `G4.1` FAIL that carries **no information about the model at all** — a vacuous FAIL. That is precisely the `FINDING 56` failure class already on record in this project (Leg-4's `600/600` `G4.7` was a model-repo default covering for a broken harness): a gate whose verdict is produced by the harness rather than by the thing under test. The methodology rule *"gates must be seen failing"* was written to catch gates that **cannot fall**; it does not, on its own, catch a gate that **falls for the wrong reason**. This would be the second instance in Step 4.

Conversely, if the coverage implementation *is* canonical, then the battery's band failure is the anomaly and needs its own explanation.

### The proposed first move — costs nothing

**A pure source audit, zero compute, no sbatch.** Read the stratum-eligibility rule in all three producers and in the frozen `4thJ_step4_thresholds.py`, and write down, as a table:

- what each one requires of a stratum before it is scorable (`N ≥ 100` both sides? one side? no minimum?);
- which of them the frozen threshold file actually blesses as the definition of `G4.1`;
- for each of the seven scored runs, which implementation produced the recorded FAIL.

Three outcomes are possible and they are mutually exclusive:

- **(i) the battery is canonical** → the two coverage-side FAILs are vacuous, must be re-labelled `NOT COMPUTED` rather than `FAIL`, and the real `G4.1` question is the band failure, still unexplained;
- **(ii) the coverage rule is canonical** → the gate has never actually been evaluable on this corpus (no stratum reaches N ≥ 100 both sides), and `G4.1` ships as `BLOCKED`, not `FAIL` — a materially different claim in the paper;
- **(iii) both are legitimate under the frozen file** → the gate is under-specified, which is itself a finding and must be declared.

Note this audit **may dissolve the gate** — outcome (ii) removes a FAIL from the board and replaces it with a declared limitation, which is a change to what the paper claims, not a cosmetic one.

## 2. `D-S4-12` — `G4.4`: an unattributed FAIL → PASS flip

### What is on disk

On the regenerated Leg-5 `es` set (job `1286546`, produced **after** the `D-S4-8` `eos_token_id` repair), `G4.4` (MI ratio) moved:

| set | `G4.4` | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| pre-fix (morning, kept as control) | 0.544 | FAIL |
| post-fix (evening, job `1286546`) | 0.819 | PASS |

This flip was **not predicted**. It is currently recorded in `RESUME.md` as observed and explicitly **not attributed** to any cause.

### Why an unattributed PASS is the worst state to ship

A FAIL with a known cause is publishable. A PASS with an unknown cause is not: it cannot be distinguished, from the outside, from a lucky sample. There are exactly two live hypotheses and they have opposite consequences for the paper:

- **H1 — the repair caused it.** The `eos` fix changed what the generated text *is* (before the fix, a batch ran to its slowest member and diaries were padded with post-terminal continuation). If the MI ratio was being computed over that junk tail, the pre-fix 0.544 was measuring a harness defect, and 0.819 is the first honest reading of `G4.4` on this fold. Clean story, and it strengthens `D-S4-8`.
- **H2 — it is sampling noise.** Generation is stochastic; 0.544 → 0.819 may simply be inside the run-to-run spread. In that case `G4.4` has no demonstrated verdict on this fold in either direction, and claiming the PASS would be unsupported.

**Nothing currently on disk separates these.** Note that H1 and H2 are not exclusive — the repair could move the mean while the noise band is still wide enough to matter.

### The proposed measurement — two arms, one sbatch

- **Arm (a) — re-score, do not regenerate.** Run the *current* `G4.4` scorer over the **kept pre-fix generated set**. This is scoring only, no generation, so it is cheap. If it reproduces ≈ 0.544, the scorer is stable and the difference lives in the text → evidence for H1. If it now returns ≈ 0.819 on the *old* text, then the scorer itself changed between the two readings and neither number means what we thought — a third possibility that must be excluded.
- **Arm (b) — regenerate under a second seed.** One extra generation of the same fold with a different seed, everything else identical, gives a two-point spread. Two points is a weak noise estimate, but it establishes an order of magnitude: if the seed-to-seed gap is ~0.01 the flip is real; if it is ~0.3 the flip is noise and must be reported as such.

**Predictions must be registered in this document before the job is submitted** — that is the project's standing rule, and the last registered prediction (on `G4.6` parity) was *wrong*, which is exactly why the practice is kept. My predictions, on the record:

> - Arm (a) reproduces 0.544 ± 0.01 on the old text (scorer stable).
> - Arm (b) seed spread is **below 0.10**, i.e. smaller than the 0.275 flip → H1 favoured.
>
> If arm (b) returns a spread above 0.10, I was wrong and `G4.4`'s PASS on this fold is not claimable.

### Caveats stated in advance

- This is one fold (`es`). It does not license a statement about `uk` or `it`.
- Two seeds is not a distribution. The output is an order of magnitude, and must be written as such — never as a confidence interval.

### The question

> **`D-S4-12`:** Do I run the two-arm `G4.4` attribution job, with the predictions above registered as written?

- **(a)** Yes, both arms. *(recommended)*
- **(b)** Arm (a) only (re-score the old set; no new generation) — cheaper, but yields no noise band, so the flip stays unattributable.
- **(c)** Arm (b) only.
- **(d)** No — record the flip as observed-and-unexplained in the paper and move on.
- **(e)** Something else (write it below).

### AUTHOR'S ANSWER

🟢 **Option (a) — YES: Run both arms (a) and (b) with the registered predictions as written.**

- **Rationale**: An unattributed PASS is scientifically unpublishable. Arm (a) tests whether the scorer itself changed on the kept pre-repair text. Arm (b) estimates the two-seed generation variance under the repaired codebase.

## 3. `D-S4-13` — `G4.6`: is 4–19 % parity arithmetic, or is it bf16?

### What is on disk (already closed as a verdict by `D-S4-10`)

`G4.6` (merged-vs-unmerged LoRA logit drift ≤ 1e-4) fails, and its **consequence has now been measured** across four runs:

| run | control `unmerged_vs_unmerged` | measured `merged_vs_unmerged` identity |
|---|---|---|
| leg-5 `es` | 48/48 (frac 1.0) | 18.75 % |
| leg-4 `es` | 48/48 | 6.25 % |
| leg-4 `uk` | 48/48 | 4.17 % |
| leg-4 `it` | 48/48 | 8.33 % |

Median first divergence occurs **10.5 %–17.2 % into the diary** — parity is not merely imperfect, it breaks early. The control passing 48/48 on all four runs is what makes these runs valid rather than VOID. **My registered prediction ("high but not perfect") was wrong**, and that is on the record.

### The one thing still not known

Every one of those numbers was taken in **bf16**, the shipping dtype (per `D-S4-1`). So the low parity has two candidate causes that the existing measurement cannot separate:

- **C1 — bf16 rounding.** Merging `W + BA` into a single bf16 tensor discards mantissa bits that the two-matrix path retains. Under this hypothesis the merge is arithmetically correct and the drift is a precision artefact of the shipping format.
- **C2 — the merge is genuinely lossy.** Scaling, `alpha`, dropout-at-inference, or which modules get merged — an actual defect in the merge path, which bf16 merely reveals.

The consequence for the paper is different in each case. Under C1 the honest sentence is *"merged weights are not bit-equivalent in the shipping dtype; use the adapter"*. Under C2 it is *"the merge path is defective and every merged-weights result in this work carries a caveat"* — a much heavier claim.

### The proposed measurement — one run, same prompts

Re-run `tools/4thJ_step4_g46_merge_parity.py` in **float32** on the **same 48 prompts**, everything else untouched. Read-out is pre-registered:

- parity rises to ≈ 100 % → **C1**, dtype artefact, and the paper says exactly that;
- parity stays in the 4–19 % range → **C2**, the merge is lossy independently of precision, and the caveat is the heavy one.

The control (`unmerged_vs_unmerged`) must be run in float32 too and must pass 48/48, or the float32 run is VOID by the same rule that validated the bf16 runs.

### The mitigation is the same either way — and it is free

Whichever branch lands, the practical action does not change: **ship and score the unmerged adapter, never the merged weights.** The 48/48 control already proves the unmerged path is stable across all four runs. This costs nothing, requires no retraining, and removes the question from the critical path — the float32 run is for the *explanation in the paper*, not for the pipeline.

⚠️ Bound on the claim, to be repeated wherever these numbers appear: **48 prompts, one dtype per run.** It is not a distribution over the corpus.

- **(a)** Yes to both. *(recommended)*
- **(b)** (ii) only — adopt the unmerged-adapter rule, skip the float32 run, and report the bf16 numbers with the cause left open.
- **(c)** (i) only.
- **(d)** No to both.
- **(e)** Something else (write it below).

### AUTHOR'S ANSWER

🟢 **Option (a) — YES to both: (i) run the float32 parity measurement on the 48 prompts, and (ii) adopt "score and ship the unmerged adapter everywhere" as the standing pipeline rule.**

- **Rationale**:
  1. **Disambiguating C1 vs C2**: Testing parity in float32 determines whether the $4\%\text{--}19\%$ bf16 parity is a harmless mantissa truncation artefact (C1) or a structural defect in the LoRA merge scaling/alpha path (C2).
