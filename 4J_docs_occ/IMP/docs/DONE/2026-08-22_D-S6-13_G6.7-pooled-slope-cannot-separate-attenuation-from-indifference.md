## 2. 🔴 It does not ignore it. It receives it and attenuates it.

Per aggregate, `AC2` (employment) — the channel the λ tilt principally moves:

| fold | `AC2` **R²** | `AC2` slope | requested range | delivered range | **gain** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `es` | 0.845 | +0.166 | 79.3 min | 14.3 min | **18 %** |

| `it` | **0.984** | +0.120 | 72.5 min | 9.2 min | **13 %** |

`AC2` is the **highest-R² aggregate of the six in two folds of three**, and 0.845 in the third.
An R² of 0.98 across five λ levels is not indifference — the model orders the levels almost
perfectly. It simply delivers between an eighth and a half of the amplitude it was asked for.
`AC3` corroborates: +0.822 (`es`), +0.504 (`uk`), +0.863 (`it`), R² 0.90–0.96.

🔴 **The statistic cannot see this.** The `ignore_prefix` perturbation — which by construction
destroys all conditioning — scores pooled **0.0000**. Baseline `it` scores **0.1899**. Those two
numbers are 0.19 apart, while the underlying behaviours are "tracks the vector at R² 0.984" and
"cannot track it at all". **A gate whose statistic separates the true model from its own null
perturbation by 0.19 is not discriminating between them.**

## 3. Why the pooling destroys the signal — a budget-closure effect

The six level-1 aggregates are a **closed system**. Measured on the generated batches, they sum to
**1439.2 – 1439.6 minutes** of the 1440-minute day in every fold and at every level (expected
vectors: 1435.2 – 1437.9). The six deviations must therefore sum to ≈ 0.

So a model that under-delivers on five aggregates is **forced** to over-deliver on the sixth. That
sixth is `AC4-8` (leisure), the residual bucket, and it behaves exactly as the constraint predicts:

| fold | `AC4-8` slope | R² |
|---|---|---|
| `es` | **−1.259** | 0.843 |
| `uk` | **−1.023** | 0.732 |
| `it` | −0.399 | 0.293 |

The `es` and `uk` values sit at ≈ −1 with high R²: the leisure channel is absorbing, almost
one-for-one, the amplitude the other channels failed to deliver. Pooling then averages an
attenuated-positive set against a forced-negative one and lands near zero.

🔴 The consequence: **a model with uniform gain `g < 1` does not score pooled ≈ `g`.** It scores
far below `g`, because closure converts its shortfall into negative slope in the residual bucket.
The bar of 0.80 is therefore not "80 % of the requested amplitude" — it is stricter than that by an
amount that depends on which bucket absorbs the residual.

## 5. The question

**(a)** Keep the pooled slope as the verdict, and additionally **report** the per-aggregate slope,
R² and gain (already computed and printed). Zero risk, no band moves; but the headline number
stays one that cannot separate attenuation from indifference.

**(b)** Move the verdict to a **per-aggregate rule** — e.g. require slope ≥ 0.80 on the aggregates
the λ tilt actually targets, and report the rest. Measures what the clause means; but "which
aggregates are targeted" must be pre-registered, and the residual bucket must be excluded by name.

**(c)** Keep the pooled slope but **exclude the residual bucket** (`AC4-8`) from the fit, on the
stated ground that budget closure makes it a dependent quantity rather than a sixth independent
measurement. Smallest change that removes the cancellation; still one number.

**(d)** Add a **second, independent clause** the null perturbation *can* separate — e.g. pooled R²,
or a rank-correlation over the five levels — and require both. `ignore_prefix` would score 0 on it
by construction, which is the discrimination the present statistic lacks.

> ### Answer
>
> **Option (c) + (d) — Exclude Residual Closure Bucket and Require Dual Responsiveness:**
> 1. **Exclude the residual closure category `AC4-8`** from the pooled slope calculation on the mathematical ground of simplex closure ($\sum \Delta = 0$), preventing forced leisure over-absorption from artificially cancelling active tracking.

>    - **Fidelity/Steering**: Primary targeted channel (`AC2`) must demonstrate strong monotonic tracking ($R^2 \ge 0.80$ and positive slope $> 0.0$), separating true conditioning from `ignore_prefix` (which yields $R^2 = 0.0$).
>    - **Amplitude**: Slope $\ge 0.80$ evaluated across the 5 independent non-residual aggregates, with gain and per-aggregate slopes transparently reported.

### Detailed Rulings and Directives

#### 1. Mathematical Grounding of the Budget-Closure Correction
* **The Simplex Invariant**:
  Because all generated and expected time-use categories strictly sum to 1440 minutes, the sum of category deviations across the full profile is constrained to zero ($\sum_{k=1}^6 \Delta_k = 0$). Any under-delivery of requested amplitude across active categories ($\Delta < 0$) is mathematically forced to manifest as an equal and opposite over-allocation in the residual category (`AC4-8` leisure), producing a steep negative slope ($\approx -1.0$) with high $R^2$.
* **The Mathematical Defect in Naive Pooling**:
  Averaging positive tracking slopes (e.g. `AC2` at $+0.60$) against the forced negative residual slope ($-1.02$) yields a misleading pooled slope near zero ($0.24$), even when the model tracks the synthetic vector with $R^2 = 0.981$.
* **The Dual-Criteria Solution**:
  1. **Active Slope**: Fit the slope over the 5 structurally independent activity channels (`AC0`, `AC1_TR`, `AC2`, `AC3`, `AC9A`), excluding the passive leisure balancer (`AC4-8`).
  2. **Responsiveness Verification**: Demand $R^2 \ge 0.80$ on the primary targeted dimension (`AC2` employment). This rigorously separates an attenuated model (which tracks the vector with near-perfect ordering) from the `ignore_prefix` null perturbation (which exhibits $R^2 = 0.000$ and zero correlation).
