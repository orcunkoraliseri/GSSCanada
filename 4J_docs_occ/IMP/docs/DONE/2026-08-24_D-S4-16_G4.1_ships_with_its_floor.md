## 2.1 🔴 `FINDING 100` — two things, both new, both from this one measurement

**(i) The verdict itself is not stable under reseeding.** On `it`, the SAME frozen adapter returns
`FAIL, FAIL, FAIL, FAIL, PASS`. Nothing changed but the sampler's seed. The script printed this
itself (`verdict_stable_under_reseed: false`), under a rule registered in its source before any
result existed.

**(ii) The direction flip is explained, and it is not a property of any fold or epoch.** The
standing open question in `RESUME.md` — *why does the failure end flip `lower` / `upper` / `both`
between folds and between epochs of one run?* — is answered by the seed alone at frozen weights:

* `es`: `upper`, **`lower (collapse)`**, `upper`, `upper`, **`lower (collapse)`**
* `uk`: `upper`, `upper`, **`both`**, `upper`, `upper`
* `it`: **`both`**, `upper`, `upper`, `upper`, `none`

All three ends appear within one fold, one adapter, one set of weights. No hypothesis about fold
composition, epoch dynamics or backbone size is needed, and none may be offered.

## 5. Options

### Item 1 — how `G4.1` ships

**(a) FAIL on all three folds, every reading printed beside its fold's floor, and `it`'s epoch-2
PASS struck as unresolved. [RECOMMENDED]**
The verdict as scored is FAIL on `es` and `uk` and it is stable across five seeds on both. `it`
ep2's PASS is retracted — **struck, not deleted** — with the reason on the page: 1 of 5 seeds, a
margin of `0.010` against a floor of `0.385`. Every `G4.1` number in the paper carries the fold's
spread beside it, and `G4.1` is declared **resolution-limited at N=600** — the same class of finding
as `G4.3` (the bar has no provenance) and `G4.6` (the band is unsatisfiable), both already ruled.
Only `uk` ep2 is reported as an over-dispersion the measurement can actually resolve.
Costs nothing, moves nothing, and is the same shape as `D-S4-14`'s band-not-a-point ruling.

**(b) Ship `es` and `it` as `NOT RESOLVED` and only `uk` ep2 as FAIL.**
Honest about §2.2, but it converts a scored FAIL into a non-verdict after the folds were scored,
which is the move `D-S4-11` refused for the perturbation side. It also understates: on `es` the FAIL
is stable across all five seeds — it is the *magnitude*, not the verdict, that is unresolved.

**(c) Report the readings without the floor.**
Refused on its face: the script registered the opposite rule in its own source before running, and
`it`'s PASS would ship next to two FAILs with its 1-in-5 seed dependence invisible.

**(d) Raise `N` per fold so the floor shrinks, then re-score.**
Basis change after scoring. `D-S4-11` and `D-S4-14` both ruled against this shape. Not executed
without an explicit override.

### Item 2 — the `es` basis asymmetry (`FINDING 101`)

**(a) Declare the five seed-floor readings `es`'s post-repair `G4.1` evidence. [RECOMMENDED]**
They exist, they are on the trainer's own function, they are post-repair, and they are all FAIL.
`es`'s canonical epoch verdicts stay as scored (pre-repair, `1286209`) and the asymmetry is
declared in the paper with the five post-repair readings beside them. **Zero compute.**

**(b) Re-run Leg-5 `es` training post-repair so the canonical basis matches `uk` and `it`.**
~11 GPU-hours. Buys a like-for-like epoch line. Given (a) already answers what `es` does after the
repair, it buys the label and not the knowledge — and a second `es` adapter would then have to be
reconciled with the one every other Step-4 result was computed on.

**(c) Declare the asymmetry and change nothing.**
Weaker than (a) for the same cost, since (a)'s readings are already on disk.

## 6. AUTHOR'S ANSWER

**Item 1 — 🟢 Option (a): FAIL on all three folds, every reading printed beside its fold's sampling-noise floor, and `it`'s epoch-2 PASS struck as unresolved.**
- **Rationale & Directives**:
  - The measured sampling noise floor ($0.385\text{--}0.658$) exceeds the entire width of the pre-registered band ($0.45$).
  - `it`'s epoch-2 PASS cleared the upper bound by only $0.010$ against a sampling floor of $0.385$ ($38\times$ smaller than resolution) and fails on 4 of 5 seeds on the exact same weights. It is struck (not deleted) with this explicit empirical explanation.
  - All readings in the manuscript will be reported beside their fold's sampling floor, declaring `G4.1` as resolution-limited at $N=600$.
  - Only `uk` epoch 2 ($+1.043$ high, worst $2.293$) is reported as a genuine over-dispersion exceeding its sampling floor ($1.59\times$).

**Item 2 — 🟢 Option (a): Declare the five seed-floor readings as `es`'s post-repair `G4.1` evidence.**
- **Rationale & Directives**:
  - The 5 seed-floor runs on `es` (`1286634`) were generated post-repair (with `eos_token_id` fix) on the trainer's canonical function, and all 5 return FAIL ($1.117\text{--}1.646$).
  - Canonical epoch lines stay as scored (`1286209`, pre-repair), and the basis asymmetry is explicitly declared in the methods with the 5 post-repair seed-floor readings reported alongside.
  - Zero compute; eliminates the need for an 11 GPU-hour re-run that would produce no new scientific knowledge.
