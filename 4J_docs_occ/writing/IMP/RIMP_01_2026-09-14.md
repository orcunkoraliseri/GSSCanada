C. THE NEGATIVE RESULT, ON ITS OWN
1. Does the paper eliminate the possibility that the model simply did not have enough capacity?
The paper does not eliminate capacity as an explanation because its three interventions are narrow and lack independence. Comparing a 1.48 billion parameter model to a 7.30 billion parameter model provides only two points, which the paper itself concedes cannot establish a scaling trajectory. Furthermore, both the 92-fold parameter expansion via full fine-tuning and the alternative 7B model family evaluation were executed on a single fold rather than the full leave-one-country-out design, leaving open the possibility that larger frontier architectures or multi-fold full fine-tunes would behave differently.

2. Does the paper eliminate the possibility that three countries was too few?
The paper does not eliminate the possibility that three countries was too few, explicitly admitting in Section 7.1 that training on two countries directly bounds the generality of the findings. The author notes that no published theoretical threshold exists in the literature to prove three countries is sufficient, meaning a formal defense based on external theory is unavailable. The paper instead adopts a symmetric rhetorical posture, arguing that the absence of a published threshold also prevents critics from dismissing the result outright, leaving the three-country design as an empirical data point rather than a resolved question.

3. Is the null a fair competitor, or is it secretly advantaged?
The null possesses a profound structural advantage because every record in its donor pool is an authentic sequence of human activity with natural dwell-time distributions, valid transition structures, and realistic co-presence. The null only needs to adjust demographic weights via iterative proportional fitting, whereas the generative language model is tasked with synthesizing complex temporal and joint distributions from scratch without those structures being provided in its demographic prefix. Although both methods receive identical published demographic marginals and the null's uniform seed discards source survey design weights, evaluating synthetic generation against intact empirical diaries on distributional metrics gives the null an inherent structural lead.

4. Could the result be an artefact of the scoring measure rather than of the model?
The negative result is not merely an artefact of the primary time-budget mean absolute error metric, as five additional pre-registered transfer gates evaluating budget agreement, held-in regression, joint distribution structure, and country discrimination fail uniformly. However, the choice of null did influence the margin: Section 5.1 reveals that the pre-registered raked-donor null actually proved to be the weakest of the three registered baselines in six of nine cells, meaning alternative registered baselines would have widened the model's loss. Furthermore, structural evaluations of dwell times and transition matrices exhibit failures between three and nine times their registered tolerances, showing that the deficit persists across temporal, categorical, and marginal dimensions.

5. Is the pre-registration credible as described, or does it read as having been written after the numbers were known?
The pre-registration is highly credible as documented, backed by an MD5 hash frozen on 2026-08-18 before training and enforced by automated hash verifications during execution. The clearest evidence of authenticity is that several pre-registered hypotheses and instrument configurations produced outcomes contrary to the author's expectations, such as the pre-registered null proving to be the weakest rather than the strongest baseline, and multiple gates failing due to flawed instrument assumptions rather than being retrofitted. A researcher constructing a post-hoc pre-registration would not deliberately retain hypotheses that were contradicted by their own data or include checks that suffered from demonstrable denominator errors.
