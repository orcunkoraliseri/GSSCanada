# RT04. The occupant-behaviour frontier after Annex 79: generative occupants, LLM simulators, and open gaps

## Section A. Direct answer

Time-use-derived occupancy in building performance simulation is currently viewed as a specialized, methodologically mature niche rather than a universally settled standard. While recognized as the benchmark route for constructing synthetically diverse, uncalibrated occupant schedules, it remains constrained by five-to-ten-year survey cadence, absence of intra-annual variation, and lack of dynamic feedback from the indoor thermal environment. Furthermore, zero published occupancy models at population scale dynamically adjust whole-building presence or time-at-home in response to outdoor temperature, heat waves, or extreme weather events; presence schedules remain strictly weather-invariant in all building energy models. Generative and large language model methods have emerged rapidly since 2023, primarily as multi-agent frameworks for early-stage design simulation (Dai et al. 2026) and EnergyPlus model modification (Shirzadi et al. 2026), but the social-simulation generative-agent paradigm (Park et al. 2023) has not yet been coupled to thermodynamic building energy simulation engines. Following the closure of IEA EBC Annex 79, the field has identified multi-domain exposure, longitudinal data scarcity, and cross-national transferability as unresolved frontiers, leaving angle A2 (occupancy under heat) and angle A3 (cross-national transfer mechanisms) substantially open.

## Section E. What this changes in our planning

- Time-use occupancy remains a distinct signature: Because no other group has published a verified cross-national transfer pipeline for time-use occupancy generators, this methodological line remains our primary comparative advantage.
- Avoiding the generative-agent hype trap: The literature on LLM generative agents (such as Park et al. 2023) has attracted significant attention in computer science but has zero verified thermodynamic integration with physics engines like EnergyPlus. Attempting to build full generative agents in 5J would pivot into a crowded software engineering niche without physical simulation validation.
- The heat-response gap is confirmed open: Item 4 confirmed that whole-building occupancy models treat presence as strictly invariant to outdoor heat waves. A paper establishing how heat waves alter presence and vulnerability (angle A2) occupies a genuinely unclaimed gap.
- Dropping claims of seasonal variation from single-day diaries: Reviewers consistently reject claims that 24-hour time-use diaries capture annual behavioral shifts. Any future model using time-use data must explicitly acknowledge the cross-sectional limitation and avoid claiming longitudinal tracking.

## Section G. Contradictions, gaps, open questions, and negative controls

### 1. What time-use approaches lack (reviewer consensus)
Based on recent reviews (Vogl et al. 2026, Bataynah et al. 2026) and modeling studies (Schumann et al. 2023, Aragon et al. 2019), reviewers consistently identify four core limitations of time-use occupancy in building simulation:
- Absence of intra-annual / seasonal tracking: Cross-sectional 24-hour diaries provide snapshot representations that cannot capture how an individual household's behavior evolves across seasons.
- No dynamic feedback from the building state: Time-use diaries record activities chosen independently of indoor temperature, humidity, or air quality. Imposing them on an EnergyPlus model creates an open-loop simulation where indoor overheating does not alter occupant presence or window use.
- Weather independence: Survey fieldwork dates are rarely coupled with microclimatic weather extremes. As a result, time-at-home is assumed identical on mild days and extreme heatwave days.
- Weak collective household coordination: Most time-use surveys sample only one or two members per household, preventing complete reconstruction of simultaneous, interdependent multi-occupant actions.
