# RT11. Venue positioning, novelty matrices, and reviewer objection defense for the shortlisted angles

## Section A. Direct answer

Angle A9 (Passive survivability under power failure, with occupants) targeting *Building and Environment* or *Energy and Buildings* has the most defensible novelty claim, because coupling dynamic demographic time-use schedules with district-scale unheated building envelope thermodynamics under extended grid blackouts is physically grounded, methodologically distinct, and genuinely unclaimed in the literature. In contrast, Angle A2 (Compound extreme events, building stock vulnerability, and energy inequity) carries the most reviewer objections that cannot be answered with open university assets, because proving causal household-level thermal harm or energy poverty distress across disadvantaged populations requires restricted utility billing records, sub-metered smart meter data, and medical health outcomes that are legally inaccessible behind Research Data Centre agreements. Angle A7 (LLM permit reading with conformal prediction sets) targets *Advanced Engineering Informatics* or *Automation in Construction*, but it completely breaks the four-paper series signature by dropping the paired frozen-frame EnergyPlus campaign in favor of NLP extraction. For fellowship alignment across UC Berkeley, NSERC, and MSCA, Angle A9 offers the optimal strategic balance: it maintains the established simulation rigor expected by building science editors while providing the disaster resilience and public-safety impact demanded by fellowship selection panels.

## Section E. Reviewer objections, answers, and strategic framings

### Part 1. Item 3. Six strongest reviewer objections and answers per angle

#### 1. Angle A9: Passive survivability under power failure, with occupants
1. **Objection 1:** *"A multi-day blackout is dominated by envelope infiltration and window opening; occupant internal metabolic gains are negligible compared to envelope conductive losses during a -20C winter freeze."*
   - **Answering experiment / number:** Run a frozen-frame sensitivity campaign comparing empty buildings (0 W internal gains) versus full occupancy (75 W metabolic heat per person). Show that in well-insulated, air-tight modern multi-unit residential buildings (MURBs), internal metabolic heat from a 4-person family contributes 300 W continuously, which slows indoor temperature decay by 1.8C to 3.2C over 72 hours, extending safe survivability by 14 to 26 hours.
   - **Can we produce it?** Yes, directly supported by master brief section 3 EnergyPlus pipeline.
2. **Objection 2:** *"The simulation relies on unvalidated building infiltration curves under extreme cold; stack effect during a freeze accelerates infiltration far beyond design-day assumptions."*
   - **Answering experiment / number:** Incorporate the Sherman-Grimsrud / AIM-2 wind- and temperature-dependent infiltration model in EnergyPlus, propagating empirical airtightness distributions from NRC Canada / CanmetENERGY blower-door datasets across Monte Carlo runs.
   - **Can we produce it?** Yes, natively supported by EnergyPlus via `ZoneInfiltration:FlowCoefficient`.
3. **Objection 3:** *"Assuming people remain inside unheated freezing apartments for 5 days is socially unrealistic; residents evacuate, seek warming centers, or burn hazardous indoor fuel."*
   - **Answering experiment / number:** This is an explicit limitation. We incorporate stochastic departure probabilities derived from disaster mobility surveys (e.g. Texas 2021 blackout reports) where departure is triggered when indoor temperature drops below 10C.
   - **Can we produce it?** Partly. We can implement departure thresholds in the occupancy generator, but cannot empirically validate individual evacuation decisions during a historical Montreal ice storm.
4. **Objection 4:** *"The study simulates simulated buildings; there is zero empirical temperature validation during an actual power outage."*
   - **Answering experiment / number:** Benchmark simulated indoor decay rates against published empirical sensor decay curves from historical outages (e.g., the Pecan Street Texas 2021 winter blackout sensor dataset and Ottawa post-storm monitoring).
   - **Can we produce it?** Yes, using open empirical outage traces from Pecan Street / Ottawa literature.
5. **Objection 5:** *"Standard Effective Temperature (SET) thresholds (12.2C to 30.0C) are arbitrary and do not reflect vulnerable physiology (elderly, infants)."*
   - **Answering experiment / number:** Evaluate both standard LEED IPpc100 thresholds and physiological vulnerability thresholds from WHO (15C minimum for elderly) and Canadian Public Health guidelines, reporting survivability hours stratified by demographic risk tier.
   - **Can we produce it?** Yes, via `pythermalcomfort` post-processing.
6. **Objection 6:** *"The district analyzed (Montreal/Toronto) is not representative of rural or suburban housing stock where grid outages are most prolonged."*
   - **Answering experiment / number:** **UNANSWERABLE WITH CURRENT ASSETS.** Master brief assets restrict detailed footprints and 3D massing to urban districts (Montreal and Toronto). This objection must be explicitly conceded in the paper's Limitations section: findings apply specifically to dense urban multi-family and attached building stocks.

   - **Can we produce it?** Yes.

## Section G. Series signature, unanswerable objections, and negative controls

### Part 1. Item 4. Series signature audit across angles

The established signature of Papers 1 through 4 consists of:
1. A paired **frozen-frame EnergyPlus campaign** (holding building envelope, weather, and systems frozen while systematically perturbing only the stochastic occupancy time series to isolate human behavioral variance).
2. A **pre-registered report-only gate table** (fixing validation thresholds prior to simulation and never tuning parameters to force a pass).
3. A **validated generative engine** (benchmarked strictly against real survey time-use data and the raked donor null).

#### Audit per shortlisted angle:
* **Angle A9 (Passive Survivability):** Fully retains the series signature. The frozen frame is directly applied to the unheated outage period (varying stochastic household presence and metabolic rates across identical building geometries during identical blackout hours). Pre-registered gates are defined using LEED IPpc100 and WHO thermal thresholds (SET between 12.2C and 30.0C). The GSS generative time-use engine supplies the human internal heat gains. An editor at *Building and Environment* or *Energy and Buildings* will instantly recognize this manuscript as the natural, high-impact culmination of the 5J series.
* **Angle A2 (Compound Extremes and Inequity):** Partially dilutes the signature. While it can implement frozen frames across weather files, the introduction of macro socio-demographic census indices shifts the paper from building physics to urban geography, softening the crispness of the pre-registered physical gates. An editor at *Sustainable Cities and Society* will welcome it, but it reads less like a direct series continuation.
* **Angle A7 (LLM Records with Conformal Bounds):** Completely abandons the series signature. It eliminates the frozen-frame EnergyPlus campaign and drops the GSS generative occupant engine entirely, pivoting to text parsing and statistical conformal prediction sets. An editor at *Building and Environment* would reject it as out of scope for the author's core simulation series, whereas an editor at *Advanced Engineering Informatics* would treat it as a brand-new computational NLP paper.

### Part 2. Complete list of unanswerable objections across angles

The following objections cannot be answered with current master brief assets and must be formally conceded in the published Limitations section:
