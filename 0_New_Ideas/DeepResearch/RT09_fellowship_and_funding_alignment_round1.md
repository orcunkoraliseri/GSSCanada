### Part 2. Doctoral continuity versus distinctness: Canadian versus European panels

A critical structural conflict exists between Canadian and European fellowship evaluation cultures:

1. **The Canadian Reading (NSERC CPRA):** Canadian peer review committees (Civil, Industrial and Systems Engineering) strictly penalize proposals that read as an incremental extension of doctoral research ("thesis chapter 5"). Because the doctoral thesis centered on generative occupancy modeling, continuing with pure synthetic time-use generation (A1, A4, A5) risks immediate downgrade under the Research Potential and Training Plan criteria. A Canadian panel reads Angle A9 (Passive survivability under power failure, with grid disruption and envelope thermodynamics) as a distinct, high-impact pivot into infrastructure resilience and public safety.
2. **The European Reading (MSCA PF at ETH Zurich):** MSCA evaluators under the Excellence criterion (50%) place heavy weight on the candidate's established track record, verified methodology, and the feasibility of two-way knowledge transfer. Presenting a completely disjoint topic where the applicant has no prior first-author publications creates perceived execution risk. A European panel reads Angle A4/A5 or A9 (coupled to the proven generative occupancy engine) as strong evidence of mature competence and high feasibility.
3. **Synthesis:** Angle A9 bridges this conflict effectively: for NSERC, the narrative emphasizes the thermodynamic outage physics, building envelope vulnerability, and grid disruption (distinct from the PhD); for MSCA, the narrative highlights the integration of the doctoral time-use engine to model occupant presence and vulnerability during crises (continuity and methodological maturity).

2. **Thesis Continuity vs Independence:** MSCA at ETH Zurich rewards demonstrated mastery and direct publication continuity from the doctoral occupant engine, whereas NSERC CPRA committees penalize proposals that appear to be a direct continuation of thesis work.
3. **Local Actionable Policy vs Generalizable Method:** UC Berkeley requires direct translation into public action and energy equity for Californian disadvantaged communities, whereas European calls (KTH Digital Futures, Horizon Europe) prioritize generalizable digital architectures and cross-border open-source standards.
4. **Tool/Agent Speculation vs Empirical Rigor:** KTH Digital Futures and Schmidt welcome speculative frontier AI agent frameworks (A3), whereas NSERC engineering panels dismiss LLM agentic UBEM as uncalibrated software hype lacking physical validation.
5. **National Jurisdiction Lock-in:** Canadian archetype transfer (A8) directly satisfies NSERC and Canadian municipal stakeholders, but it instantly disqualifies interest from European and Californian fellowship reviewers who demand local or continent-wide relevance.

**Can a single paper serve both sides?**
A single paper cannot simultaneously maximize opposing evaluation criteria; attempting to combine municipal permit NLP extraction (A7) and multi-day winter freeze building physics (A9) into one manuscript will produce an unfocused paper that top journals and fellowship panels reject. The researcher must choose a single core narrative for Paper 5J (recommended: A9 passive survivability with rigorous statistical uncertainty quantification), while using the submitted/accepted status of prior doctoral papers (occupant generation) and a targeted open preprint (conformal record reading or surrogate modeling) to satisfy the AI and European continuity panels.
